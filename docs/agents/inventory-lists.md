# Copies, Inventory Lists & Detail Pages

Internal numbers, copy counts, grouping, duplicate detection, chips, and what a detail page is for.

- **Quick offer mode** (#537) is the Copies list's bulk-listing pass: the platform and the starting status are set once in a bar pinned under the toolbar (`inventory/quick-offer-bar.tsx`, `ListToolbar`'s `footer` since #848) and every **Add to new offer** from then on — the row's `⋮` entry, its promoted icon and the selection bar's `New offer` buttons (#277/#497) — calls `createOfferAction` outright instead of opening `OfferFormDialog`. It goes **further than #234**, which pre-fills the dialog: pre-filling still leaves the dialog, and the dialog is the mouse trip. The offers carry **no price and no URL** deliberately — that is what makes it a bulk pass, and both land on the offer's own screen once the listing exists — so the catalog-value pre-fill (#230) is the ordinary flow's, not this one's. The bar **is** the mode's whole affordance and is why a silent create is safe: which platform, which status and how many offers the pass has made are impossible not to see, and a failure has nowhere else to be reported. It is therefore **not persisted**, unlike every other way of working this list remembers (`groupMode`, the split axes, the worklist platform): a mode that skips a confirmation must never be in force on a screen the collector has just opened. The `⋮` entry **renames itself** (`New offer on Colnect`, via `CopyRowActions.quickOffer`) rather than reading the same in both modes.

- The pass states the platform and the status and **nothing else**, which is exactly why the platform's own listing defaults have to reach it (#589): `readOfferInput` passes an *absent* `listingType` on as `undefined` rather than normalizing it to `fixed`, so `resolveOfferPricing` falls back to `Contact.defaultListingType` and, on an auction, to `defaultStartingPrice` (#449/#553) — a form that never asked the question must not answer it. A submitted-but-unrecognised value still reads as `fixed`. It is the server that applies them, not the bar, so quick mode and the create form cannot end up with two answers. A platform with **no `platformCurrency`** does not arm it — the currency is fixed at the platform on its first offer (#196) and that choice belongs in the create form, so the ordinary dialog stays in effect rather than the pass failing per click or quietly fixing a currency for good.

- Where the bar **sits** was settled by #848: inside the pinned toolbar block, so it stays on screen through a pass that scrolls, and **above** the selection bar — quick offer is the mode, the selection is what is being done inside it. Both are rows of the block and **both push the list down when they arrive** (#885). For this one that was always right — it goes up on a click aimed at it when nothing is under way, so the shift reads as feedback. For the selection bar it is an accepted flaw rather than a good outcome: it arrives on a ticked checkbox mid-pass, where a shift moves the row the next tick is aimed at, and a mechanism that held the rows still by scrolling the viewport instead shipped and was then removed, because it could only work on a list long enough to scroll and so made the screen's behaviour depend on its row count (#884). The full reasoning, and both rejected mechanisms, sit on `ListToolbar`'s `footer` prop; the redesign expected to settle it is #849. Both take their shape and colour from `LIST_BANNER_STYLE` and their width from the block, so neither can drift from the other again.

- **Card scans is the Copies list's other way in** (#725) — `/c/[slug]/inventory/scans`, a sidebar entry of its own under the Inventory group (#762) and a *Scan a card* link beside *Add copy*. It is the purchase screen's Card scans section (`shared/scans-card.tsx`) with the order taken out: scan a whole stockbook card, cut it into tiles, pair the backs, and identify each piece — for stamps **already owned**, which is what the whole flow could not reach while it lived only under a purchase (#566/#567 were built for a parcel arriving). The alternative was a synthetic `Purchase` the way a closed trade makes one (#644), and it is what the issue rules out: there is no money, no lot and nothing for a pool to be split across, so the record would be a fiction the Purchases list and every ROI figure would then have to keep explaining away.

- So the **owner moved up** instead — `ScanSheet`/`ScanTile`/`ScanUpload` carry a NOT NULL `collectionId` and a nullable `purchaseId`, and `{ collectionId, purchaseId: null }` is not *any card in the collection* but exactly the ones with no order, which is what lets one `listScans` serve both screens (`scanOwnerWhere`, `src/lib/scan-sheets.ts`). A copy identified here gets **no lot and therefore no cost basis**, and lands `delivered` rather than `ordered` — all three are shapes `Item` already had, and are what *Add copy* has always written, so nothing downstream needed an exclusion; `intakeStamps` takes `{ lotId } | { collectionId }` for it, two shapes rather than a nullable id so a caller that means "no lot" has to say which collection instead. The lot question therefore **disappears rather than being answered**: `lotChoice` is left off the condition step, which is the shape it already had for the one-lot stockbook purchase.

- *Assign this tile to a copy that already exists* stays and **widens to the collection** — an order narrows to its parcel (#586) because a settled auction's card is matched against that parcel's described lines, and a card with no parcel has nothing to narrow to; while digitising a shelf that is the common case, most pieces being already recorded and wanting photographs rather than identification.

- Batch numbers come off a **second counter**, `Collection.nextScanBatchNo` beside `Purchase.nextScanBatchNo`: merging the sequences is tidier and was rejected because the migration would renumber every existing order's batches, and a batch number is written on a physical card — the rule #268 and #432 state about copy and purchase numbers. The two lists stay **apart on purpose**: a parcel's cards are on that order's screen where the lot question and the auction assign path are, and one combined list would have had to explain per row which of two identification flows a tile is about. The screen draws the section `alwaysOpen` — it is collapsible on the order screen because it is one of that screen's sections, and a page whose whole content is one closed line has to be opened before it says anything.

- A **copy's notes print in full under its row**, never behind a hover (#116). The chip they replaced (`notes`, with the text as its tooltip) was the one field on the row with no fixed vocabulary — a postmark type, why this copy was kept — and hiding what only the note can say behind a hover made it the one thing on the row that had to be hunted for, while every chip beside it says its piece outright. The line is drawn **only when there is a note**, so a list of copies without them keeps its height exactly — which is also why it is a line rather than a reserved area. It sits **inside the row's text column**, so it starts where the row's words do rather than under the photo, and it is `pre-wrap` and untruncated: a note typed as two lines reads as two, and a note clipped at a width is a tooltip again. Mirrors the auction lot row, which already printed `lot.notes` this way. Because `InventoryItemRow` is rendered from eight screens the line reaches all of them, which is right — a note is a fact about the copy, not about the list it is being read in.

- A copy's **internal number** (#268) is a per-collection sequence on `Item.itemNo`, unique with `collectionId`, assigned at creation and never editable. It is handed out by bumping `Collection.nextItemNo` in one `UPDATE ... RETURNING` (`allocateItemNumbers` in `src/lib/items.ts`, which takes a range for a bulk intake) — **never** `max(itemNo) + 1`: the number gets written on the physical piece, so a deleted copy must retire its number rather than pass it to the next one. Every `item.create*` site must supply it. Display and search parsing are pure in `src/lib/item-number.ts` (`formatItemNo` → `#00123`, zero-padded so a column lines up; `parseItemNoSearch` accepts `123` / `00123` / `#00123`). A numeric search matches the number **in addition to** the text search, never instead of it — `200` is also a plausible catalog number. It rides in the row's meta line and the Edit dialog's title, not in a form field: a permanently disabled input earns no space. How wide it renders is the collector's choice — `Collection.itemNoPad` (Settings → General), display-only, so changing it renumbers nothing and cannot break a search (leading zeros are insignificant to `parseItemNoSearch`). Client surfaces read it through `useCollectionItemNoPad`, a query rather than a prop, because `InventoryItemRow` is rendered from eight screens. In listing texts it is `{itemNo}` / `{itemNo:N}` (#268) — padded to the collection width, or to `N`; an unusable `N` falls back to the collection width rather than emptying the token, and **no `#` is added**, since a template is literal text. `TitleTemplateCopy` therefore carries `itemNo` *and* `itemNoPad`: collection-level data on a per-copy shape, because the pure engine has no other channel.

- The **short number** pattern is now every major entity's (#432), and the box that consumes them is #431. `Issue.issueNo`, `Purchase.purchaseNo`, `Sale.saleNo` and `AuctionLot.auctionLotNo` join `Item.itemNo` (#268) and `Offer.offerNo` (#416), each off its own `Collection.next<Entity>No` counter through one shared `allocateEntityNumber` (`src/lib/items.ts`) — the same atomic `UPDATE ... RETURNING`, never `max + 1`, because a number that has been quoted must not come to mean something else. One counter per entity rather than one shared sequence: a collector counts purchases and issues separately, and sharing would make every number jump for reasons nothing on screen explains. Every `create*` therefore runs **in a transaction** — `createPurchase`/`createSale`/`createAuctionLot` gained one for exactly this, so a failed create cannot burn a number. `AuctionLot` is the one without a unique index: a lot carries no `collectionId` (it reaches its collection through its sale), and denormalizing that would be a second answer to "which collection is this lot in" for the sake of a safety net the counter already provides; `auctionLotNo` is also deliberately **not** `lotNo`, which is the *house's* number — typed in, optional, repeating across sales, and the field an Allegro capture writes the offer id into (#355). `AuctionSale` gets no number: the lot is what one refers to, and it carries its sale's address. The numbers render through one `EntityNoChip` (unpadded — `Collection.itemNoPad` exists because a copy number goes on the physical piece and a column of stock cards has to line up; none of these do). The **box** is `parseQuickJump` in the pure `src/lib/quick-jump.ts` (prefix table: `i` `o` `p` `s` `iss` `lot`, longest-prefix-first so `iss12` is never `i` + `ss12`) plus `resolveQuickJump` in `quick-jump-server.ts` behind `GET /api/collections/[collectionId]/quick-jump`. It is a **jump, not a search**, and does nothing until Enter: a bare `200` is refused rather than guessed at, because it is as likely a catalog number, a year or a price. The slug in the returned href is read **server-side** off the authorized collection, never taken from the query — the answer is an address the browser is about to follow. Where each lands is a product decision stated in that module: copy and issue to their list filtered to the number (neither has a screen of its own), offer to the `/o/<slug>/<no>` redirect (#416) so a jump and a followed marketplace link are one journey, purchase/sale to their detail screens, and a lot to its **sale** with the lot scrolled to and flashed once (#374, #850) — a lot is read in the company of its parcel.

- **Copies held per stamp** (#348) come from `src/lib/copy-counts.ts` — one `groupBy` over the disposition flags for a whole page of stamps, never a count per row — and render through the shared `CopyCountBadge` on every surface that lists a stamp (issue tree, flat list, pickers). Counted **per stamp exactly**, never rolled up from variant children (the tree shows the child's own badge one line down, so a rollup would show one copy twice), and **excluding sold copies**, because the badge sits beside *View copies* and must agree with what that popup lists (#207). The in-flight delivery states are deliberately *not* filtered — a copy in transit is bought — while the two exception outcomes (`UNAVAILABLE_DELIVERY_STATES`) and disposed-of copies are, since #396: the badge answers *how many of this do I have*. A stamp with none shows **nothing**, the same unmarked-default rule as the subtype chip and the single format. The disposition figures are markers that overlap (in collection *and* for sale), so they are listed in a panel, never summed.

- That panel is a **hover** since #721 — the shared `Tooltip` with a small grid of labelled figures in it, the `maxWidth` shape #457's cost cell uses, not a second hover mechanism — and the **click opens the copies**: the very `InventoryPopupDialog` the row's *View copies* entry opens (#110/#125), reached by handing `useInventoryPopupAction`'s `open` to the badge as `onOpenCopies` rather than letting the chip build a popup of its own, so the chip and the `⋮` entry cannot drift into two surfaces answering one question. That popup **filters** since #724, on the five axes the Copies toolbar carries — condition, disposition, format, delivery state, certificate — because the question asked of a well-held stamp's copies is almost never "show me all forty" but "which of these is mint", "which are still in the post". Same controls (`MultiSelectFilter`, `FilterChip`), same sentinels (`single`, `none`) and the same server-side `InventoryItemFilters` the list uses, so an answer in the popup and an answer on the screen cannot differ; the filtering is the **query's**, never a client-side pass over the loaded page, which would narrow one page of an endless list and call it the answer. The state is the dialog's own and is deliberately **neither in the URL nor remembered** (#325/#693): the popup is opened at a stamp, read and closed, and a narrowing carried into the next stamp's popup would hide copies nobody asked to hide. The dialog's `dismissable` follows the menus' `onOpenChange` — one boolean for all of them, as `want-acceptance-fields` does — since a `MultiSelectFilter` owns its own Escape and is not a layer (#361), and one press must not close the menu and the popup under it. The empty state says which emptiness it is: *no copies match these filters* is not *none recorded yet*. The reason for the swap is that the click was worth more than the breakdown was: what the chip is usually being looked at *for* is the copies behind it, and spending the only click on a read-only summary left the menu as the sole way there from a row whose chip was pointing straight at it. Moving to `Tooltip` also drops the portal, the outside-click listener and the **stopped** Escape the chip carried, all of which existed only to make a click-opened surface survive inside a dialog.

- Only the two list screens pass `onOpenCopies` — the issue tree (through `StampDetailLine`) and the flat stamp list — and where none is passed (the pickers, the identify dialog, the stamp detail page and its variants card) the chip is a plain `role="img"` `<span>`, not a dead button: it still previews on hover, and a chip that looks pressable and does nothing is worse than one that never claimed to be. `WantChip` beside it deliberately keeps its click-popover — its popover is still the only place its wants are listed, so its click is still the way in, and the two chips differ because what is behind them differs.

- What the **variants** hold is the *second* number **inside the same chip** (#528, `countVariantDescendantCopies`, drawn as a muted parenthesised `3 (+2) copies` by the same `CopyCountBadge` — a second chip on a line that already carries catalog numbers, a Colnect link, a subtype and a price read as noise): copies of every descendant whose effective `actsAsVariant` is true, at any depth — ADR-0010 §3's rule as #101/#130's valuation and #238's price rollup apply it, so a distinct entry (error, plate flaw, overprint) is out while a variant filed *under* one is in. Two numbers, never one sum: they answer different questions, and the tree already draws each child's own badge one line down. Its totals come from `countCopiesByStamp` rather than a second `groupBy`, so "a copy you hold" cannot come to mean two things; `loadStampCopyCounts` is the one entry point loading the pair, because every surface showing one shows the other. It is the one thing that makes the badge show a **zero** — `0 (+2) copies`, when the stamp has no copies of its own, which is the ordinary shape of an unknown-variant umbrella whose copies are all filed under specific variants; the no-badge-for-none rule is untouched, since that row does hold something.

- The **intake step** states the same holding as a *line* rather than a chip (#562): picking a stamp into a purchase lot is where the keep-or-sell call is taken, and answering it meant leaving the dialog for Inventory several hundred times a parcel. It carries two axes the badge does not — the **conditions** those copies are in, and **where each copy is** (`countHeldCopyRowsByStamp`, one `groupBy` over condition, delivery state **and** the disposition flags at once, because a disposition figure taken over one set beside a condition figure taken over another is a sentence whose halves describe different copies; rolled up by the pure `summarizeHeldCopies` in `held-copies.ts`, which is a separate module precisely because `copy-counts.ts` is `server-only` and the line is drawn client-side). The **counting set is the badge's exactly** — one shared `heldCopiesWhere`, so what "held" means cannot drift between the axes — and what differs is the *sentence*: the headline counts **delivered copies only**, because a copy bought and not yet arrived is rightly counted by every figure in the app (ADR-0032 §7) and "you hold 1" is still the wrong thing to say about it. Two cases hit that on the first real parcel: an auction settlement creates the lot's identified copies `ordered` with every disposition flag false (`auctions.ts`), so the line read *1 with no disposition (MNH)* at a collector holding the only physical copy in tweezers; and the second copy from the stockbook being sorted right now, entered ten minutes ago, read as existing stock. So every other bucket is its own clause with its own count and conditions and **no disposition markers** (an in-flight copy's is unset or provisional — a blank dressed up as a decision), `to_sort` standing apart from both sides since the copy is here but not filed, and the *purchase* deliberately unnamed: the useful fact is the state, and a link would not fit on one line.

- The buckets are **`COPY_BUCKETS` in `delivery-state.ts`** — `copyBucket` lifted out of `wants.ts`, which now reads it from there — one declaration of order, of which delivery state each stands for, and of both wordings the axis is read in: a bare tally (`1 held · 1 in transit`, the want list) and a sentence clause (`1 on its way`, this line). Two surfaces answering "have I got this" must not be able to disagree, and the clause hues come off `COPY_BUCKET_COLOR` for the same reason. The per-condition figures inside a clause appear only when the clause spans **more than one condition**, never merely more than one copy: with one condition the clause's count has already said it (`2 being sorted (2 MNH)`). Conditions are **listed, never ranked** — ADR-0032's rule, so *you hold a worse copy, keep this one* is not expressible and must not be invented. What the earlier draft of #562 also proposed and the issue then **cut** is **presetting the disposition chips** from the counts: the remembered last-used disposition (#121/#234) carries a run-wide intent ("this whole stockbook is stock") the counts cannot see, the interesting half of the call is the unrankable one, and a line read at a glance already answers it — a preset is a separate issue to be argued from experience, not guessed at spec time. The three `groupBy`s in `copy-counts.ts` now share one `heldCopiesWhere`, so what "held" means cannot drift between the axes. The **want marker** rides on the same line, judged by `WantChip`'s own `copy` against the condition and certificate *currently in the form* (format null — intake has no format field), which is the point: on the copy rows and in the after-intake review it speaks about a decision already taken. `listAllIssues` therefore loads `loadStampWantSummaries` beside the copy counts it always loaded, since the picker's rows are `StampDetailLine`s and the badge was already drawing there while the want chip was dark.

- **Which offers something is in** (#276, #349) is a read-only popup off the Copies, Stamps and Issues row menus, fed by `listOffersForTarget` (`offers/for-target`, a static segment ahead of `[offerId]`) — unpaginated, because a target sits on a handful of listings at most. It is scoped by an `OfferLookupTarget` (`item | stamp | issue`) mirroring the copies popup's targeting (#110), and a **stamp matches exactly**, never rolled up from its variant children — the same rule as the copies-held badge (#348), since the tree shows a child's own entry one line down and a rollup would report one listing twice. The stamp/issue surfaces go through `useOffersPopupAction`, the `{ action, dialog }` row-action hook; the Copies list keeps its panel-level dialog union. It lists **every** state, terminal included (unlike the offers list, which hides closed ones by default, #245): a sold or withdrawn listing is exactly what one asks a piece's history for. Ordered live-first (`active → paused → ready → preparing → sold → withdrawn`), newest first within a rank, so "where is this on sale right now?" is answered by the top of the list. The row reuses the Offers list's presentation (platform/state chips, needs-action + bidding badges, asking price with base-currency equivalent), opens the offer's detail screen on click, and links the platform listing separately. The action is **unconditional** — a sold copy's past listings are as much the question as a live one's, and the row carries no offer count to hide it by.

- A **platform exclusion** (#506) is the collector saying the *not offered on platform X* worklist (#259) has already been answered for one copy: `ItemPlatformExclusion`, a `(itemId, platformId)` row whose *presence* is the whole state — no reason column, because the worklist only needs the question settled and the copy's own `notes` are where a why belongs, and because a flag with nothing to fill in is what lets the bulk write be one press. Both FKs **cascade**, unlike every reference that records something that happened: an exclusion is a preference, so deleting the platform contact must not be blocked by it and the copy takes its own with it. It reads as one more `and.push` inside the same `notOfferedPlatformId` branch — every other clause there says a copy *cannot* be listed yet, this one says the collector has decided — beside `excludedPlatformId`, the review half, which carries **no `forSale` implication** on purpose: a copy set aside and then taken off sale would otherwise fall out of both readings at once and be unreachable. One `setItemPlatformExclusion` serves the row and the bulk bar, because a decision about one copy and about a thousand is the same decision; it is idempotent in both directions (`createMany … skipDuplicates` / `deleteMany`), which is what makes it safe to point at a selection already in a mix of states, and it **refuses** an unknown platform while merely *dropping* copy ids from elsewhere — the platform is what the call is about, the copies are a set. The copy form owns the whole set (`excludedPlatformIds`, absent meaning *leave alone*, a list meaning *replace*), while the row's ⋮ entry and the bulk button turn exactly one platform on or off and are therefore offered **only while the filter names one** — a decision always names a platform, and inventing a picker for it would be a second way to answer what the form already asks. Both halves live in one toolbar `<select>` (`EXCLUDED_OPTION_PREFIX`, two `optgroup`s) since the second exists only to correct the first; only the worklist choice is remembered per collection (#275), because the review is somewhere one goes to look, not the list one works from.

- A **stamp × condition collision** (#513, narrowed by #732) is the *other* duplicate question, beside #167's "this very copy is on two active offers of one platform": an offer already holding **a set of exactly these stamps in exactly these conditions**, through other copies, is the mistake worth catching before the copies go on — Colnect refuses the second offer outright, and on a marketplace that allows it the collector is still competing with themselves. Every surface below therefore states the **fact** and never a rule (#524): *this is already offered on platform X, same stamps and conditions*. The dialog lists on whatever platform was picked, so asserting Colnect's rule while creating a Delcampe offer was simply wrong.

- The pure half is `src/lib/offer-collision-rules.ts` (`collidingItemIdsByOffer`, unit-tested), keyed through `copyGroupKey` under `DEFAULT_GROUP_AXES` — literally #372's duplicate key, so the warning and the grouping cannot drift, and format/certificate never split it. A candidate the offer **already lists** is deliberately not reported: that is `containsItemIds`' fact, and one thing said twice in two vocabularies is worse than said once — but such a copy still contributes its key to its set's composition, since a *second* candidate beside it would still be the offer's second copy of that stamp.

- **The comparison is set equality, per set** (#732), and that is the correction worth recording: #513's first reading matched copy by copy, so an offer of the series 1–3 warned about selecting the single stamp 2 and offered to merge it in — but a marketplace entry is a *composition*, and on Colnect the series and the single are two different items, so listing one stamp out of a listed series duplicates nothing. Distinct keys and not a multiset, so selecting the series twice over is the same entry at a larger quantity and still collides; per **set** and not per offer, so a mixed offer is asked about each of its compositions in turn and its sets are never pooled into a third composition it does not list; and a strict subset or superset is **silent** on every surface — no chip, no facet entry, no bulk-bar warning — rather than downgraded to a quieter hint, since a warning that fires on the ordinary act of listing a single is a warning that gets read past. The answer is therefore all-or-nothing: where it fires it names the whole selection, which is why no surface counts affected copies any more.

- The read is `collidingItemIds` in `offers.ts` — **two** `offerSetItem` queries, the first narrowing to the sets that hold one of the candidates' stamps and the second loading *every* copy in those sets, because a composition cannot be compared from the members that happen to share a stamp with the selection: read that way a listed series 1–3 arrives as `{2}` and matches a `{2}` selection, which is the very report #732 exists to stop. It feeds both `findStampConditionCollisions` (route `offers/stamp-condition-collisions`, hook `useStampConditionCollisions`) and `ComposeTargetOffer.collidingItemIds`. It counts **every live offer** — preparing/ready/active/paused, unlike #167's active-only — because a duplicate is worth catching while both listings are still drafts.

- Three surfaces, all warnings and never gates: the `AddToOfferDialog` picker marks a colliding offer with an amber chip (pickable, and nothing left out of the add — only *literally* already-listed copies are dropped) and grows a **Conflicts** facet beside the state ones, which cuts across them and is therefore a group of its own, unpersisted (it is a question about *this* selection, not a way the collector likes the picker to open), hidden at count zero and **released** rather than shown empty while the targets are still loading; the stacked create form takes `notice` + `onPlatformChange` on `OfferFormDialog` and re-asks when the platform changes, since the platform is what a collision is relative to; and the Copies list's bulk bar asks only while `scopedPlatform` names one (#506's shared reading of the filter), offering **Add to #12 instead** — `AddToOfferDialog`'s `initialTargetOfferId`, which also opens the picker **on the conflict facet** and forces the state facet to *All offers* until touched — arriving from the conflict is a request to look at exactly those offers, and the remembered facet would otherwise hide the very offer the picker was opened to confirm. The bulk bar's own **＋ New offer** shortcuts and **Add selected to offer** turn amber while that warning is up (#660): the banner beside them was still being read past in real use, and the button that would create the duplicate is the last thing looked at before it is created. Colour only — both still do exactly what they say, per this bullet's own rule that every one of these surfaces is a warning and never a gate.

- **Duplicate grouping on the Copies list** (#372) collapses the list to one row per *bag of interchangeable copies*, and **multi-select** (#373) is the escape hatch for the compositions it cannot express. The key's fixed part is `stamp × condition` — Colnect refuses a second offer for the same stamp in the same condition, so a group mixing conditions would produce an offer that cannot be posted — with **format** and **certificate** as two independently joinable axes (`CopyGroupAxes`, remembered per collection). Both on, the key is exactly the one `valuateItemRows` is computed on, so every group has one unambiguous per-copy figure; where members disagree on an axis left at *any* the row is marked **mixed**, which with the axis on cannot occur by construction. The pure half — key encode/decode, `mixedAxes`, `outlierCopyIds` — is `src/lib/copy-groups.ts` (unit-tested); the read is `listItemDuplicateGroups` in `items.ts`, beside `listItemsPaginated` so both narrow through the same private `buildItemWhere`.

- Grouping is **server-side** because the list is offset-paginated: a client-side grouping would split a group at a page boundary and report two half-counts. Groups order **count descending** then `stampId` (total, so pagination cannot repeat or skip one), which is why the sort control is *hidden* rather than left offering an order it cannot honour.

- Grouping is **not filtering** — the sidebar filters decide which copies you look at, the toggles what counts as the same item, and both compose — and since #692 that is the *whole* rule: the grouping forces **no eligibility of its own**. It used to force the composition picker's (**for sale, delivered, unsold**), which greyed out the delivery-state and *Include sold* controls and re-narrowed the holdings bar, on the reasoning that listing the group as one offer was the only thing a group was for. #682 had already moved that question off the row and onto the **selection**, where the listing actions ask it of the copies ticked; the filing and issue groupings (#421/#424) never forced anything; and a mode that silently changes which copies are on screen is a mode whose row counts cannot be trusted against the list they sit in. So `GROUP_ELIGIBILITY` is gone, `groupMemberFilters` pins the key and nothing else, the delivery-state and *Include sold* / *Include no longer held* controls stay live in every mode, and the holdings bar counts the filtered set exactly as it does with no grouping at all. A copy that has left is out because the list hides sold and disposed copies until asked (#207/#394), which is the filters speaking and not the grouping.

- **Expanding a group needs no endpoint of its own**: a key *is* a filter combination, so members come back through the ordinary items query with the panel's filters plus the key (`groupMemberFilters`) — which is what the `"none"` certificate sentinel exists for, null being a value exactly as `"single"` is for format.

- The group row's own action is a **selection** shortcut, not a second listing flow (#398): its members carry the list's ordinary checkboxes and the row's own checkbox — a `⋮` entry until #422 moved it into the select gutter — ticks them, so what happens to a bag of copies is decided once, in #373's bulk bar. There was a dedicated *List all as one offer* dialog first, and two ways to compose an offer out of the same rows is one more than the screen can explain — the packaging question it answered is already `AddToOfferDialog`'s *Add as* (`createOffer`'s `seedPerCopy`: one single-copy set each rather than one set holding everything). **Outliers** (differing from the group's *most common* value on an *any* axis, never a fixed "format ≠ single" test) keep their marking on the member rows and are **left out of** the quick select-all, but tick individually like any other copy. The shortcut is **not** conditioned on the group being open: it expands the row and walks the member pages to the end before ticking (a partly loaded group would tick a subset and call it "all"), holding the request as a nonce whose *served* value is a **ref** — bookkeeping about a request is not something the row renders, and a second state would be a `setState` inside the effect that discharges it. Because a grouped panel runs no flat query, `CopySelection` carries the **items**, not ids (`onToggle(item)`, `onSetMany(items, selected)`) and the panel's selection is a `Map<id, ItemListItem>`: there is no page here to resolve a member's id against. Which copies are still free **on a platform** is deliberately not re-asked in that dialog — the list's own *Not offered on…* filter (#259) is the entry point the feature exists for, and it also pre-fills the platform into the form. `AddToOfferDialog` takes `items: ItemListItem[]` throughout; `ComposeTarget*.containsItemIds` replaced the old boolean, so an offer holding *some* of a selection keeps those copies out of the add and says so, and only one holding *every* copy is disabled. Its packaging choice is **one control, not two submit buttons** (unlike `ComposeSetDialog`), because it governs the stacked create form too, whose submit lives inside the offer form.

- **A filter change no longer throws the selection away** (#1021, 2026-09-10) — the Copies list taking the rule that governs every list with a selection and a filter, stated with what it rejects and the two surfaces it names as exceptions in [`ui-patterns.md`](ui-patterns.md) and landed one surface earlier by #1020: *a filter is a way of looking, so it never unticks anything, and a bulk action never reaches a row the collector cannot see.* Until then the selection was keyed on `JSON.stringify(filters)` and reset **during render**, so touching any filter discarded the whole thing. **That is superseded rather than overruled, and the difference is the point.** The comment beside it gave a precise reason — *a selection surviving a filter change would act on copies no longer on screen* — which was true and is now impossible: the bar counts, labels and acts on the ticked copies **in view**, so the hazard the reset existed to prevent is gone and what the reset was still costing was the collector's whole selection every time he narrowed the list. **The superseded reasoning stays in the file, quoted and dated**, because a later reader meeting a selection that survives a filter change has to be able to see that the hazard was known and is handled, or they will "fix" it back.

- **What *in view* means here had to be decided, and it is not what it means on the scans strip.** There *in view* is a predicate — every tile is loaded, so it is `tilesInView(allTiles, chip)`. Here the filters are resolved **server-side**, so nothing on the client can ask whether a copy ticked ten minutes ago still matches a search or an area subtree. What the screen *can* say is which rows it holds, which is the same answer from the other end: a copy is in view exactly when the current filter set produced it. So the lists **report** what they hold — `use-rows-in-view.ts` collects it, `rows-in-view.ts` is the pure arithmetic and is where the tests are — and the flat list needs no reporter at all, the panel having its pages in hand. Grouping (#398) is the whole of the difficulty and takes **one** reporting point: all three groupings go through `useGroupMembers`, which is where a group's members arrive. Three consequences. **A folded group still reports its members**, deliberately: expansion is a way of reading a row that is *on* the screen, its header draws the tri-state box reporting these very ticks (#422), and a bar refusing to count what that box reports would be two controls on one screen answering one question and disagreeing — it also keeps tick-a-group / fold / tick-the-next / act working, which is what a select-all is for, and `useGroupMembers` already opens a folded group on select-all so the copies are visible at the moment they are ticked. **A copy in a page nobody has scrolled to is ticked and uncounted** until it loads: nothing can reach it, nothing is lost, and it comes back on scrolling exactly as a hidden tick comes back on releasing a filter — but the count is *what has loaded*, not *what the filter holds*, and that is a real edge rather than a wrinkle in the wording. And **the whole register is dropped on a filter change**, keyed on the same signature the selection used to be reset by: a group row surviving the change holds the previous filter's members until its own query answers, and one stale id is one row a bulk action could reach.

- **Two narrowings now sit on one bar and the labels must not blur them** (#1021). *In view* is what the bar's own headline carries — `2 of 5 ticked copies in view`, with a second line saying the other three are still ticked and come back when the filter is released, drawn only while any are hidden so a resting bar reads exactly as it always did. *Qualifies* is the older one and stays where it was, on the listing buttons: `listableCopies` is the part of the selection an offer can be made of (#682), and it is now computed over the copies **in view**, so the two compose — a copy has to be on screen *and* qualify — rather than competing. Its hint says *the 3 of the 4 copies in view that are for sale and in hand*, naming which of the two questions took a copy out. Everything else on the bar acts on the copies in view: the bulk edit, the platform exclusion and its all-or-nothing *selectionExcluded* reading, and the collision query through `listableCopies`. **The bar is up while anything is ticked**, not while anything is in view — with all five hidden it reads `0 of 5` and keeps only *Clear*, that being the only place those five are visible and the only control that can still reach them, and the action group is **absent rather than disabled**, #682's own reading of the same choice. **Clearing reaches the hidden rows too**, with the hint saying so while there are any: unticking only what is on screen would empty the bar and leave ticks nothing on screen could reach, and clearing writes nothing to a copy in any case.

- A checkbox beside an `InventoryItemRow` sits in the shared `SELECT_STRIP` (`inventory-copy-list.tsx`) — a full-height `<label>` gutter with the box centred in it — on every surface that has one: a copy row is four lines tall, so a top-aligned box reads as belonging to the first line, and a 13-pixel target is poor for a list one works through by ticking. The sale-line picker keeps its own shape, where the whole row toggles.

- **Bulk location and disposition** (#682) are the selection's two non-listing actions, and adding them is what made #373's eligibility wrong: a checkbox appeared only on a copy an offer could be made of — for sale, in hand, still held — because listing was the only thing a selection had ever been for, and re-organising physical storage or re-flagging a batch is about exactly the copies that test excludes. So **every copy still held is now selectable** (`isEligible` is `disposedAt == null`), and the **listing** actions ask the old question of the *selection* instead (`isListableCopy` → `listableCopies`): they act on that subset, their labels carry its count where it differs from the bar's (`Add 4 to offer`, `New offer · 4 sets`), and with none qualifying they are **absent rather than disabled** — a selection of album copies is a perfectly good target for the actions beside them, and a dead button among live ones reads as a fault. The collision query (#513) narrows with them, a copy that cannot be listed being unable to collide. The platform exclusion (#506) deliberately does **not** narrow: it is a standing decision about a copy, which the copy form offers whatever the disposition says.

- The two new actions are **one dialog** (`bulk-edit-copies-dialog.tsx`), not two bar buttons — the bar already carries four controls, and filing a drawer of duplicates away *and* flagging it for trade is routinely one pass — with each half's *leave as is* being what keeps them independent; location is a three-way (*leave as is · move to… · clear*) because clearing a filing is not the same act as choosing none of them. Disposition is **each flag answered on its own**, with *leave as is* among its answers: never a whole combination, since the three overlap by design (a copy is in the collection *and* for sale) and writing all three would flatten every copy in a mixed selection onto whatever the dialog happened to show — but not one flag per pass either, which was the first cut and was wrong for the act it is meant to serve. A drawer moving from stock to swaps is *for trade on* **and** *for sale off*, one decision; splitting it into two applies means two writes over the same copies with the list re-fetching in between, and a half-applied state in the middle. So the flags left at *leave as is* are the ones written nothing at all, and every flag answered rides in the same update.

- The **ref rides with the location** as it does when a purchase is stored (#565): offered only while one is being chosen, and a move with the box blank clears the refs the copies carried, a slot name from the old album addressing nothing in the new one.

- **No new write**: it posts to `bulkUpdateLotItemsAction` (#121/#565), whose `LotBulkChanges` already holds exactly these fields, so a copy filed from the Copies list and one filed while its purchase was being sorted cannot be written two different ways — the client half of that contract moved to `shared/bulk-copy-changes.ts` when the second screen started sending one.

- **Condition, certificate and format** joined the same dialog in #723 rather than becoming three bar buttons, by that bullet's own reasoning: the bar is full, and the changes overlap in time — a batch relabelled to a better grade is routinely the batch whose certificate has just come back. They are `<select>`s and not the segmented controls above them because a segmented control states every answer at once, which is right for three and wrong for a collection's twenty grades. Two of them carry the **null value as an option** — *No certificate*, *Single* — beside the real ones rather than as a *Clear* mode of their own: null is a value on those axes (ADR-0006 §2; ADR-0020), so it belongs in the list, and the location's mode picker exists only because "no location" and "leave the location alone" are genuinely two acts there. **Condition has no such option at all**: `Item.conditionId` is not nullable, so an empty `conditionId` field on the wire reads as *no opinion*, while an empty `certificateStatusId` or `formatId` reads as the null value — the same present-but-empty rule `locationId` already used, which is why no sentinel string travels (one could collide with an id). `LotBulkChanges` grew the three fields and writes them in **one** `updateMany` (one decision, one pass over the rows), and both entry points validate every named dictionary row against the collection (`assertVariantDictionaries`) — a foreign id would file the copies against another collection's grade, which no read here would ever show back. There is **no history row**: a re-grade is a correction of what the copy always was, unlike a re-pointed stamp (`ItemVariantHistory` is `stampId` only). Catalog value is looked up per condition, so a re-graded copy is valued against its new grade from the next read — worth saying in the dialog, since nothing else on screen would say it.
- **Tags on copies, and the one bulk write here that is not a *set this value*** (#1181). #152 gave the collector the vocabulary and hung it on an issue and a stamp; a copy is where it earns most of its keep — *to check*, *for expertising*, *duplicate for the swap box* are statements about the piece in hand, and none of them is a question the fixed schema asks. `ItemTag` is `IssueTag`'s table one level down, cascading at both ends, and **nothing is inherited**: a tag on a stamp is not on the copies of it, and a copy carrying several stamps (ADR-0044) takes tags of its own rather than any of theirs. The copy's own screen gets the shared `TagsCard` and writes a **replace** (`setItemTags`), the issue's and the stamp's rule exactly, because its editor is the same picker. **The Copies list's bulk edit is the exception, and it is an exception of kind rather than of degree**: every other section of that dialog answers *what is this copy's location / grade / for-sale flag*, where *leave as is* is a third option beside the values — a copy carries **any number** of tags, so there is no single value a picker could show, and the pass therefore names what to **add** and what to **remove** and leaves every unnamed tag exactly where it is on each copy. A replace over a mixed drawer would flatten forty copies onto whatever the dialog happened to hold, which is precisely the silent damage a bulk action must not be able to do. The two lists ride in `LotBulkChanges` (`addTagIds` / `removeTagIds`) and are applied by `applyItemTagChanges` **inside `applyLotBulkChanges`'s own transaction**, so the fields and the labels land together or not at all; it is the one change there that cannot be an `updateMany` — a tag is a join row, so the targeted ids are resolved inside the transaction, and only when the pass actually carries tags, which is what keeps the scoped write (#172) from paying for it. A tag ticked to be added is **not offered** on the remove side and the other way round, so the contradiction cannot be stated at all; remove-before-add exists in the domain only to make the write total. The chips are drawn on the row's **copy** line, not the stamp line above it, and there is deliberately **no `⋮` quick-assign**: the bulk edit covers the many and the copy's own screen covers the one. **Filtering the Copies list by tag is #1182** and reads `item_tag(tagId)` in exactly that direction: the copy's **own** tags, under an *any* / *all* mode the panel carries in its own footer — see `catalog-and-stamps.md` for the mode's reasoning and for why *all* cannot be one `some`. It is `REMEMBERED_FILTER_KEYS`' newest pair (`tagIds` + `tagMode`, remembered together, since a remembered selection read back under the other reading is a list nobody asked for), and it unticks nothing, #1021's rule holding for it as for every filter on this bar.

- The Copies list's **three actions live in the filter bar** (#847) — *Quick offer mode*, *Scan a card*, *Add copy* — through `ListToolbar`'s `actions` slot, trailing the filters at the right of the row. They were in a header group above it, which is what #537 argued for at the time ("a way of working through the list, not a way of narrowing it, so it belongs beside Add copy"): that reasoning holds, and the group simply moved as a whole, because the header and the bar were two rows of controls a hand had to travel between to use one of each. The arrangement is `offers-list-panel.tsx`'s, followed rather than re-invented: one trailing `marginLeft: auto` group, `flexShrink: 0`, the primary action filled. **Running out of width is answered here, not left to the browser**, and answered the same way Offers answers it — the filter half is `flex: 1 1 auto` with `minWidth: 0` and its own wrap, so it shrinks and wraps within itself first, and only once that is exhausted does the actions group drop to a line of its own, still right-aligned. That ordering matters: the alternative, actions wrapping first, would put the screen's primary button under the fold on the exact screens that are most crowded.

- **The bar's figures are grouped** (#1183), decided on the purchase screen and landing here because it is one component. The reasoning is in [`purchases-and-intake.md`](purchases-and-intake.md).

- **An empty figure on the bar states no amount** (#1184) — decided on the purchase screen, landing here because it is one component. A filtered set of copies with no catalogue prices, no auction evidence or no frozen cost basis reads *not worked out yet* instead of `0.00`, while a genuinely-zero total and an **empty scope** both still read `0.00`. The rule is `src/lib/summary-figure.ts` and the reasoning is in [`purchases-and-intake.md`](purchases-and-intake.md).

- **`HoldingsSummaryBar` collapses** (#845), to its catalog-value line alone, on `offers-summary-bar.tsx`'s model (#317) — every figure it can show, shown always, is a block of numbers between the collector and the list they came for. **Catalog value leads on all three of its mounts and takes no prop to say so**: it is the one figure none of the three screens states anywhere else, the purchase screen putting what was *paid* directly above this bar and the Copies toolbar saying nothing of the kind — so the headline-per-screen prop the issue left open was not needed. **#852 then removed that premise on the two purchase mounts, and the headline moved with it**: what was paid is no longer above the bar, it is the bar's first row, so on any mount handed a `spend` the order or lot **total** leads and catalog value drops inside the expander. The rule is unchanged, not overruled — lead with the figure the screen states nowhere else — and it still takes no headline prop, the presence of `spend` being what decides; on the Copies list, which has no purchase to be a total of, catalog value leads exactly as it did. The expanded state is `usePersistedFlag` through a **`storageKey` the caller supplies, keyed by role and not by record**: one for the Copies list, one for a purchase order's own bar, one shared by every lot bar on the page. The purchase screen is what forces the prop — it mounts two of these on one page, and a single key would have made the order's bar and a lot's open and close together, which is wrong because they answer different questions; a key per *lot* would be wrong the other way, growing localStorage a row at a time and leaving a newly opened lot collapsed beside an expanded neighbour, for a preference that is really "do I want lot detail today". The **copy count** on the Copies list's line is a call-site prop for the same reason it is not on a lot's: the rows there already answer it. It comes from `countItems` / `useItemCount` and **not** from the summary beside it — `getHoldingsValuation` lifts the disposal exclusion on purpose (#396) so it can state a write-off line, so a count read off its buckets would over-report by exactly the disposed copies, silently. Grouping leaves the figure alone, deciding what a *row* is rather than which copies are in scope.

- The **Copies filter bar's order is the collector's** (#846), left to right: search, the platform worklist, delivery state, disposition, condition, certificate, format, location, the spare-filter bundle, grouping, sorting. It reads outward from *which copies* to *how they are shown*, and the two controls that are not filters — the spare bundle and grouping — sit at the end where they cannot be mistaken for one. Sorting comes last through `ListToolbar`'s `sortLast`, rather than in its usual place beside the search box, where it read as the first filter. Three things came **off** the bar in the same pass and each for its own reason: the **issue filter** went entirely (the URL parameter, the remembered key and the autocomplete — the `issueId` item filter itself stays, since the issue *groups* and the related-copies card address their members through it); the **▦ before the grouping select** went, the select's own label saying *Group …* in every state it can be in; and the **subtree switch** moved inside the location dropdown, which took two conditions with it — the switch is drawn whenever that panel is open rather than once a branch node is picked, and the panel stays open on a pick (`closeOnSelect: false`), or the control it carries appears only after the interaction that dismissed it. The search box is capped shorter than the shared default — it is the one control here that is never left set, so its width was the cheapest to give back. The four spare switches (`SPARE_FILTERS`) are one grouped `MultiSelectFilter`, and the grouping is what makes them readable: two narrow, two widen, and the predicates behind all four are exactly what they were as chips.

- The Copies list's **grouping is a mode, not a flag** (#421, #424): `none | duplicates | location | ref | issue`, one persisted per-collection preference, because they are readings of one list and exactly one can be in effect. The **control** it is picked in is a `SingleSelectFilter` carrying #372's two duplicate-key switches inside its own panel (#868, `ui-patterns.md`) — read that before moving anything on that bar, because the rule it is built to is *choosing a filter must never change the layout of the filter bar*, and the mode's own labels and the sort control's reason for being greyed are pure in `lib/copy-grouping.ts` rather than inline in the panel. The mode reaching the control is the **effective** one, not the stored one: a filing grouping remembered from before the last location was deleted already fell back to the flat list, and the dropdown must not claim a grouping the list is not under.

- **Filing groups** collapse the list by *where a copy is*, and differ from #372's duplicate groups on every axis that matters. They force **no eligibility** — "where is it?" is a fair question about a sold copy still on the shelf, so `Include sold` / `Include no longer held` stay live and the holdings bar is not re-narrowed. A location groups **exactly**, never rolled up: a nested location is its own row stating its full path, since a roll-up would report one copy under every ancestor and the location filter's own subtree scope (#385) is already how one asks for a branch. A ref only means anything inside its location, hence `(locationId, locationRef)` and not `locationRef` alone. The reading order is location path then #330's `compareLocationRef`, neither of which SQL can express, so `listItemLocationGroups` reads the groups **whole and pages them in memory** — bounded by the collection's locations × refs in use, not by its copies, and a *total* order, which is what makes paging over it neither repeat nor skip a group. Null and `""` refs are folded into one "no ref" group after the `groupBy`, which the database cannot do for us. Two sentinels carry the buckets an absent filter cannot express — `NO_LOCATION` / `NO_LOCATION_REF` (`location-groups.ts`, pure, so a client row can name them without importing a server module), the `"single"`/`"none"` pattern again — and a group addresses its own members through the **ordinary items query** with `locationExact: true`, so no per-group endpoint exists. A filing row is deliberately **thin** next to a duplicate row: a group is a *place*, so it states the place and the count, and what the copies are is what the member rows answer.

- **Issue groups** (#424) are the same shape a third time — thin row, no eligibility forced, read whole and paged in memory, members addressed through the ordinary items query — and differ in the two places the subject differs. The reading order is the **Issues list's own** (year, then `primaryCatalogSortKey`, then name; #181), because a collector scanning copies by series is reading the list they browse the catalogue in and two orders for one subject is two things to learn; the issue-less bucket sorts last, being the absence of the subject rather than a thin edge of it. And the issue lives on the *stamp*, which `groupBy` cannot reach, so the filtered set is scanned and counted in memory over a two-field select — `listItemYearFacets`'s own shape, the year being on the stamp for the same reason. Membership is many-to-many but a copy is counted under **one** issue, its stamp's first, so the groups partition the list and their counts add up to it; `FIRST_ISSUE_MEMBERSHIP` (`orderBy: { issueId: 'asc' }, take: 1`) is what makes that *one* answer — an unordered `take: 1` let the row, the grouping and the title reads each name a different issue for one copy, which is a latent bug this fixed rather than a new rule. Counting on the first membership while reading members back through the ordinary `issueId` filter (which matches **any**) is #172's existing pair, deliberately not a third rule: they differ only for a stamp filed in two issues, and an issue filter meaning "and no earlier one" would make the sidebar's own filter answer a question nobody asks of it.

- An issue group header also states **how complete each of the issue's checklists is, condition by condition** (#594) — `MNH 3/5 · U 5/5`, one chip per condition, and no gap where a condition is finished. A **finished condition is filled in `success` and ticked** (#671), not merely tinted: a green numeral beside a grey one is not what an eye scanning six chips at 0.75rem catches, and being caught is the whole job of this chip — so fill, weight and glyph say it together, and the chip still reads complete where colour does not carry. The treatment is `SET_COMPLETENESS_CHIP_COMPLETE` in `chip-styles.ts`, shared with #563's lot header, whose chip gave up the words “— complete” for the same tick: a finished set must not read as an achievement on one screen and a slightly greener number on the other. It is #519's grid minus its disposition axis, and the axis is missing because the two surfaces mean different things by *which copies*: the grid asks the whole collection, so it has to say; a group header describes the rows underneath it, so **the copies it counts are the ones the list is showing** — every filter in force, exactly the `where` the group counts come from (`listIssueGroupCompleteness`, beside `listItemIssueGroups` so both narrow through the same private `buildItemWhere`). A list narrowed to one klaser reporting a series complete out of copies filed three rooms away is the failure this avoids, and the price is a fraction that moves with the filters, which is why the chip's hover states its scope outright — the deliberate opposite of #563's lot header, whose figure ranges over the collection's whole for-sale stock precisely because it is about acting on stock that is *not* on screen.

- That hover is **figures first, in an aligned two-column block, and the caveat after** (#670): it was one run-on sentence with the numbers bolded inside it, and the numbers are what it is opened for — a fraction read out mid-clause is slower than the chip already on screen. Label left, figure right and right-aligned, the issue page's own grid again; the scope sentence follows in muted text, where a caveat belongs. The complete-set count **prints at zero here**, unlike on the chip line, because the figure has been asked for: *held the set once over* and *held five of eight* are different answers, and a row that disappears at zero makes the reader guess which one they got. Membership is the **checklist's**, not the grouping's: a stamp whose first issue is elsewhere is counted here while its copies sit in that other group, since a set does not stop being incomplete because one of its stamps is filed under a neighbouring series.

- The chips are **sparse** — only conditions actually held get one, the format axis's rule (#133), a dictionary of eight against a series held in two being six zeros per row — and the **complete-set count stays in the hover**, a second number per chip being a line nobody reads. The pure half is `computeConditionCompleteness` (`checklist-completeness-rules.ts`), sharing the owned / complete-sets tally with the grid's `gridRows` so one figure is not computed two ways. Read **batched over the groups on screen** through a route of its own (`items/issue-groups/completeness`), never per row: that is what #133 meant by keeping the expensive breakdown out of the base list query, and it is what lets a completeness figure sit on a list at all — the cost is a re-ask as scrolling lengthens the id list, and the alternative is a request per group.

- **Reset filters** (#733) is the other half of #693: a screen that remembers a dozen filters has to offer one way back out of them, or the memory becomes a thing to be undone control by control on every visit. It is a single write through the panel's one `updateParams` funnel naming **every** remembered key with `""` — which is exactly what clears the stored set too, since a key left unnamed is read straight back out of storage by `remember` (`ui-patterns.md`) — plus the search box and both halves of the platform worklist (#275/#506, whose own stored value is cleared beside it). What it deliberately does **not** touch is the **area and the year**: `use-collection-filter-store` shares those with the Stamps and Wants lists (#143), so clearing them here would re-shape two screens nobody was looking at — the button says *this list*, and it has to mean it. The subtree scopes are untouched for a different reason: they are ways of *reading* a tree (#385), global and not narrowings at all. `hasResettableFilters` is therefore a **narrower** predicate than the empty state's `hasActiveFilters`, which still counts the year — a control offered when clicking it would change nothing is worse than no control. The button renders only while something is on, as bare accent text rather than a fourteenth chip (the `offers/allegro` *Clear filters* shape), since a control that looks like its neighbours reads as one more way of narrowing the list. Clearing the **search box** is what made this need a change in `list-toolbar.tsx`: the box debounces into its own `useState`, so before #733 an external `search` was silently ignored and the field would have gone on showing a phrase that was no longer filtering anything. The toolbar now resyncs on a `search` it did not itself push, tracked in a ref — comparing the prop against the *input* instead would pull half-typed text back out from under whoever is typing, the prop lagging by the debounce for exactly that whole time.

- The Copies list **remembers its filters, not just how it is grouped** (#693). Grouping mode, the duplicate-key axes, the sort and the platform worklist were each remembered on their own (#372/#421/#424, #325, #275) while the dozen filters beside them were not, so every visit started by re-narrowing a list the collector had already narrowed — and the memory a screen has of itself should not stop at the controls that happen to sit outside the URL. One `usePersistedFilterParams` per collection (see `ui-patterns.md` for the mechanism and why `updateParams` is where it is written) covers condition, format, certificate, delivery state, location, issue, the three disposition flags, *No photos*, *Missing catalog value* and both *Include* toggles. A URL that names a filter still wins over the stored one filter by filter, so a link from another screen reads as it always did, and touching any control stores the set as it then stands.

- Every **grouped row shares one shell** (`inventory/copy-group-shell.tsx`, #422): the expand arrow, the select-all box, the member list and the page-walking `useGroupMembers`. The arrow is **vertically centred** (a group row is several lines tall, and one pinned to the top reads as belonging to the first line), and quick select-all (#398) is a **checkbox in the same `SELECT_STRIP` gutter the member copies use** — one click, in the column the eye is already in — rather than a `⋮` entry, which was two clicks and a menu to read for the thing the row is most used for. The duplicate row's `⋮` therefore disappeared entirely: it held nothing else. `allSelected` stays **derived from the copies actually ticked**, never from the pending request, so a box can never claim "all" before the page walk that makes it true has finished. The count chip is the shell's too since #869 (`GROUP_COUNT_CHIP`), having been written out three times: the three headers are meant to agree, and a rule about where the count sits is not one anybody can hold while the chip itself is copied per file. **The shape all three now share is one line.** Line 1 is the `×N` count and *what the group is* — for a filing group its place, for an issue group its name and year, and for a duplicate group the **keying**: the condition, plus the format and the certificate where those axes are on (#869). Everything else is a line that is drawn **only when it has something on it**. The duplicate row is what forced this: its keying chips had a line of their own at the foot of the row, and with both optional axes off — their default — that line carried one small condition badge and nothing else, which is what the collector reported. The keying went up rather than the condition alone, because condition, format and certificate are the same kind of fact and splitting them across two lines would have left the emptiest line still being drawn. What stays behind on that last line is the *status* — mixed formats, mixed certificates, `N of M already listed` — a different kind of statement, and an occasional one, so the line goes with it when none of the three applies. A duplicate group row is therefore three lines in the ordinary case and four when something is mixed or listed, where a copy row is always four: a copy row's last line carries per-copy facts (its number, its disposition, where it is filed) that a group simply has none of.

- A **sold** copy carries its own chip on the copies list (#393), tinted `success` rather than the disposal chip's error tint — it left the collection the way it was meant to, so the chip states an outcome instead of raising an alarm. It can only ever appear under *Include sold* (#207), which is exactly the mixed view it exists for. `ItemListItem.sold` comes from `_count.saleLineItems` on `ITEM_LIST_SELECT` — the very relation `excludeSold` filters on, so the chip and the filter cannot disagree. Soldness stays outside `isHeld` (#394), as it always has.

- A **detail page reads; it does not become a second editor** (#517/#518/#519). A copy, a stamp and an issue each have a screen of their own — `inventory/[itemId]`, `stamps/[stampId]`, `issues/[issueId]` — reached from the list row's `⋮` menu — first entry — **and promoted to the row's quick actions** (`useDetailPageAction`, `shared/use-detail-page-action.ts`), first icon there too, since going to a record's screen is the one thing every one of these rows can do. The icon is on **rows inside the detail screens as well** (an issue's stamp tree, a stamp's variants and issues, the copies on either) through the same `RowQuickActions`: a row in a card is still a row, and reaching a record should not be a different gesture depending on which screen you met it on. That is also why a `readOnly` copy row renders that icon rather than none — `readOnly` means the copy is not edited from a screen that is not the Copies list, and it would be an odd kind of read-only that refused to let you read more (so the #110 popup gains it too). Since #676 it may render a second one, **Edit stamp**, where the screen passes `onEditStamp`: that does not soften what `readOnly` means, since the copy is still not edited from there — what opens is the shared editor for the *stamp* behind it, a record the surface never claimed to own. The offer's sets are the screen that asks for it, being where a wrong catalog number is noticed while the listing text is being written. Whole-row click was weighed against the icon and **rejected** for these rows, the `offer-row.tsx` / `OfferTargetRow` precedent notwithstanding: a copy row is selectable (#373's checkbox is right beside it) and an issue-tree row expands, so a click there is already spoken for, and the rows are dense with controls that each mean something else (a chip copies, a thumbnail opens a lightbox, a price edits inline). They **consolidate**, they do not replace: the popups they gather (#110 copies, #114 prices, #276/#349 offers) stay exactly where they are, because a popup answers one question without leaving the list and a page is for when the whole picture is wanted — and every field on these screens is still written through the dialog that already owned it, a second editing surface for one record being a second place to keep honest.

- *Reads* is about **fields**, and #630 is what draws the line: the stamp screen's Variants card adds, edits, deletes and reorders the variants under the stamp, because a variant tree is the relationship *between* stamps rather than a field of any one of them, it has no other home on that screen, and building one (`309 → 309A → 309AP → 309APa`) otherwise meant leaving for the Issues list and expanding back to where you were four times over. The card gains the tree's **operations** and not one field of its own — every write goes through `StampFormDialog` / `DeleteStampDialog`, the very dialogs the Issues list opens — so the invariant holds where it means something. #673 draws the same line from the other side: the copy screen carries **Edit** and **Identify variant** on its identity band, and both open the Copies list's own dialogs (`InventoryItemFormDialog` in edit mode, `IdentifyVariantDialog`), so there is still exactly one editor per record and not one field on the page becomes typeable in place. What they remove is the trip back to the list to hunt down the row the page was opened from, which is the whole reason the screen exists. They sit on the identity band and not on the cards they would change, being about the copy as a whole — a button per card is four of them saying the same thing — and **Identify variant** follows the row's own rule and shows only on an unknown-variant copy, there being nothing to decide elsewhere. The page is server-rendered, so a save is shown by `router.refresh()`: the same copy, from the same query, with nothing on the client to keep in step. It raises **no want review** (#532), unlike the list's own edit: that question belongs where copies arrive in bulk, and the purchase screen's copy edit already does without it.

- #751/#752 finish the thought and make it the **rule for every detail screen**: the stamp and issue screens gained the same band button (`StampFormDialog` / `IssueDialog`, the Issues list's own two), and the purchase screen an **Edit header** beside its status control (`PurchaseFormDialog`) — so a record's own screen can now start the edit of that record wherever one exists. The sale, auction-sale and trade screens already had theirs and were left alone. What it is **not** is a licence to type on the page: every one of these opens the list's dialog, so there is still exactly one editor per record, and the invariant is about *fields on the page*, not about which surface may open a dialog. The **offer screen is the deliberate exception** — every field there is already edited in place through `InlineText` (#209/#363), so a dialog over the same fields would be the second surface the invariant warns about, and it was ruled out under #752 rather than overlooked. The wiring is the same on all three: `router.refresh()` because the pages are server-rendered, plus the originating list's invalidation in the same breath, since the collector arrived from one of its rows and the back link is the way out. On the stamp and issue screens that invalidation is `invalidateStampsAndIssues`, one call for both catalogue caches (#918); the purchase screen keeps `useInvalidateInventory`'s own. The purchase screen routes its save through the panel's existing `run`, which already does both. **Where the button sits** follows #673 — the identity band on a detail-kit page, and beside the status control on the purchase screen, which is the other thing on that card that changes the order rather than its lots. `PurchaseFormDialog`'s `purchase` prop is a `Pick` of the header fields (`PurchaseHeaderFields`) rather than `PurchaseListItem`, because the order's own screen reads through `PurchaseDetail`: both carry those seven and neither should have to pretend to be the other; `PurchaseDetail` gained `contactId` / `platformId` for it, the dialog's two contact pickers being addressed by id. See `catalog-and-stamps.md` for the issue-scoping that follows from it.

- The chrome is one kit (`shared/detail-page.tsx`: `DetailLayout`, `DetailCard`, `DetailFullRow`, `FieldGrid`, `Field`, `EmptyNote`), and the layout is a **dashboard, not a column**: one column of cards down the middle of a wide monitor is a screenful of scrolling beside a grey plain. It is **two explicit columns** — `DetailColumns` + one `DetailColumn` per side — and **which card sits where is each screen's own decision**, stated in its JSX. The split is the same question on all three: the left column is what the record *is*, the right is what the collection *does* with it (copies, offers, purchase, sale) — which is also the reading order when a narrow window collapses the two into one. Two earlier attempts are worth not repeating: a `grid` places a card in a *row*, so the row is as tall as its tallest card and every shorter one beside it leaves a hole, and `dense` packing cannot rescue it (it only pulls a card forward into a gap something actually fits into, and a four-field card against a stamp tree fits nowhere); CSS **multi-column** does fix the holes — it balances columns rather than filling rows — but it *decides the order*, and the pairings that make these screens readable (an issue's stamp tree beside its completeness grid, a copy's physical facts beside its trade history) are not something a height-balancing algorithm knows about. So the columns ending at different heights is **accepted**, the alternative being a hole or an order nobody chose; keeping the taller column's tail short is what the ordering is for. `auto-fit` over a minimum width is the single-column fallback and is not a breakpoint — this app has none, being desktop-only.

- **A card with nothing on it is not rendered at all** (#536) — `empty` on `DetailCard`, which returns `null`, so emptiness is the card's own business rather than each page remembering its own `&&`. The Notes card was the first to work this way and the Wants card (#532) the second; #536 made it the rule, because a heading over "never sold" is furniture that answers a question nobody asked and pushes the answers that do exist further down a column these screens are trying to keep short. Pass a query's loading flag into `empty` too: a card that appears once, filled, beats one that flashes an empty shell or fills and then vanishes. `EmptyNote` survives for the other case — a card that *does* hold something and has nothing to draw right now (a checklist filter that matched none of the tree). The **exception** is a section whose empty state carries the only way to create the first entry ("No lots yet. Add a priced lot…" on a purchase order, "No lots in this sale yet" on an auction sale): that is the way in, not clutter, and it stays — which is why the older detail screens (#165 offer, #166 sale, #120 purchase) were audited under #536 and left as they are. Vertical spacing is a **margin per card**, not `gap`: `row-gap` does nothing in multi-column; the container cancels the last one. `DetailCard` carries `break-inside: avoid`, which costs nothing outside a masonry. `DetailFullRow` is also the identity band across the top.

- Each card that shows *related records* is shared with the popup it mirrors rather than re-rendered — `RelatedOffersCard` (which owns `OfferTargetRow`, the popup importing it back) and `RelatedCopiesCard` (the popup's `InventoryCopyList` in `readOnly`), so a listing or a copy cannot read one way inline and another in a dialog. The header identity is `shared/stamp-identity.tsx`, shared by the copy and stamp screens for the same reason. Each page reads through the **list's own** enrichment — `getStampListItem` / `getIssueListItem` beside the existing `getItemListItem` — so the page and the row it was opened from cannot describe one record differently; the copy page reads through `listItemsPaginated({ ids })` rather than `getItemListItem`, because that one is owner-scoped and the URL carries a collection slug.

- `CatalogPricesCard` shows the **cross-catalog averages** inline and puts the per-edition breakdown behind #114's own dialog: the average is the figure a value is read off, and copying the whole matrix onto three screens would be #114 rewritten three times.

- The issue screen's **completeness grid** (`issue-completeness.ts` + the pure `issue-completeness-rules.ts`) is the disposition × condition half of #133, computed on demand for one issue exactly as #133 reasoned — the list keeps #54's lightweight indicator. Two figures, and they answer different questions: **owned** is progress, **complete sets** is the *minimum* across every required member, so one missing stamp is zero however deep the pile of the others. Dispositions **overlap** (a copy is in the collection *and* for sale), so the rows never sum to *Any*; the exclusions are `copy-counts.ts`'s exactly, or the card and the badge beside it would disagree about what having one means. Format is deliberately **not** an axis here — that is the rest of #133.

- In-location refs (`A234`) are ordered by `compareLocationRef` (`src/lib/location-ref.ts`): prefix first, then the trailing number, separators ignored, blanks last. Use it anywhere refs are sorted — a plain numeric collator disagrees as soon as the separator varies.

- A **multi-stamp copy** — an indivisible carrier bearing more than one stamp: a cover, a fragment, an FDC — has its **schema and its write module** ([ADR-0044](../decisions/0044-multi-stamp-copies.md); #744 done, #745 the exclusion, #746 the editor, #747 valuation, #748 the list, #749 tokens, #750 intake still open). Nothing on this page behaves differently yet: every copy has exactly one entry and a `stampCount` of 1 until #746 gives the collector a way to add a second. Read the ADR before touching anything on this page that counts copies. The rule it turns on: `Item` stays one physical thing (ADR-0020 §2's refusal to decompose, applied to a carrier), a new `ItemStamp` holds **every** stamp on it with a quantity and an optional per-component format, `Item.stampId` degrades to a denormalised pointer at the first entry — kept only because 171 files read it — and **a carrier holding more than one stamp is a copy of none of them**, the leading one on exactly the same terms as the rest. That last part is what reaches this file: the copy drops out of `heldCopiesWhere`, the checklists, wants, completeness, `duplicate-catalog.ts`, `auction-duplicates.ts` and the Colnect source predicates, while staying fully a copy for everything about the *object* — offers, sales, trades, location, disposal, cost basis, photos, `itemNo`. The trigger is a materialised `Item.stampCount`, so each of those stays a flat `where` rather than a join. The carrier type is a `StampFormat` row like any other, which is also what makes a **one-stamp** cover price itself through ADR-0020 §5 with nothing new built. What #744 settled beyond the ADR: `ItemStamp.stampId` **cascades** rather than restricting — a RESTRICT there is checked before the cascades queued beside it, so it fired on every ordinary copy and made a collection undeletable — and the refusal the ADR wanted lives in `deleteStamp`, which removes the stamp's own copies and then refuses if another piece still carries it. Every path that creates a copy writes its one entry through `item-stamps.ts` in the same transaction, so an `Item` with no entry is not a state the app can produce.
