# Market Valuation & the Valuation Dialog

What the market paid, as against catalogue value and what the collector paid — and where each shows.

- **Market valuation** (#24/#455/#456; ADR-0022) is the layer above auction tracking: what the market *paid*, as against `valuation.ts`'s catalogue valuation of copies (ADR-0007 §7) — two modules that are never merged, because they answer different questions. The arithmetic is pure, in `src/lib/market-value.ts`: a **datapoint** per `AuctionLotLine` of a `closed` lot carrying a `finalPrice`, at `finalPrice × fxRateToBase` (the rate frozen at `endsAt`, #354) and **excluding** premium and shipping, since those are the seller's terms rather than the stamp's worth. The derived outcome is deliberately not consulted — `won`, `lost` and `observed` are all real prices, and dropping the won ones would discard the results the collector knows best. A single-line lot is `amount ÷ quantity` and needs no catalogue price at all; a **mixed** lot is split pro-rata by each line's *total* catalogue value (unit × quantity) and is skipped **whole** if any line has none, because a partial split hands the missing line's share to its neighbours. A foreign-currency lot with no frozen rate yields nothing — `AuctionLot.fxRateToBase` is null both when no conversion was needed and when none could be had, so the pure input carries an explicit `inBaseCurrency` flag beside the rate. The key is `stamp × condition × certificate × format` — the `StampCatalogPrice` key minus the **edition** axis, since a hammer price belongs to a date and not to a published book — with nulls matched exactly, no fall-back. Aggregation is over **all** datapoints with no minimum sample and no time window (a self-built base cannot afford a threshold that throws away most of what has been recorded), and the headline is the **median**; thin and stale evidence is instead answered by a 0–100 **confidence score** from sample · recency · agreement · purity, bucketed `low`/`medium`/`high`, always shown next to the facts that produced it and never keyed off by any rule. `realizationRatio` is `median ÷ catalogueValue` in base currency, and a key with no datapoints shows **no** market value — nothing is ever synthesized from comparable keys. The server half is `src/lib/market-values.ts` + `GET …/stamps/market-value?stampId=…`: computed **on demand, nothing stored** (ADR-0022 §7 — no table, no queued recompute, no invalidation), one query for the closed lots *mentioning* the asked-about stamps and then **all** of those lots' lines, whose catalogue values come from `valuateItemRows` so the unknown-variant rollup (#238) and format pricing (ADR-0020) are reused rather than re-derived.

- **Where market value shows** (#457/#458; ADR-0022 §8) is two surfaces and no third: the **Valuation dialog** — the read-only window behind a row's ⋮ → *Show valuation*, renamed from *Catalog prices* because the catalogue is now one of two answers it carries and a window named after one of them hides the other — and the holdings summary bar. Deliberately **not** the stamp *edit* dialog's Prices tab, where the ADR first put it and where this was built before being moved: that tab is where catalogue prices are **typed in**, and a figure that cannot be edited sitting under a grid of inputs reads as one more thing to fill in, while the Valuation dialog is already read-only, already covers a checklist as well as a stamp, and is already reached from every list a stamp appears on. The section **leads** that dialog, above the cross-catalogue average and in the same `CollapsibleSection` box every other section is (extracted to `shared/collapsible-section.tsx` for it), open by default like the average: a section drawing a heading of its own would read as a caption on the page rather than as one more thing to open, and burying it under a dozen collapsed edition tables is how a second answer goes unread. It is rendered at the dialog's top level and fetched on its own, so it answers while the catalogue tables are still loading and is not repeated in each half's no-prices branch. The toolbar toggles stay **pinned above** it even though neither reaches it — they are the window's controls, and a control that scrolls away is one a reader has to go looking for. It is the dialog's **own grid** — conditions × certificates, `MatrixTable` extracted to `shared/price-matrix.tsx` and shared with the catalogue tables, certificate columns unioned across all of them (`marketCertCells`) so one appearing only in an auction result still earns a column everywhere: a median and a list price are read cell against cell, and a reader learns the window once. The cell is a **button** — a grid holds a number but not an argument, so the lots open *under* the table, one key at a time, and the rest (mean, range, n, span, ratio) is the hover. The confidence badge is the **colour of the amount** (`marketConfidenceColor`, the chip's own three), never a chip beside it: a chip made every column ragged, and a column of prices that does not line up cannot be scanned — which is most of what a grid is for. The score is still spelled out in the hover and in `aria-label`, colour alone being nothing a reader can be required to act on; `MarketConfidenceChip` itself stays, for #511's popover and for that hover, both of which have the width for it. The hover is a **panel of labelled lines**, not a sentence — mean, range, sample, span and the catalogue comparison strung together with separators ran into a block that had to be picked apart word by word — and it is the existing `Tooltip` with a new `maxWidth` prop rather than a hover popover of its own: the placement, the viewport clamping and the nesting rules are the hard part and are already right there. The **format is a table, not a column**: it is the axis the catalogue grid has nowhere to put, so each format is its own matrix under its own caption, captioned only when more than one is on screen. Which is also why the market read carries `conditionSortOrder` / `certificateSortOrder` / `formatSortOrder` — the grid is laid out on them, and `-1` for a null certificate or format is the same convention that makes *No cert.* and the single lead everywhere else. Neither toolbar toggle reaches it: a hammer price has no catalogue edition and is aggregated in base currency to begin with. A key with **no** datapoints is absent rather than zero, no-catalogue-prices no longer short-circuits the dialog to an empty state (a single-line lot yields a datapoint with no catalogue price at all, §3), and the no-evidence case is a sentence naming where the figures would come from — an absent section is indistinguishable from one that failed. A **checklist** gets the members' medians summed per cell, and what replaces the per-stamp badge there is **coverage** on the cell's second line (*7 of 40*, amber below full), with no expansion — a set's evidence is every lot of every member: averaging forty confidence scores describes nothing in particular, while a total resting on a fifth of the set is the fact a reader needs. `MarketConfidenceChip` is shared with #511's bid popover rather than drawn twice, two chips being how `medium` stops meaning one thing. The collection total is `aggregateMarketHoldings` beside `aggregateHoldings` in `valuation.ts`, fed by `readMarketMedians` — a *projection* of `readStampMarketValues`, never a second query, so the figure a total is summed from and the figure the dialog prints cannot drift apart. Its **coverage counts are the point**: `valuedCount` / `noEvidenceCount` ride beside it and a copy with no results contributes nothing rather than a catalogue-derived stand-in; copies no longer held are out, exactly as #396 has them. `valuateItemRows` moved to its own `item-valuation.ts` (re-exported from `items.ts`) for one reason: `market-values.ts` values a lot's lines with it and `items.ts` now needs the medians back, and a module below both is what keeps that from being an import cycle.

- **Estimated value** (#602; ADR-0022 §6 as revised) is the dialog's fourth answer and the only one that is not a measurement: `catalogue × the learned realization ratio` (#520; ADR-0029 §2), per cell, between **Market value** and **What I paid**. It exists because the two screens contradicted each other about the same stamp — the lots screen recommending 35.50 from `catalogue 105.00 × 34%` at the ladder's collection-wide bucket, the dialog answering *"No auction results recorded for this stamp yet"* — and nothing on screen said the two answer different questions. §6's refusal to synthesize an estimate **into the Market value figure** still holds and is why this is a section of its own with no confidence badge; the ADR was **revised in place** rather than quietly contradicted, on the ADR-0029 §8 precedent. **Scope is the dialog and nothing else**: the holdings summary bar, the collection value and the checklist set totals stay measurement-only, because a ratio resolves for very nearly every cell of every stamp (bucket 4 needs three ratios in the whole collection) and feeding it into a total would turn "what these have fetched" into "catalogue times a constant" while still wearing the measured figure's name. Three rules keep it from reading as measured, and each is a rule about not overstating: a cell with a **measured median is left empty** (an extrapolation printed beside the measurement invites reading the gap between them as a signal, and it is not one); a cell with **no catalogue value is empty**, there being nothing to multiply; and there is **no confidence badge and no confidence colour** — ADR-0022 §5 scores the datapoints *for this key*, of which an estimated cell has none, so borrowing the badge would make the estimate look measured. The **fallback rung still renders**, in the warning tone and labelled *policy, not evidence*, because that is exactly where the collector most needs to know which it is. It is a **grid of its own, never a column of Market value's**, and the reason is the ratio's shape rather than tidiness: the ladder buckets on *condition*, so each **row** resolves its own bucket at its own `n`, and the bucket's name is the whole justification for the figure — `MatrixTable` grew `renderRowAside` (plus `labelWidth`) so the row can state it beside the condition instead of hiding it in a hover, which is the mistake the section exists to correct. The figure is `≈`-prefixed and muted italic — #238's existing vocabulary for *inferred, not recorded* — and clicking it expands the lots the **bucket** was learned from, other stamps' lots by construction. The domain half is `src/lib/estimated-values.ts`: keys enumerated from the catalogue price rows (the stamp's own, plus its descendants' when it is an unknown-variant umbrella — the rollup itself stays `valuateItemRows`'s, so a key from a non-variant descendant simply resolves to nothing and drops out), catalogue values from **`valuateItemRows`, never the dialog's cross-catalogue average**, since that is the ratio's own denominator and the figure the bid recommendation multiplies — reconciling the two screens is the point, so it has to be the same number. The ladder gained `ResolvedRatio.observations` (the deduped list the median was taken over) and `RealizationRatioResolver.describeLots`, deliberately *not* on `RealizationRatio`: only the expanding surface wants lot rows, while `resolve` runs per line for a whole page of lots (#511). A **set** follows Market value's rule — a total with its coverage count and no expansion — and the two counts are complementary, since a member with a measured median at a key is counted there and never here; a row can hold several buckets, so one is named only when the members agree and the count is stated otherwise.

- **What the collector paid** (#560) is the Valuation dialog's third answer and the only one that is a fact about *this collection* rather than about the stamp — market value is what other people paid (#457), the catalogue is what a published book asks. It sits **below Market value and above the catalogue sections**: those tables are the evidence market value is read against so they belong under it, and what was paid is evidence for neither — a different axis, placed beside the other transaction-derived answer. Laid out on the same `condition × certificate × format` grid (`FormatTables` moved to `price-matrix.tsx` for the second reader), sharing the window's unioned certificate columns, so "I paid 40 / the market pays 55 / the catalogue lists 120" reads off one cell position. The arithmetic is pure, in `cost-basis.ts` beside `resolveCostBasis` (`aggregatePurchaseCostsByKey`); `src/lib/purchase-costs.ts` does only what needs a database. **The figures cover the settled copies alone**: a `pending` cost basis (#123 — the copy sits in an open lot, its share of the pool not worked out) and a `none` are **counted on their own line under the figure**, never averaged in at zero and never guessed at, which is why a cell can carry counts and a — instead of a number; that is an answer, and an absent cell would say "none held". Scope is the held, unsold copies of **that stamp exactly** — `copy-counts.ts`'s filter and #348's no-rollup rule, deliberately *not* #238's catalogue rollup from the lowest variant child: a price is a property of the stamp, a copy is a thing on the table. Stamp-only — a checklist's cost is a sum over members, not an average over one key.

- The Valuation dialog is reachable from **every list a stamp appears on** (#114) — the Copies list row's ⋮ was the last that could not open it, its only route being *Edit stamp*, an edit surface where the question was read-only. Ungated there, unlike the stamps and issues rows which still gate on `mainCatalogPrice`: the window answers three questions now, and the two that are not the catalogue stand on their own — a row that *is* a copy always has something for #560 to say. An **auction lot's composition line** reaches it too (#601), from both of the composition's surfaces — the watchlist's *Lot contents* dialog and the sale screen's lot cards — because the question a ceiling is set against is what the stamp has actually fetched, and the line already names the `stamp × condition × certificate × format` the grid is read on. Ungated for the same reason the Copies row is, and it **survives `readOnly`**: the line row's flag suppresses the *editing* entries, on the argument that the edit form is not on screen, which says nothing about a read-only window — the same split the `+ catalog value` slot already makes. In the dialog surface it is portalled to `<body>` beside the two dialogs already there, that panel being transform-centred and so the containing block for a nested `position: fixed`. One dialog per surface remembering which line opened it, not `usePriceDetailsAction` per row — a hook cannot be called in a loop, which is `useChecklistPriceActions`' shape (#531).
