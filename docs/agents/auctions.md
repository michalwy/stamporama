# Auction Tracking & Bidding

The auction watchlist model, bid anchors and recommendations, exposure, and the auction screens.

- **Auction tracking** (#23; ADR-0021, schema in #350) is a *bidding watchlist with a fork at the end*, not a register of market results fed from outside — the market data is a by-product of losing. `AuctionSale ⊃ AuctionLot ⊃ AuctionLotLine`. The parent is **one settlement with one seller** — what ships in one parcel — never "an auction as an event": a house's own sale for a house, an open-ended basket of everything currently bid on with one seller for a marketplace. That definition is load-bearing, because it makes shipping distribution fall out of ADR-0009 §3 for free (the grouping is made at bidding time, so settlement never asks "which purchase does this go into?") and it is what lets the sale-level rollup add shipping **once**. Seller and platform are two `Contact`s exactly as on `Purchase`, both `Restrict`. Currency and the two fee components live on the **seller** and are **seeded** onto the sale — the #308/#319 seeding rule again, so a seller raising their premium cannot re-price a parcel already tracked or settled — and `platformCurrency` (#196) does not apply, because an aggregator like philasearch carries houses listing in EUR, CHF and GBP alike. Outcome is **per lot, never per sale**: within one settlement some lots are won and others lost. The bid is a single overwritten figure with `checkedAt` and **no history** — refreshing is manual (no scraping, no scheduled polling), so `checkedAt` answers the one question a history would; `finalPrice` stays optional, because a lot that vanished from view is an absent observation rather than an error. **Recording the outcome** (#354, `recordAuctionLotOutcome`) is `won` / `lost` / `cancelled` / back to `watching` from the row's ⋮ menu. The two priced outcomes share one dialog and differ in exactly one rule: a lost lot's price is **optional** (an unseen result is an absent observation) and a won lot's is **required** (you cannot have missed what you paid, and #28 prices its purchase line from it — a `won` lot is also the input that action operates on, so it must be reachable here). Marking won stops at status + price and writes **no** `Purchase`: a parcel settles as a whole, once, when the seller invoices it. It does immediately sharpen the sale rollup, which costs `won`/`watching` lots at `finalPrice ?? currentBid`. Marking lost freezes `AuctionLot.fxRateToBase` the way `Purchase` does at `purchasedAt` (ADR-0009 §4, #20), and **only when a price was recorded**: a rate exists to convert a figure, so null covers base-currency, no-rate and no-result alike. Nothing is inferred into the price — the last observed bid is a lower bound, so the field opens blank and blank is a valid answer — and `cancelled` clears price and rate, as does reopening, since neither carries a datapoint. Once a result exists the row renders it in place of the bid: it is what the lot went for, and it is already what `allIn` is computed from. Composition is structured (`stamp × condition × certificate × format × quantity` — the shape a catalogue price is keyed on; the certificate arrived with #353, null = none as on a copy, because a house lot sold with a Fotoattest is worth a different figure and matching is strict) because free text makes catalogue value uncomputable and a lost lot unusable, and it **reuses** the unknown-variant rollup (#238) and format pricing (ADR-0020) rather than re-deciding them — literally, through the exported `valuateItemRows` (`items.ts`), which values a `stamp × condition × certificate × format` row of which a copy is one instance and a lot line another at a **null certificate** (a lot is described before it is owned). `auction-lines.ts` calls it **once per page of lots**, so the factor table and area tree load once. Catalogue value pivots **base currency → the sale's**, converted once per sale currency, because the bid, premium and ceiling are all denominated there; a missing rate makes a line **unconvertible**, which is not *unpriced* — it has a value that cannot be counted, and calling it unpriced would send the collector to enter a figure that already exists. A composition entered but unpriced is `null`, never `0.00`: a zero makes every headroom read as a catastrophic overbid. Unpriced/unconvertible lines are **counted beside the total**, never dropped. A **row's** headroom excludes shipping (premium only, exactly as `allIn` does) and the **sale's** includes it once. The price slot carries the shared quick-price dialog (#228/#341) over a `QuickPriceSubject`, which a lot line satisfies structurally. Composition has **two surfaces, deliberately**: on the **sale's own screen** each lot is a collapsible **card over its lines**, the purchase-order intake / offer-detail layout (#121/#165) — same `LotIssueGroupHeader`, same *Group by* / *Only* / *Sort* toolbar persisted through `lot-view-prefs`, and the watchlist row itself as the card header so a bid is still refreshed in place; on the **flat watchlist** the same list opens as a dialog off the row. The split follows the question each screen asks — "what do I bid on next", across sellers, versus "what am I actually paying for", within one parcel — and is the same one the offers list has with an offer's own screen. Only the sale detail carries the lines (`AuctionLotDetailItem`); the flat list would otherwise fetch forty compositions to draw forty collapsed rows, and its row only needs the total. Entering a line is **two modals**, the purchase intake's flow (#121): `StampPickerBrowser`, then `AuctionLotLineDialog` for condition / certificate / format / quantity, with the pick restated and a ← Back. Two sources, two questions — the catalogue tree answers the first, the listing text the second. The dialog **portals to `<body>`** (it is opened from inside other dialogs, whose transform-centred panels would otherwise be the containing block for its `position: fixed`), and pre-fills condition + certificate from `LS_LAST_CONDITION`/`LS_LAST_CERT` — the same remembered pair every add-copy entry point uses. Going through `StampPickerBrowser` directly (not `StampSelect`) is also what gives the purchase intake's **whole-issue** pick: a series expands — in the *action* layer, `resolveAuctionLineStamps` — into one line per member `requiredForCompleteness`, because a house lot is routinely "Mi 1–12 complete" and twelve trips through the picker is why it would go undescribed. The issue is an **entry shortcut only**; a stored line is always one stamp, since catalogue value sums per stamp and a lost lot must be attributable per stamp. Editing offers stamps alone — one line becoming twelve is not an edit. The remaining fields do not render until something is picked — a line *is* a stamp at a condition, and four greyed-out selects is a form pretending to be busy. **Add lot** carries the same composition section and `createAuctionLot` writes lot + lines in one nested create, every line validated first so a refusal leaves no half-entered lot. A lot card on the sale screen passes `showParties={false}`: a sale is one settlement with one seller, so both are fixed and the header states them. A lot with no `title` is **named after its composition** (`deriveAuctionLotLabel`, pure in `auction-rules.ts`): collapsed catalogue numbers **with their prefix** (`Mi·PL 1-12`) — through the listing engine's own `compactCatalogNumbers`, now exported, since two implementations of #150's collapsing would drift. The prefix rides *inside* the collapsing (it is just the numbering family's constant part), so it is written once around a span and a lot spanning two areas stays honest as `Mi·PL 1-3,Mi·DE 5`. Resolved server-side through the same pure `buildAreaVendorMaps` + `formatStampCN` the client renders chips with, over `readCollectionAreas` — `getCollectionAreas` minus the ownership check, split out rather than threading an `ownerId` into modules with no other use for one — then the issue when the lines share one, and a mixed lot reads as its size (`14 stamps · 3 issues`) because a name is scanned, not studied. **Derived on every read, never stored**, unlike an offer's title (#209/#365): a lot name is never published anywhere, and a lot is described line by line, so a stored value would freeze on the first stamp. It outranks `Lot {lotNo}` in `lotLabel` — the number is already its own chip and says only where the lot sits in someone else's catalogue. That precedence — title, then derived name, then `Lot {lotNo}` — is **one pure rule**, `auctionLotName` in `auction-rules.ts`, with each surface supplying only its own last resort (`Untitled lot` on a watchlist row, the sale's name on an action item). It was four copies until #556, and the copy that mattered was the one **settlement** did not have: `settleAuctionSale` wrote `title ?? derivedTitle` and nothing else, so a lot bid on sight-unseen — no title, no composition typed in, which is exactly what buying an unopened stockbook looks like — became a purchase line with no name at all, positionally numbered by the purchase screen and unattributable to the lot it was won as. The settle dialog had shown `Lot 42` for that lot all along; what it previewed and what it wrote disagreed. `AuctionLotLineItem.catalogNumbers` is ordered **primary vendor first** (`buildPrimaryVendorByAreaMap`, once per page) so the name picks one catalogue rather than three. A line row is **not** `InventoryItemRow`: a line is a stamp a lot is *described* as holding, so there is no copy number, location, disposition, delivery state or cost basis to show — it mirrors the copy row's four-line layout and adds quantity. `copy-sort.ts` was generalised to a structural `SortableCopy` + `sortSortableCopies(…, amountOf)` so both orderings are literally one comparison, the amount differing because a copy's is a base-currency catalog figure and a line's a sale-currency total. The arithmetic is pure in `src/lib/auction-lot.ts` (`allIn` = bid + percentage + fixed + shipping, `headroom`, `summarizeAuctionSale` over the *payable* — `watching`/`won` — lots): a ceiling set against the hammer price alone systematically overpays on cheap lots. **Settlement** (#28, `settleAuctionSale`) is a **1:1 transcription** into `Purchase`/`PurchaseLot`, the links unique and `SetNull` so deleting the purchase leaves the bidding record standing — which is also the undo, `assertLotEditable` freezing a settled lot because the purchase now holds what was actually paid. Seller/platform/currency/shipping come off the sale; each settled `won` lot is one line at `settlementLinePrice` = hammer + premium and **never** shipping, which becomes `Purchase.shippingCost` and is distributed across those very lines by ADR-0009 §3 — charging it per line would bill it twice. The composition is **written as copies, not proposed**: a line already is `stamp × condition × certificate × format × quantity`, so a confirm step would re-ask what was typed to decide the bid; a line of quantity N becomes N `Item`s (`ordered`, out of collection, cost pending, one `allocateItemNumbers` range for the parcel) and the existing intake runs unchanged. What *is* reviewed is the money — date, shipping, every line price and **which** won lots are in the parcel — because the seller's invoice is the authority, not our arithmetic; a won lot left out stays `won` and unsettled, since a lot shipped separately is a fact, not an error. Refused while any lot is still `watching` (the parcel's contents are not known) and with nothing selected (a sale where nothing was won is **closed**, not settled). The review dialog is built from the already-loaded `AuctionSaleDetail` — there is no settlement-preview endpoint, because a won lot's line price is the row's own all-in figure.

- The **learned realization ratio** (#520; ADR-0029 §2) is the catalogue side of a bid anchor: `ratio = perUnitMarketValue ÷ catalogueValue`, one per ADR-0022 datapoint, resolved through a five-rung ladder in the pure `src/lib/realization-ratio.ts` — area × condition × period, area × condition, area, collection, then `bidFallbackPercent` (#508) — taking the **median** of the most specific bucket holding at least `MIN_RATIO_SAMPLE = 3`. Condition drops out *before* area on purpose (the catalogue already prices condition; it does not carry a whole area running high or low), the period axis is a sliding `issuedYear ± 2` window centred on the stamp being anchored rather than a decade bucket, and split-derived ratios are **deduplicated by lot within each bucket** — ADR-0022 §3 gives every line of a mixed lot the same ratio by construction, so counting them separately would let one dealer lot dominate the median. Both constants are code, not settings. `src/lib/realization-ratios.ts` is the domain half: one read of the collection's closed, priced lots per page (nothing stored, nothing invalidated), catalogue values from `valuateItemRows` as everywhere else, and the bucket **named** (`Poland, MNH, 1948–1952`) because a learned ratio has to be arguable rather than magic.

- **What anchors an auction lot line** (#510; ADR-0029 §1, §5, §7) is `src/lib/auction-lot-anchors.ts`, the layer between the valuation modules and `bid-recommendation.ts`: market median for the line's exact key when it has any datapoint, else catalogue × the learned ratio, else nothing — resolved **per line, never per lot**, since one lot routinely mixes a well-recorded key with one never seen. It decides nothing the modules below it decided: the composition rollup, `readStampMarketValues` (the ownership-checked `getStampMarketValues` split the way `readCollectionAreas` is) and #520's ladder are reused whole. Catalogue anchors need no conversion (they already roll up in the sale's currency and a ratio is unitless); market medians are converted base → sale at the **current** rate, and a line that cannot be converted is *unconvertible*, never unanchored. Copies already held come per `stamp × condition` from `countCopiesByStampAndCondition` and are **shown, never computed with** — a duplicate haircut would under-bid exactly the material bought deliberately for trade.

- The **bid recommendation on the lots screen** (#511; ADR-0029 §3, §5, §8) is where the four modules above meet, and the split is the composition's own (#353): the **three figures ride on the lot read** — `AuctionLotListItem.recommendation`, filled by `recommendationsFor` over the compositions `compositionsFor` already valued, which is why `resolveAuctionLotAnchors` takes a `valued` map rather than valuing a page twice — while the **evidence is fetched per lot only while its popover is open** (`auctions/lots/[lotId]/recommendation`, `getAuctionLotBidEvidence` in `src/lib/bid-recommendations.ts`, the domain half of #509's pure module). The figures have to be on the row because `REC` is a quick fill and must know what it would write before it is clicked; the evidence must not be, or a forty-lot watchlist fetches forty lots' market medians to draw forty collapsed rows. `recommendationsFor` is **best-effort** like `attachBaseRates`: a recommendation is an extra column on a screen whose other figures are the record, so a failed market read leaves the rows without one rather than failing the list. On the row `fair` is a **column of the grid's *worth* section** (track 10, beside `Catalogue`, against the same `value` / `headroom` labels — which is what the grid's own note already said anything answering "what is this worth" should be), and its `headroom` cell is the catalogue column's very subtraction against the other value, `headroom(fair, finalPrice ?? currentBid, fees)`. That **cell is the popover's trigger**, exactly as the catalogue cell is the way in to the composition editor: a disclosure revealed on hover is one most collectors never find, and the evidence is the whole argument for trusting the figure. `REC` is a *third* quick fill stacked under `CAT` in the ceiling gutter and never replaces it — "what the catalogue says" and "what it is worth bidding" are different statements. `bid-recommendation-popover.tsx` is portalled and positioned exactly as `RowActionsMenu` is and for the same two reasons (clipped list overflow, and an ended row's `opacity: 0.6` trapping a fixed panel); it opens on **any described lot, including one where nothing could be anchored**, which is precisely the lot whose empty cell needs explaining. **All three levels are takeable and each is taken where its evidence is**: every level in the panel is a button writing itself into the ceiling (a `subgrid` row, hence no `Tooltip` on it — the wrapper span would become the grid item), and all three are in the `⋮` menu; the row's own `REC` stays on `fair` alone, so a scanned row carries one answer. ADR-0029 §8 originally refused the other two outright and was **revised, not quietly contradicted**: the "not in a row that is scanned" argument holds for the row and is why `REC` is still fair-only, but it was wrong to conclude the judgement should be unavailable. The trigger's caret hangs in a **reserved slot past the column's right edge** (#576, `RECOMMENDATION_CARET_SLOT`), never in flow after the figure: the amounts are right-aligned, so a caret drawn in flow pushes its own row's digits left and only on the rows that have one — the figure then lined up with neither the row above, the `headroom` line under it, nor its own base-currency reading. Every cell of that column pads for the slot (heading included) and track 10 carries the extra width, so the track is clear whether or not a given row draws anything in it. The panel also carries one row per line naming its anchor (market median with `n`/span/ADR-0022 badge, or the ratio with the bucket it was learned from and its `n`), the ownership counts as evidence only, and the unanchored/unconvertible tallies so a partial total never reads as complete.

- **What the watchlist can cost** (#523) is two figures, not one, and the distinction is the whole point: `AuctionSaleSummary.committedTotal` costs each open lot at the **proxy maximum placed** (`myBid`) — a commitment made to the platform, so it is what a budget is checked against — and `ceilingTotal` costs it at `max(maxBid, allIn(myBid))`, what carrying the bidding to the collector's own ceiling would come to. A ceiling is **already an all-in figure** (ADR-0021 §6, which is why `maxBidWithin` exists), so nothing ever runs `allIn()` over `maxBid` — that charges the premium and the shipping twice, and the issue's own wording asked for exactly that. `currentBid` is an observation and never enters either total **as a figure** — but it decides *membership*, which is #600: an open lot whose price has run past the ceiling is dropped from both (`isOutpriced`, counted as `outpricedCount`), because it can no longer be won at the ceiling recorded on it and a budget total that keeps charging for it overstates every parcel with an old ceiling in it. Two conditions, and the figure is wrong without either. The comparison is **all-in against the ceiling**, literally the one `lotHasSignal` makes for `over-ceiling` — the issue asked for the bare `currentBid > maxBid`, which would go on counting a lot for as long as the premium alone is what carries it over, and would put the total and the chip on the row into open disagreement. And the lot only drops out when the collector is **not leading**: a bid placed above the ceiling still wins at what was placed, which is the `myBidOverCeiling` case `ceilingTotal`'s `max` exists to keep honest, so only a price past *both* the ceiling and the bid means nothing can happen without a new decision. It is a **total the lot leaves, never the list** — the screens name the count beside the figures, and in the **muted** tone rather than the warning one used for `unbidCount`/`uncappedCount`: those are gaps that make a figure read low, while this lot is correctly costed at nothing. On the lots list the figure is `auctionLotExposure` over the same `lotListWhere` the list and the counts use (#151), rolled up **per sale** before it is summed — the premium and the shipping are the sale's, shipping lands once per parcel however many of its lots the filter shows — and converted into the base currency per sale, a watchlist spanning platforms having no single currency to sum in.

- **Auction sales** get their own collection nav page, and the **flat list of lots is the primary screen** (#351) — status / platform / seller filter chips (#332), sale as a column with optional grouping, inline bid editing — so "everything I have running on Allegro" needs no navigating between sales. Sale detail exists separately, for settlement and shipping. This follows from the sale being a *settlement boundary*: it matters twice (when a lot is added and when the parcel is paid for) and is in the way the rest of the time, whereas scanning closing times and refreshing bids is what happens daily. Consequently adding a lot never starts by picking a sale (#352): the dialog takes seller and platform and **proposes** the open sale for that pair when one exists, with the option to start a new one — the proposal is re-read server-side rather than trusted from the client, and refusing it (`startNewSale`) is what a second parcel from a seller who has already shipped needs. The screens live under `auctions/` (`/auctions` the lots, `/auctions/sales` the settlement list, `/auctions/sales/[saleId]` the detail), the vocabulary + staleness + parsing in `auction-rules.ts`, the reads/writes in `auctions.ts`. Four decisions the code depends on. **Staleness is relative**: a reading may be a quarter of the time that was left when it was taken old before it counts stale, capped at a day — one ratio instead of a threshold table, tightening exactly as the close approaches; a live lot past `endsAt` reports `closed` instead, because that is the only signal that never fixes itself. The toolbar filters on **two different questions**, and the split is deliberate: *signals* (`LotSignal` in `auction-lot.ts` — can-still-bid, outbid, leading, over-ceiling, won-pending) are **derived** from the figures and answer "what do I do about this now", while *statuses* are **recorded** and answer "how did this end". A watchlist is worked through by the first and filed by the second. Signals describe `watching` lots only. They span two tables plus arithmetic, so no `where` expresses them: `resolveSignals` walks the live lots once in memory and hands ids to the page query — the offers list's needs-action pattern (ADR-0013 §4), and sound for the same reason, a watchlist being bounded by what one person is currently bidding on. **Colour means "act now"**, and nothing else: an ended lot is *muted* (row opacity included), never red — there is nothing to react to, and an alarm on it would compete daily with the lots that can still be won. That is why the `Ended` / `Closing today` / `This week` window filter exists (server-side on `endsAt`, the boundary read from the server's clock so two tabs and the counts cannot disagree; deliberately **not** remembered between visits, unlike the other filters, since a list silently narrowed to old lots would hide everything running). Past the close, `BidStandingChip` stops reading as a position and reads as a **result** — *Won?* / *Lost?*, with the question mark, because it is inferred from the last bid anyone recorded rather than from the platform; a lot whose outcome *is* recorded renders no standing chip at all, its status chip saying it without the doubt. The closing time is tinted by `closingUrgency` — **absolute** thresholds (past / ≤2 h / ≤24 h), deliberately unlike the relative staleness rule beside it: staleness asks "is this reading still good?", which depends on the time left, while urgency asks "must I deal with it now?", and an hour is an hour. Its wording follows the *clock*, not the status: a `watching` lot an hour past its close reads "closed", because it plainly does not still close in the future. A recorded bid **always** stamps `checkedAt` — including an unchanged one via *Bid unchanged*, since "still at 40" is the observation being asked for — while editing anything else on the lot leaves it alone, or a three-day-old reading would look current after a title fix. The last platform is remembered **per seller and in the database** (`Contact.defaultAuctionPlatformId`, a self-FK, `SetNull`), not as one value and not in `localStorage`: where you bid follows from who you buy from, so a single "last platform" would be wrong for every seller but the one just used, and the memory has to survive a change of device. It is deliberately **not** the offers screen's remembered platform — an auction platform is not a selling platform. It is *remembered, not configured*: nothing in the contact form writes it, `rememberSellerPlatform` does, from `createAuctionLot`/`createAuctionSale` — server-side, because a seller and a platform typed in by name only become contacts once the server has resolved them, which is where both ids first exist. Its `updateMany` guard needs the explicit **null branch** (`OR: [{ id: null }, { not: id }]`): `<> 'x'` is NULL for a NULL column, so a seller who has never been tracked matches no inequality. Pre-filling it remounts the picker (`key`), because `PurchaseContactSelect` seeds its own state on mount, it is adjusted **during render** rather than in an effect (the value arrives with a query), and a platform the collector has touched is never overwritten. The `sales/open` endpoint therefore takes `platformId` as **optional** — the pre-fill must be answerable before a platform exists, or it would deadlock the very field it feeds. A lot carries **three** money fields that are constantly confused and must not be merged: `currentBid` is an observation of the auction (what it stands at, whoever is winning), `myBid` is a commitment the collector made to the platform (a proxy/maximum bid), and `maxBid` is a private valuation (the ceiling). Only the first stamps `checkedAt` — it is the only one that dates an observation. **Leading / outbid is derived** (`bidStanding`, `myBid >= currentBid`, a tie going to the bid already placed), never stored: a flag would be a fourth thing to keep current and would be wrong the moment the price moved. *Bid my ceiling* uses `maxBidWithin` — the **inverse of `allIn`**, `(ceiling − fixed − shipping) / (1 + pct/100)`, rounded **down** — because a ceiling is an all-in figure while a bid box takes a hammer price, so bidding the ceiling itself overshoots by exactly the fees. It is one of **three quick fills** (#370/#371) that place a figure the row already knows into a field beside it: catalogue value → ceiling, catalogue value → *Mine*, ceiling → *Mine*. Anything landing in a **bid** goes through `maxBidWithin` (`catalogBidRoom` is the catalogue one, computed server-side beside `bidRoom`), because catalogue value is an all-in figure too — it is what `headroom` subtracts `allIn` from; only the **ceiling** takes its source unchanged, being itself an all-in valuation. None of them writes `currentBid`: that is an observation, and filling it with a figure nobody saw would stamp `checkedAt` with a reading that never happened and make `bidStanding` say the collector is leading against their own arithmetic. Each is offered **twice** — a `⋮` entry and a hover control — built from one `QuickFill` descriptor (`value === null` *is* unavailable, `hint` says why), so the two surfaces cannot disagree. The control is **the source column's heading, abbreviated** (`CEIL`, `CAT`), set in the grid's own heading style: it names the *source*, never the target, so the *Mine* column can offer two of them unexplained. Arrows were tried first and cannot do this — an arrow shows direction, and all three of these move a figure leftward into a neighbouring column, so up and down were left naming a source they had no way to name. They live in **gutter tracks of their own** (`quickFillSlot`, explicitly placed like `GRID_RULE` so everything else keeps auto-flowing) and **centred across both rows** of the column they write: a quick fill sets the whole column, and putting each control in the cell it happens to write scattered them over two different rows for no visible reason. A column offering two **stacks** them — side by side they read as one two-word label on the figure beside them. The gutter goes **after** its column, not before: amounts are right-aligned, so the empty part of a track is on its left, and a gutter placed ahead of a column ends up one gap from the *previous* column's figure and several from the one it fills. Track width is fixed unhovered, or revealing a control would shunt a figure out of its column. A quick fill goes through the same pending override an inline edit does. A **row click** opens the lot's sale with `?lot=<id>` (#374). The sale screen scrolls that card into view and flashes it once with `just-added-flash`, but the flash is only the *arrival*: the mark itself is an accent **ring** (a `box-shadow`, so the card does not resize) plus a labelled strip with a ✕, and it **persists** — a flash is over before the eye has finished reading the parcel's other lots, leaving the collector to find the row again. The URL is the whole state; clearing deletes that one param and `replace`s, so undoing a highlight is not a step to walk back through. Clicks originating in `a, button, input, select, textarea, [role="button"]` are left alone — the click-to-edit cells present as the last of those — as is a click that ends a text selection, and a modified click opens a tab. It is off (`linkToSale`) on the sale's own screen, where the click would land on the page already being read. `AuctionLot.startingPrice` is a **record, never a figure**: it is what the lot opened at, so it is displayed muted in the bid slot while no bid exists (`from 20 PLN`) and enters no arithmetic at all — a lot nobody has bid on costs nothing whatever it opens at. The row renders the three as a **3 × 2 grid** (Auction / Mine / Ceiling × bid / all-in): each figure exists twice, and columns are what make them comparable. The catalogue figures sit beside them **as a second section, behind a rule and under their own row labels** (`value` / `headroom`), because the worth side does not share the cost side's vocabulary: `all-in` there names a figure recomputed with fees, while the worth side's second line is a *subtraction* between the two. Labelling both `all-in` promised an operation the last column does not perform. Anything else answering "what is this worth" — a recommended price — is another column in that section, never a fifth cost column. Exactly one cell of each pair is **stored** — the two bids are hammer prices, a ceiling is an all-in valuation — and the other is computed from it and shown muted, which is why the ceiling's *bid* cell is `bidRoom`, the very figure *Bid my ceiling* places. But the collector's own two columns, *Mine* and *Ceiling*, are **edited from either side**: the two cells are one fact stated two ways, and which one happens to be stored is an implementation detail. A figure typed into the muted half is converted back and the stored half is what is written, through **two helpers that exist only because rounding direction is the whole question**: `bidCosting` for an all-in typed into *Mine* (the inverse rounded to the **nearest** cent, so the total reads back as the one that was typed; a target the fees alone swallow clears the column rather than inventing a bid) and `ceilingAllowing` for a bid typed into *Ceiling* (`allIn` rounded **up**, the smallest ceiling that still lets that bid be placed, with an epsilon so a whole-cent cost is not pushed to the next one). Neither is `maxBidWithin`/`allIn`: those answer a *cap* question and round down, which is right for `bidRoom` and *Bid my ceiling* and wrong here — on a 20% + 1.50 house a stated all-in of 200 came back as 199.99, which reads as arithmetic going wrong rather than as a bid being chosen. Nothing in the two-sided conversion is a limit, so nearest/up is safe: a placed bid is a *record* of a commitment, and whether it outruns what the lot is worth is what the ceiling and `myBidOverCeiling` are for. **Auction stays one-way**: its bid is an observation, and there is nothing to state twice about a price someone else set. This is why the row carries `premiumPercent`/`premiumFixed`: the derived half is computed in the row from the figure **as displayed**, through the pure module, so one pending override covers both cells of a column instead of the two disagreeing while a refetch is in flight. The two bid cells are tinted **together** by `standing` (green/red), because they are one comparison; the collector's own cells go **amber** when `myBidOverCeiling` — their commitment costing more than their ceiling is a different fact from the market outrunning them, and the only one they can take back. A row's **all-in excludes shipping** (premium only): shipping belongs to the parcel and the sale's total adds it once, so charging it per row would double it the moment two lots are open with one seller. And the list's **reading order** is decided by the status chip — `watching` soonest-closing first, a terminal status most-recent first, no chip at all newest-tracked first, because the two orders contradict each other in a mixed list.

- The lots list **hides closed lots by default** (#504) — won, lost, observed and cancelled alike, which is `outcomeWhere("pending")` applied in `lotListWhere` whenever no explicit `outcome` is chosen — on #245's rule for sold offers: a watchlist is what is still to be decided, and the archive grows without bound. An explicit outcome chip wins, and a remembered `includeClosed` flag (`Show closed`, the counterpart of `Show sold/withdrawn`) is the escape hatch the single-select chips otherwise leave no room for. A **sale's own** lots (`saleId`) are exempt: that screen is one parcel's settlement, where a won lot is the point. With the default no longer a mixed list, `lotOrderBy` reads it soonest-closing first.

- An **auction amount is converted with a rate, not on the server** (#498, `auction-base-amount.tsx` + `formatBase`). `AuctionLotListItem` / `AuctionSaleListItem` carry `baseCurrency` + `baseRate` and the screens do the arithmetic, because half the figures on a lot row are computed *there* from the seller's fees as they are typed (a bid's all-in cost, the bid a ceiling allows) — a server-converted field would describe the row as it was fetched. The rate is a lot's **frozen** `fxRateToBase` where it has one (a recorded result is a dated observation, ADR-0009 §4) and today's otherwise, filled by `attachBaseRates` in one batch per currency, best-effort like `attachBasePrices` (#208). Where it renders is the split #498 settled: the flat lots list and the sales list convert the **headline figure only** (the bid; the parcel's all-in total) since a list is scanned down a column, and the sale's own screen — cards and totals card alike — converts **everything**, via `AuctionLotRow`'s `baseAmounts="full"`. On the figures the collector **declares** — *Mine* and *Ceiling*, each from either of its two sides — the converted line is also **editable** (`AmountWithBase`'s `onSaveBase`, inverse `fromBase`): what leaves the account is the collector's own money, so a limit reasoned in PLN should not need dividing by a rate in the head. The stored value stays the sale's currency, exactly as the bid ↔ all-in pair stores the hammer price whichever side was typed. The *auction's* bid is deliberately not editable this way — it is an observation transcribed from the listing, in the listing's currency.
