# Purchases, Intake & Storage of Copies

Scan-sheet ingest, delivery and disposal state, sorting, storing, ref cards, and order ROI.

- **Scan sheet ingest** (#566; ADR-0033) inverts the intake pass: a whole stockbook card is scanned, the scan is cut into per-stamp **tiles**, and identification happens from images afterwards (#567) instead of alternating with photography stamp by stamp. Three decisions carry it. **A tile is its own entity, and `Item` is not it**: `Item.stampId` is NOT NULL and stays that way, because every read in the app assumes a copy points at a stamp and a nullable column would need each of them to grow its own exclusion. Lot closing decides it — the pool is split across copies by primary-catalog price, before the parcel arrives (ADR-0009 §3/§5), so a stamp-less stub has no price and therefore no weight in that split; as a separate entity it is simply not in the split at all, and the header warns *N tiles unidentified* the way the existing `N to sort` does, a **warning and never a block** (the arithmetic is fine without them; it is the collector's memory that needs the nudge). **The cut happens on the original, before the photo pipeline** — `processImage` caps every upload at `FULL_MAX_EDGE`, so a sheet pushed through it first turns a card of forty into ~600 px tiles, while cutting first gives each ~800 px crop (600 dpi card) its own `full` and `thumb` as if it had been photographed alone. That failure is silent: the tiles look fine, merely soft, and by then the stockbook has been broken up — hence the ADR and the reasoning parked in `photos/sheet.ts`, the one place that could get it backwards. **The sheet is not a `Photo` and the tile's images are**: a `Photo` is two derivatives of something with the upload discarded, whereas a sheet's bytes *are* the point (never downscaled, needs an `original` variant the closed `PhotoVariant` union has no room for, and would have to be excluded from every existing photo reader), so `ScanSheet` is its own table with its own key, its own `original`/`view` variants and its own serving route — while the crops are ordinary `Photo` rows under a **fourth owner**, `tileId`, the `itemId` → `stampId` (#137) → `offerId` (#311) path walked a third time, which is what makes #567's "the images move onto the copy" a reassignment of one column. Retention is what makes a bad cut recoverable (a stockbook cannot be re-scanned once broken up), so retained originals are counted in the collection's storage total — they are the largest objects the app stores — and their bytes are removed on **purchase** delete (a lot delete leaves them standing since #586, see below) with addresses read **before** the row delete and files removed **after** it, since that delete can be refused. **Pairing is by position, not index**: each stamp is turned over *in place*, so a back goes to the front whose centre is nearest, mutual-nearest, compared in fractional sheet coordinates, with **no mirroring** (mirroring is right when a whole group is turned at once and would break exactly what turning in place guarantees) and **no distance cap** (mutuality is the guard; a cap is one more constant to be wrong about). A differing count is a **signal, not a failure to hide** — *front 12, back 11*, naming which fronts found no back — and an unmatched back becomes a **back-only tile** dragged onto a front, the sparse case handled with the same entity rather than a third mode. Since #647 that count is also the **precondition**, not only a report: mutuality was expected to carry a sparse back sheet and does not — with a subset on the second card the missing stamps' neighbours become each other's nearest, mutually, and most backs land one square off — so unequal counts pair **nothing** and hand the whole card to the manual path. Counts are a weak signal for *the same card turned over*, chosen because it is the one that is cheap, exact and impossible to be subtly wrong about: being wrongly turned away costs a drag per stamp, while being wrongly admitted costs a mis-paired card, and a wrong back is silent afterwards where an absent one is on the strip. The mode rides out in `CutReport.pairingMode`, because *no back found for tile 3, 5, 9* would be describing a search that never ran. **A back can also be taken off the tile it is on** (#648, `unpairTileBack`): it becomes a back-only tile appended to the batch — the same square an unmatched back arrives as, placed by the same drag — so a mis-pairing costs one press instead of deleting the batch and with it every tile identified beside it. It is the inverse of `pairTilesManually` in shape as well as in effect: one `Photo` row changes owner, the box columns move with it, and the batch's `batchDoneAt` is cleared because a card with a back waiting to be placed is not finished with (#578 sweeps the ones that are). Refused on a settled tile for the reason the drop target is — a consumed tile's images belong to its copy, and a discarded one is one press from being back in the queue. **The review editor is the primitive, not a fallback**: draw / move / resize / delete / split / merge over the scan itself, in rectangles because a crop is one (not because stamps are — triangles and diamonds are not), with nothing created until commit, so the whole review is free to be wrong. Coordinates are **original-sheet pixels** throughout, scaled for display by one measured factor. Geometry lives in one pure module (`src/lib/scan-boxes.ts`) that neither half owns and **nothing below it asks how a box was made** — reading order groups rows by top edge within a tolerance from the **median** height, never the maximum, which would swallow two rows of definitives beside one block. Re-cutting is refused once a tile is `consumed` (#567 hands a copy the tile's very `Photo` rows), and the client reads the previous boxes off the tiles *before* the action runs so the editor reopens on the old cut rather than an empty canvas. **Automatic detection is #574**, and it kept that promise: one module (`src/lib/scan-detect.ts`), one call (`proposeCut` → `proposeCutAction`), `Box[]` into the same editor, nothing downstream changed. It **proposes and never decides** — `proposeCutAction` answers with *no boxes* rather than an error, so a scan it cannot read opens on the empty canvas #566 shipped; drawing by hand is the primitive, not a fallback. The method is a port of a Python/SciPy implementation (~1,450 photos): background estimated **per image from the border ring**, several dark clusters kept on purpose (comparing against the darkest alone once ballooned a mask to 96% of a frame), threshold on **L∞ distance from the nearest cluster and never on brightness** — a global greyscale threshold, `sharp.threshold()` included, split single stamps' own artwork into 13–24 "stamps" — then close 3×3, fill holes, open 5×5, fill holes, erode with an **L1 diamond** (`r` passes of the 4-neighbour minimum; a separable square is not the same operation), label 4-connected, grow each bounding box back by the erosion radius, drop by minimum area and by containment. **No fill-ratio** (it throws away triangles and diamonds), **no aspect-ratio** (a strip of five is one piece), **no maximum area**, and **no filter for reference slips** — a surviving slip costs one click while a stamp wrongly dropped is absent from a card already broken up with nothing saying so. Four constants were refitted for a card of dozens rather than a photo of 1–8 stamps (working resolution 900 → 2600 px, erosion radius now tied to it, minimum area stated in **mm² of card** from the scan's own density, reading-order tolerance from the median). **A fifth had to move for a reason the issue did not foresee, and it is the one to remember**: the threshold floor 28 → 52, because a **1200 dpi flatbed resolves the stockbook card itself** — the ridges its rows are creased along, its weave, its fibres — which a phone photograph of a few stamps did not. At 28 a crease under a row of stamps was foreground, joined their bottom edges, closed the row into a ring, and the hole fill then took the gaps *between* the stamps as interior: a row of six came out as one box, and no erosion undid it (eroding hard enough to break the crease broke single stamps first). The corpus differed from a stockbook card in its **subject and its instrument**, and only the first was in the spec. The reference's whole-frame escape (`mean(mask) > 0.90` → one box) is deliberately **not ported**: a card always shows a black margin, a dense card can approach that coverage, and the failure would be the entire card as one tile. Measured on **eight real 1200 dpi cards, 120 pieces: 2 need a hand — 1.7%** (reference ~1.6%), both being the documented limits (one interlocked pair merged, one stamp-plus-coupon split). The regression set lives in a **gitignored** `tests/fixtures/scans/` with a committed `scan-expectations.json` beside it, and `tests/unit/scan-detect.test.ts` **skips entirely** when the folder is absent so CI stays green without binaries. **Re-run it after every parameter change**, including ones that look unrelated — in the reference a change to the background estimator silently altered 558 of 1,429 photos. A count is a **lower bound** (cancelling errors read as zero), so placement was verified by rendering the boxes over each card and looking at them; the set's gaps — touching stamps, uneven lighting, a non-rectangular stamp, a front/back pair — are recorded in `_gaps` and printed by every run, because a gap unmentioned reads as coverage. Two limits are physical and no tuning crosses them: what is **joined stays one region** (a se-tenant pair, a block, a strip is one tile and, once identified, one copy with a format — never several singles), and **interlocking perforations are an information limit**, white paper against white paper, so the card must be laid out with about **one perforation tooth of gap** between stamps. That rule is in `docs/user-guide/purchases.md` because it is the difference between a clean pass and hand-drawing boxes. Identification is **#567**, and the measure of decisions 1 and 2 having been right is that it needed **two columns and no new table**: a tile reaches one of three ends and in all three the images move by one `UPDATE` — `scan_tile.itemId` (`SET NULL`) says what a `consumed` tile became, and `scan_sheet.batchDoneAt` says when the **last** tile of a batch left `unidentified`, which is when its retained original stopped being able to do anything (**#578**'s retention sweep is what reads it, and it is **cleared** when a discard is put back or a batch is re-cut so the sweep never counts down on a batch still being worked). That sweep (`purgeFinishedScanSheets`) takes the **bytes and leaves the row**: the sheet is marked `purgedAt` with its `sizeBytes` zeroed — so the storage total moves without any reader learning about the flag — while a re-cut refuses with *the scan has been deleted* and the batch still lists what the card held, rather than a missing file surfacing as a read error, which is the worst way for a collector to find out. The refusal earns its place on the **all-discarded** batch, the one case a consumed tile is not already refusing for. It is **off by default** and a per-collection setting, because a scan is a source and not output). The three ends are a **new copy** (`intakeStamps` entered from a tile rather than from a stamp picker, so the arrived-order rule, the copy number, the format and the dispositions stay that function's and are not reimplemented), an **existing copy on the order** (the auction path: settlement created identified copies that want *photographs, not identification*, and settlement is deliberately **not** changed to produce tiles — a tile can be counted against no want (ADR-0032), carries no catalogue price so closing before arrival would be blocked, and the order's catalogue-value bar would read zero until delivery), and a **discard that leaves a trace** (keeps its image, leaves the unidentified count so it stops nagging, survives the close — for a stockbook bought sight-unseen the discarded tiles are the only record of what was inside, so *evidence, not a queue item*). Assign and discard are allowed on a **closed** lot where creating a copy is not: closing froze the money (ADR-0009 §3) and a photograph is not money. The tile intake dialog drops its **photo uploader**, because front and back are singleton slots per copy and an upload beside the tile's crop would be a second front. The tile dialog **opens on an outcome, never on a menu of them**, with the other two in the footer one click from wherever it opened — a chooser in front of them would be a screen whose whole content is three buttons, and a card of forty would mean forty of them showing nothing. Which outcome it opens on is **derived from whether there is anything *this tile* can be assigned to**, never from `fromAuction`, which survives only to word the sentence about described lines. **The list asks exactly what the write asks**: front/back are singleton roles per copy, so an assignment works only if the copy holds *none of the roles the tile carries* — front-only onto a copy with no front, back-only (the unpaired-back case) onto one with no back, paired onto one with neither. That rule lives in the pure `src/lib/tile-photo-roles.ts`, read by both `assignTileToCopy`'s refusal and the query fragment (`freePhotoSlotsWhere` + the `freePhotoSlots` option, deliberately **not** a `LotCopyFilter` — that union is a chip vocabulary and also types `LotBulkSelector.filter`, where a role-parameterised filter is meaningless). They drifted once: the list asked the weaker *"has any free slot"*, offered a front-only tile copies that merely lacked a back, and the write refused them — **a list that offers what the write refuses is the defect**. Not `no-photos` either, which would hide the back-only copy a front tile completes. The list being empty more often, and the mode landing on *identify* more often, is the correct answer for a tile that can go onto nothing rather than a reason to loosen it. The query is keyed by **lot**, so one fetch per card, and the dialog **settles once and never jumps**: the first tile waits for the answer rather than opening on identify and switching under the hand of someone already reading it (the mode is latched, not re-derived, since a refetch could answer differently).  It still earns its place, because it is the only surface that shows **both sides large enough to judge a bad crop**, which the intake step never does. The **strip is a map of the card** — position *n* on screen is position *n* on the stockbook, which is how a tile is matched to the piece in the tweezers — so a worked tile never disappears or renumbers (narrowing belongs to the chip and the per-batch *N waiting*, not the layout) and a **consumed tile draws its copy's front**, which is the tile's own `Photo` row under its new owner: an empty placeholder there made the tile that went well look more broken than the one that became nothing, the states read backwards. The single honest placeholder is a consumed tile whose **copy was later deleted** (`itemId` is `SET NULL`), which says so in words. **What tells the three states apart is a mark, not a shade** (#582): a settled tile used to be the *same* cell at `opacity: 0.6`, and on a strip of forty that is not a difference the eye finds — the scans vary in brightness far more than the fade does, so a pale stamp on a dark card already read as handled. The emphasis is therefore **inverted onto the waiting tiles**, which take the accent edge this app uses for *this needs you*, while a settled one drops to the plain border and lets only its *picture* recede (the badge and the label never dim — a mark that faded with the scan would be the same fade wearing a shape). The two ends are **different kinds of mark and not two strengths of one**, because one piece became a copy and the other deliberately became nothing: a filled accent `check` against an outlined neutral `excluded`, filled-versus-outlined being what still separates them at 15 px where two hues of one badge would not. The device is `photo-thumb.tsx`'s reserved-slot marker — an opaque corner badge with a hairline ring rather than a coloured frame — chosen for exactly the property the fade lacked, saying the same thing over a black card margin as over a bright stamp; the copy number stays in the footer as the consumed tile's own anchor, the badge being what is legible at a glance and the number what is legible on inspection. The crop is **inset from the frame** for the same reason the frame is coloured at all: a tile is a stamp on a black card, its own edges are dark, and an edge drawn flush against them merges into the picture. **A finished batch is set aside, and collapses to its header line when revealed** (#583) — and is never moved: batch order is card order and the pile on the desk is in that order too, so reordering the unfinished ones to the top would break the one correspondence the numbering exists for — collapsing answers the clutter without touching it. Collapsing alone was **not enough at the size the feature is for**: a carton is fifty cards, and fifty header lines is still a wall to read past, so the worked batches are hidden behind a **count of them** in the card's own body (`SetAsideToggle`) and come back **in place** when asked for, never gathered at the end. What the original *collapse, never hide* rule was protecting is that the record stays **reachable**, not that it stays on screen — a strip is the record of a card that came into the house and a discard is the only record of what a sight-unseen stockbook held — so *put away, never thrown away*, with the door always in the same spot. The door is on the **section**, not on the batches, for the reason the Assistant's reveal control is in the toolbar row rather than a section heading: with every worked batch hidden there is no batch header left to hang it on, and a control that vanishes with what it reveals takes the only way back with it. It is worded as a **count of what is there** (*12 worked-through batches*) rather than as a filter that is on, since a collector who has never pressed it would otherwise be reading a switch with no idea what is behind it; and it is suppressed while the *only unidentified* chip is pressed, that chip already hiding the same batches for its own stated reason — one question with two controls that can disagree is worse than the wall. The rule **reads `batchDoneAt`** and recomputes nothing beside it, and it is **derived rather than remembered** — which batches are worth looking at is a fact about the work, not a per-collector setting — so a batch with tiles still waiting opens by itself, the lot card's own idiom (#382). Neither shared expansion hook fits and the local one says why: `useCardExpansion` is collapsed-baseline plus *a card that appeared while the screen is open*, `useGroupExpansion` carries one mode for the whole list, and this is a **per-item default derived from data**. Its overrides are **explicit choices, not a XOR against the default**: a collector who opens a finished batch and then puts one of its discards back has said *keep this open*, and a XOR would shut it as the derived default moved underneath. The batch's own buttons — Re-cut, Delete batch — fold away with its contents, a destructive click over a card nobody can currently see being the one thing a summary line must not offer. **A latch over a TanStack query must wait for `isFetching`, not merely `isPending`**: a closed dialog leaves an *inactive* query, and invalidating one only marks it stale — nothing refetches until an observer mounts again — so the reopened surface is handed the previous answer with `isPending` already false. #567's tile dialog pinned its opening mode from that stale answer, and copies added meanwhile stayed invisible until a full page reload. `isFetching` is safe to gate on because the observer applies `fetchState` to its *optimistic* result whenever it will fetch on mount, so it is already true on the first render rather than briefly false with stale data in hand. Waiting then costs a refetch per open at the screen's default `staleTime: 0`, which is why that query sets a short one — `isInvalidated` short-circuits ahead of `staleTime`, so every write on the screen still forces a re-ask. **A copy write is visible in three query namespaces and must invalidate every one it is visible in** — `lot-copies` (the lot's rows), `lot-scans` (the tile strip) and **`inventory`**, which is where the catalogue side keeps what it says about the stamp the copy points at: the copies-held badge and want marker on every picker row (#348/#532), #562's holdings line inside the intake step, and the stamp's own thumbnail, which a copy's first front photo may have just been auto-seeded into (#149 — and assigning a tile runs that seed too). Missing the third is how the stamp picker, opened for the *next* piece of the same parcel, showed a stamp with no photo and no copies held when both had just stopped being true. Being generous is cheap: a namespace nobody is watching is only *marked* stale, and the refetch happens when that surface is next opened. So the purchase screen's shared `run` invalidates all three, and a **tile outcome that touches a copy invalidates all three while one that does not — discard, un-discard, note — invalidates only the scans** — the scans query is always stale after a write, but `lot-copies` is a namespace of its own, so an assign (which hands a copy the tile's photos) and an identify (which creates one) invalidate it too, while a discard, an un-discard and a note touch no copy and would only be re-fetching a lot's whole copy list to learn nothing moved. `router.refresh()` is not a substitute: the copies stream from client queries (#172), so an assign without the second invalidation leaves the copy list showing no thumbnail and the assign list still offering the copy that just took the images, until the page is reloaded. **Discard acts immediately and asks for nothing** — the note is optional and written afterwards from the settled view — which is only safe because it is reversible (*Put back in the queue*), and is what keeps the frequent answer as cheap as it is frequent. A tile whose copy is on **none of the settled auction lot's lines** is surfaced — the parcel disagreeing with its description, which is information rather than a problem to hide — and it is **derived** from the lot's `AuctionLotLine` rows rather than flagged, so a corrected mis-identification corrects the signal, and a lot with no lines says nothing about any tile instead of calling them all undescribed. A **re-cut still destroys discarded tiles** (only `consumed` refuses it — re-cutting means the card is drawn again, discards included), so the confirmation names how many are about to go. **Zoom and pan are #579**, and are not comfort work: the review's likeliest error is a box clipping a perforation by a few pixels, which at fit-to-window on a whole card is invisible (#574's reference implementation says its own by-eye verification would have missed exactly that). Two calls keep it honest. **1:1 means one screen pixel per *sheet* pixel, never per *view* pixel** — the editor shows the `FULL_MAX_EDGE`-capped derivative, so the other reading would report `1:1` over a threefold downscale. And **past the view's own scale the visible region is served from the retained original** (`extractSheetRegion` → `renderSheetRegion`), because magnifying the derivative shows a larger blur rather than more stamp; below it the view still is what is shown, since a browser cannot be handed a 30 Mpx image. The region is a **sibling route** (`/scan-sheets/[sheetId]/region`), not a parameter on `[variant]`: that one hands back a stored object and takes the redirect fork a GCS binding needs, while a region is computed per request and has no URL to redirect to — folding it in would mean one variant value skipping the fork. Requests are **debounced and snapped to a grid** of sheet pixels, since each costs a full decode of the original server-side. The transform is a **view** transform and nothing more — boxes stay whole sheet pixels at every zoom — and its arithmetic lives in a third pure module (`src/lib/scan-viewport.ts`) that neither the client editor nor the server route owns, because the client decides which crop is on screen and the server validates it, and a number meaning different things across that line is a crop of somewhere else. Its one conversion back towards the sheet is **fractional by design** and always goes through `normalizeBox`, which rounds: rounding earlier would quantise the gesture (at 8×, one pixel of stamp per eight of mouse) without changing what is stored. **#585 is that module's second caller and the proof it was general**: the identification dialog's tile is a *picture and a viewport* like any other — a rectangle of the same card, whose size in scan pixels is the tile's own box — so it zooms, pans and reads `1:1` off the same functions, and two things were generalised **in place** rather than forked. `regionOnSheet` translates a viewport's own pixels into the sheet's, the two differing by the tile's corner and a URL built from the wrong one being a crop of somewhere else; and `regionRequest` now answers **null when the derivative is not a downscale at all** — a single stamp's tile is ~1400 px under a 2500 cap, so its photo *is* the scan of it and a region could only re-send those pixels at the price of a full decode of a 30 Mpx original (quietly true of a small card in the editor too, which is why it belongs at the decision rather than in each caller). The effects around it — debounce, preload-before-swap, drop-a-late-load — are shared as `use-sheet-region.ts` for the same reason the arithmetic is. Two product calls make it replace a loupe rather than merely enlarge: **flipping front to back keeps the zoom and the pan** (a comparison restarted at fit on every flip is an expensive comparison, and a scale in *scan* pixels is what makes "the same magnification" exact across two crops of one card), and **1:1 stays measured against the scan**. The **fallback is the point #578 made testable**: a swept sheet keeps its row, loses its bytes and is answered 404 deliberately, so the absence of a deep source is *known* rather than discovered by a failed fetch — `scan-tile-view.ts` decides which sides a tile has and which still have a card behind them, pure, so the swept path is a unit test rather than a screen reached by waiting out a cron job. A **`consumed` tile is in that same case on purpose**: its pictures are read through the copy that owns them now, that copy's front can have been replaced from the Copies screen, and nothing on the tile would say so — painting a sharp crop of the card over a photograph of something else is the one failure this escalation must not have. **The card belongs to the purchase, not to one of its lots** (#586), which corrects decision 1 of the ADR: `ScanSheet.purchaseId` / `ScanTile.purchaseId` replace their `lotId` and the batch sequence moves to `Purchase.nextScanBatchNo`. A lot fits a stockbook, which is one priced line, and breaks on the other ordinary case — twenty single stamps won at one auction settle into twenty lots on **one** purchase (ADR-0009), arrive as one parcel and are scanned on one or two cards. Per lot a tile could become a copy on no line but the one its sheet was uploaded under, so the card could not be worked through at all, and the **batch number stopped meaning anything**: *batch 1* existed twenty times over in one purchase while a card scanned "into lot 7" was invisible from the other nineteen. The deeper reason is not a workaround — at a settlement the collector **does not know which lot a stamp belongs to until they have identified it**, so asking at scanning time asks before the question can be answered, and moving up makes the *assign* list every copy on the **purchase** still needing photographs, which is exactly the set one matches a scanned stamp against when twenty lines arrived in one envelope. A copy still belongs to a lot, so **identification asks which**: silently on a purchase with one lot (the stockbook case must not grow a question it never had), otherwise put to the collector at the condition step beside every other intake answer and **remembered** — per *purchase*, `LS_LAST_SCAN_LOT`, because a lot id means nothing on the next parcel — since a card, or a run of them, is worked through before the next is started. *Assign* asks nothing, the copy having a lot already, and a named lot is checked against the tile's own purchase so a stale remembered answer is refused rather than filing a stamp against another order's money. A **default lot on the batch** was considered and **rejected**: a purchase of many small lots puts a dozen on one card, so a pointer from card to lot would be *false* rather than merely unhelpful. Ownership moving is a change to the **cascades**, not only to a column — deleting a lot no longer touches sheets or tiles (the parcel is still here, and so is every other line the card holds pieces of; a tile already consumed into a copy on that lot keeps its record and goes to `itemId: null` through #567's `SetNull`, the *copy deleted* square), while deleting the **purchase** takes them and their bytes. The migration **renumbers**, since numbers were unique per lot and two lots of one order could both hold a batch 1; sheets and tiles come off one map, a tile finding its sheets through `(purchase, batchNo)` and nothing else. The scans card moved with the data, from the lot card to the **order** level where the by-issue and flat copy views live, taking the *N tiles unidentified* chip and its filter with it; the lot close dialog still warns, now counting the order's tiles, because any of them could still become a copy on the lot being closed. **A batch also carries a name** (#587): an optional short `scan_sheet.label`, given at upload beside the button and — the half that matters — editable **afterwards**, since a card often turns out to need naming only once a parcel has been left half worked for a week and the strip of thumbnails is the only thing telling one from another. Drawn beside the number wherever a batch is named, including #583's collapsed line, which is the whole of a finished batch and so exactly the case it exists for. **The number stays primary** — assigned rather than chosen, and what makes a batch findable — so the name is a gloss and never a replacement: two cards both called *Polska* must stay tellable apart. Written to **both** sheets of a batch exactly as `batchDoneAt` is, so either side answers for it and re-scanning a named card's front cannot leave it nameless; a `scan_batch` table was rejected as a second place for a batch to exist. The ceiling and the trim/blank rule live in the pure `src/lib/scan-batch-label.ts` because both halves read them and a `server-only` module cannot be where a client component gets a constant from — blank stores as null, and an over-long name is **refused rather than truncated**, truncation mangling wording the collector chose. A blank field then takes **the uploaded file's own name** (#603, `batchLabelFromFileName`) — the naming already done at the scanner, which is what makes the field useful by default rather than one more thing to type — and the difference from the typed name is that a **derived** name is neither refused nor mangled: over the ceiling it yields **null** and the card is simply unnamed, since there is no wording to preserve and a scanner's long file name must not be what fails a 200 MB upload. It is read at the **upload** rather than prefilled into the input, because the button uploads the moment a file is chosen and there is no moment in between for a prefill to be seen or corrected — the correction is #587's rename, which is where a wrong name was always fixed. Only the last extension is stripped and nothing else is tidied: underscores and digits are the name as the collector saved it, and a name that came back different from the file on disk is not the same name. The placeholder states the fallback rather than saying *optional*, a blank field that now does something having to say what.

- **The scan reaches the app in pieces** (#590), which is the difference between the ingest working at 1200 dpi and not working at all: a card at that resolution is 100–200 MB, and Cloudflare's 100 MB body cap — nginx's 1 MB default is worse — refused it with a **413 before the app ever saw it**, so the collector met a failure that had nothing to say about stamps. The mechanism is in `storage-and-jobs.md` (open → chunks in order → finalize, the parts on local disk and never in the storage backend, one dial `STAMPORAMA_UPLOAD_CHUNK_KB`); what matters *here* is that it changed nothing about the ingest itself. Finalize hands `uploadSheet` the same scan the old single-request route did — as a path to the file the parts were joined into, so a 1200 dpi card is decoded without ever being resident whole — and so `prepareSheet`, the retained original, the `view` derivative the editor draws, the cut, the tiles and #578's sweep all meet exactly what they met before — the upload got a transport, not a redesign. Two things the flow gains and one it must keep. It gains a **measure**: the chunks the server has acknowledged, which exists only because the upload is in parts, drawn as the photo editor's own upload bar (`@/app/progress-bar`, lifted out of `photo-editor.tsx` for this) in its single-file case, since a card is one file and inventing a second treatment for it would be the divergence. And it gains a **second phase that is deliberately not a number**: after the last chunk the server assembles the parts and decodes a ~140 Mpx image to derive the `view`, seconds of work with nothing crossing the wire, so the bar stops claiming to measure and the label says *preparing* — a bar sitting at 100% reads as a hang at precisely the moment the upload has in fact succeeded, and the wait that follows it (`Finding the stamps…`, #574's detection pass) already had to be named for the same reason. What it must keep is that a **failure retries the chunk and not the file**: at 200 MB over a home connection, losing everything to one dropped request is the difference between a mechanism and a nuisance, which is why a chunk the server already holds is acknowledged rather than refused and a client's retry of a lost response resumes instead of starting again.

- **The piece being identified stays on screen for as long as the identification lasts** (#592), however many dialogs deep it goes — which is a rule about *evidence*, not a thumbnail added to one dialog. Identifying a tile walks through the tile dialog, the stamp picker, sometimes a create-issue and a create-stamp dialog opened from inside it, and finally the condition step; #585 put the picture on the first of those and nothing after it, so the collector answered *used or mint?* with nothing to answer it from, and did so at the **deepest point of the chain** — creating an issue and a stamp from inside the picker — where they are furthest from where they started. Condition is read off the piece (the cancel decides used against mint, the gum and the hinge marks are on the back, the centring is on the front), so the picture is the material for the field being filled in and its absence is not cosmetic. The mechanism is one slot, **`DialogShell.aside`**, and it is shared for the same reason the header and the footer are: four dialogs each hand-rolling a two-column body would be four layouts to keep in step, and two of them (`IssueDialog`, `StampFormDialog`) are shared across the app and would each grow a private notion of what sits beside their form. The slot is a bare `ReactNode` — the shell must not learn what a scan tile is — with `asideWidth` fixed and the dialog's own content flexing, which is what lets one caller put a whole browser beside it and another a 36 rem form. The content is always `IdentifiedPieceAside` → **#585's `TileZoomView`**, never a second, poorer image view: the flip keeps the zoom, `1:1` still means one screen pixel per *scan* pixel, and past the derivative's own resolution the visible region still comes from the retained card. What travels is an **`IdentifiedPiece` and not a tile id** — `tileSideViews`' answer, computed once in `purchase-scans-card.tsx` where the batch's sheets are in hand and handed up through `onIdentifyNew(sides)`; an id would make every dialog in the chain re-derive which sides exist and which still have a card behind them, and the swept-sheet case (#578) is exactly the one a second derivation gets wrong. It carries **no `stampId` fallback and must never grow one**: this is a picture of *the* piece in the tweezers, and a stamp's catalogue photo is a picture of *a* specimen — standing one in beside a condition field invites reading a condition off the wrong stamp, so ordinary intake shows **nothing**, which is also why the aside is `undefined` rather than an empty panel when a tile has no sides at all. The condition dialog therefore has **two shapes** — 36 rem alone, `min(96vw, 78rem)` × `min(90vh, 54rem)` with a piece — and the *form column keeps the width it was designed at*, so the questions do not move: the picture is added beside the form, and #593's field lands in the same column either way. Zoom is **not** carried between steps (each dialog opens fitted to its own panel), which is deliberate rather than unfinished: what #585 promised is that a comparison survives a *flip*, and hoisting a viewport through a chain of dialogs that unmount one another would be state with no question behind it. Two viewers are briefly mounted at once — the picker's, under a create dialog stacked on it — and that costs nothing, since the covered one sits at fit and `regionRequest` answers null at or below the view's own scale, so no second decode of a 30 Mpx original is asked for.

- **The catalogue value is asked for while the catalogue is still open** (#593) — one optional field in `IntakeConditionDialog`, and it is the **upstream fix for machinery the app already carries** rather than a convenience: closing a lot is refused when a copy has no primary-catalog price for its condition, which is why `N unpriced`, its filter and the quick-price dialog on the copy row exist at all. Every one of those exists because the price is entered a month after identification, from a list of hundreds, with the paper catalogue closed. Identification is the one moment the collector already has it open at that very stamp. **The primary catalogue only, one field**: `quick-price-dialog.tsx` (#147/#170) prices every vendor active on the area and stays exactly where it is for that case, but this step already asks about the stamp, the condition, the certificate, the format, the location, the ref, the disposition and — for a tile — the lot, so a row of vendor inputs would bury the step it was added to; the primary is also the only catalogue closing ever asks about, so it is the one that answers the case. Unlike #592's picture, this applies to **every** entry into the step, hand-entry from a catalogue being exactly the situation it is for. **The price belongs to the condition it was typed for**, and the field is keyed on the subject — `catalogValueSubjectKey(stamp, condition, certificate)` — re-reading whenever any part of it changes, so *Used* shows its own recorded price rather than inheriting the figure typed for MNH. **The format picked beside it is not in that key, and the figure always lands on the single** whatever the format field says. A paper catalogue quotes singles; the block of four in the collector's hand is that quotation times the format's factor, which is exactly how `pickFormatCatalogPrice` values a non-single copy, so filing the figure under the chosen format would write a single's quotation as the block's own price *and* stop the factor from ever applying. It is also what the shared quick-price dialog does now, everywhere it opens (see `catalog-and-stamps.md`), and two surfaces writing the same fact must write it to the same row. The label naming what the value is recorded against therefore names the condition and the certificate only — naming a format would promise a row this never writes. The reset happens in the **render that changes the subject**, not when the read for the new one returns, because waiting leaves MNH's figure sitting under a condition of *Used* for the length of a round trip, which is the very reading being prevented; that render is also the last one holding the outgoing subject's own label, which is what the *stranded* notice names — a figure typed and then abandoned by a change of condition is **said, not swallowed**, since a field that empties itself without a word reads as a bug rather than as a rule. What is *written* is `catalogValueEntry`'s answer, pure and unit-tested beside the rule it enforces: nothing on a blank field (the ordinary case), nothing when there is no primary catalogue, nothing while the context is still loading, and nothing when an untouched prefill is merely retyped — compared as a **number**, since the recorded value arrives as `12.00` and `12` or `12,00` is not an edit. An existing price is therefore *edited and never duplicated*, which is `quickSetCatalogPrices`' own find-then-write and not a second mechanism. The write goes **before the intake and on its own**: a catalogue price is a fact about the *stamp* and needs no copy to exist, so folding it into the three actions this submit reaches — two server actions and one flow that creates nothing until a later step — would be three statements of one thing. It **blocks on failure** (a figure read off the paper catalogue must not vanish in silence) and is **safely retried**, because the field prefills from what is now recorded, so a second attempt at a failed intake writes nothing twice. A stamp whose area has no primary catalogue with an edition, or whose context fails to load at all, renders **no field** — the rule the format field already follows, and this is not the screen on which to report a catalogue-configuration error. **The cursor opens in it**, which is #534's rule (a dialog whose fields are remembered puts the cursor in the one that is not) reaching the field that has since become the step's real typing: everything above it comes back from the last copy, the last tile or a pick, and this is the figure the collector has the catalogue open at. It cannot be `data-autofocus`, the way every other dialog says this — the shell's focus pass runs once on mount and on that render the field does not exist, being behind the read that decides whether there is a catalogue to price against — so the claim is made in the field itself, on the first render where it is on screen and usable. That makes it a focus effect firing *later*, which is the thing #534 warns against, so it is **spent on that first eligible render whether or not it was taken** and guarded twice: the subject must still be the one the dialog opened on (a collector who changed the condition is driving the form, and the read being waited on is *their* change's), and nothing may be mid-typing (the shell leaves the cursor on a `<select>`, which no one is part-way through; a text field with a caret is). The value is **selected**, not appended to, for `data-autofocus-select`'s reason (#183).

- **A tile can be identified the same way as the one before it** (#595) — an action on the tile dialog, and the reason it is *one action* is arithmetic about what the step already remembers. `add-copy-defaults.ts` brings back the condition, the certificate, the location and the disposition per collection, and #586 added the lot per purchase; what it has never brought back is the **stamp** (deliberately — the next tile is normally a different one), the **format** (#573's own decision) and the in-location **ref** (never remembered by anything). So "the same again" is those three, and since two of them are exactly the answers that must not be inherited, the whole of it has to be asked for rather than defaulted. **It repeats the previous tile's answers, not the remembered defaults**, and the difference is the point: the defaults are collection-wide and any other add-copy overwrites them, so the two diverge precisely after the collector has changed something for this card. Nothing recoverable holds a tile's answers, so `PurchaseDetailPanel` keeps a `TileIdentification` — **component state, never storage**: *this sitting* is the life of this screen, and a record surviving a reload would offer to repeat a decision from another day. It is built from the **submitted `FormData`**, not mirrored from the dialog's fields, so what is remembered and what was written cannot describe different intakes; and it is recorded in `run`'s success callback, since an intake the server refused is not a decision that was taken. **It fills the form and stops at the ordinary confirm.** A consumed tile has no undo short of deleting the copy, so nothing may be created by a single press repeating a decision made minutes ago; one extra press buys sight of what is about to exist, beside the picture of the piece it is about (#592). Mechanically it enters the chain **at the condition step**, skipping the picker because the picker's answer *is* the record — `prefill` leads every field's initial state there, and each field asks whether there **is** a prefill rather than whether it has anything in it, since a previous tile with no certificate is an answer and an empty-string test would let the remembered default put one on a copy asked to be the same as one without. **The catalogue value (#593) is excluded**, and not as an oversight: the field prefills itself from what is recorded for this stamp × condition × certificate × format, so a repeat that carried a figure across would be typing the same price twice. **Filling the format is not a reversal of #573.** That rule is about what happens behind the collector's back — a value usually right may be remembered, one usually wrong must not be, because a wrong value nobody chose is invisible. Here the button *names* the stamp, the condition and the format it will apply, so nothing is inherited; that naming is what makes it an action rather than a default, which is also why it is offered only once something has been identified in this sitting. For the same reason **Back retires the repeat**: leaving the step is the collector saying this tile is not the same as the last, so the stamp picked instead must arrive at the ordinary defaults with no format left standing from the previous piece. The neighbouring asymmetry is worth stating where it will next be read as a bug: the fields around the stamp *are* remembered, so "why is the stamp not?" has an answer (#573's, above) and "why is there a button instead?" has the same one.

- **Several tiles can be identified as the same stamp in one pass** (#596) — tick them on the strip, answer the intake step once, and get one copy per tile: one stamp, one condition, one certificate, one format, one lot, one location, one disposition, N copies. It is the up-front counterpart of #595's repeat (duplicates *met in a run* against duplicates *seen on the card*), and neither replaces the other. **The selection is deliberately not #571's.** That model's containers, exclusions and server-resolved count all exist for one reason — the copies list streams in pages, so a ticked container names rows that cannot be enumerated — and a batch's tiles are **all loaded**, so a plain set of ids is the correct model rather than a simplification of that one: the count is `size`, exact and free, and the write is handed the very ids that were ticked. What *is* kept from #571 is the shape — a box per tile, a tri-state box on the batch header ticking what is beneath it, **one bar for the whole card** (a run of one definitive can cross two cards of a parcel, and a bar per batch would ask the collector to answer once per card for one decision) — and it lives in the pure `src/lib/scan-tile-selection.ts` beside `lot-selection.ts`. It **must not fight #584**: the tile has been one big button since clicking any state opens its dialog, so the box is a *sibling* over a free corner (top-right; top-left is #582's outcome badge) with the click stopped there, and the rest of the square still opens the dialog. Only a tile that is still `unidentified` gets a box at all — a settled one can take no identification, so a box on it would be an offer the write refuses — which is also what makes the batch box honest, being *on* when every tile that **could** be identified is. The selection is **pruned against what is on screen** on every render rather than cleaned up by each write, since a ticked tile can be consumed, discarded or re-cut away from the dialog next to it and a bar counting squares that are gone offers to identify nothing. **Each tile keeps its own picture**, which is the one thing a naive implementation gets wrong: these are N photographs of N pieces of paper, so the copies are paired with the tiles in card order and each pairing runs #567's own `tileId → itemId` reassignment — N copies pointing at one tile's photo would be worse than no photos at all, because nothing on screen would say so. The write is **one `intakeStamps` with `copies: N`**, not a loop over it: that keeps the arrived-order rule, the format, the dispositions and the consecutive `allocateItemNumbers` range that function's, and the numbers then run in card order. The whole pass is **refused before anything is created** — every tile loaded and checked (still waiting, all on the one purchase, none named twice) up front — because a stale strip in a second tab should cost a sentence rather than a half-done identification with no way to tell which half; a duplicate id is refused rather than resolved, since the second copy of one tile would get no images, the first having taken them. `identifyTileAsNewCopy` **is** the many-tile call with one tile in it, and the action is one door (`identifyTilesAction`) for the same reason. Its success field is `outcomes` and **not `copies`**, which the purchase screen's shared `run` reads as the want review's `ArrivingCopy[]` (#532) — a differently shaped list under that name would be handed to it silently. **The count is stated before anything is created**, as every bulk action here does: on the bar, in the condition step's summary box and on the confirm button. **The aside shows them all** (#592's slot, `IdentifiedPieceAside` generalised in place from a piece to a list rather than forked): with several ticked there is no single piece and *show the first* would be one photograph standing in for fifteen — ticking them is the collector **asserting** they are the same stamp in the same condition, and seeing them side by side is how a mistake in that assertion is caught before it becomes fifteen copies rather than after. One piece is still #585's viewer, unchanged; several are a grid of **thumbs** (already the strip's derivative, oversampled at that cell size, where fifteen `full` crops of a 1200 dpi card would be tens of megabytes drawn two inches wide) with any one of them **one click from that same viewer** — the doubt a grid raises is answered by a loupe, and there is already one — and the loupe **steps along the run** (`‹`/`›`, `←`/`→`, `n / N` between them), because the comparison is made by flicking between neighbours at one magnification and grid → click → grid → click is three acts per pair; it deliberately does **not** wrap, the buttons going dead at the ends, since on fifteen near-identical stamps a step that quietly returned to the first would read as *still going*. What the app must **not** do is verify the assertion or offer to *find* duplicates: telling two shades or two perforations apart is the work being done here, not something to guess from a thumbnail.

- **The tile's marks live in a strip above the picture, never on it** (#628) — a fixed band across the top of every tile card, holding #582's state badge at its left end and #596's tick box at its right. Both used to be absolute over the corners of the scan, and an opaque box over the corner of a stamp reads as a **torn corner** — on the one square the collector judges condition from, which is what makes it a bug rather than a preference. The marks themselves are unchanged (opaque, hairline-ringed, `photo-thumb`'s reserved-slot device): they were built to say the same thing over a black card margin as over a bright stamp, and that property costs nothing once they sit beside the picture. The band is reserved on **every** tile, including the ones wearing no mark at all, for the same reason the border is always two pixels — nothing may shift by a pixel as a tile settles, and a strip that appeared with the badge would jog the whole row. It is the only place a corner mark may go: a tile is 5.5 rem wide, so there is no inset on the crop large enough to hold one.

- **A selection of tiles opens the tile's own dialog, and every outcome takes a list** — the correction #596 needed, and the reason it is a correction rather than a feature is that #596 had built a *bar with one verb on it*. Ticking tiles offered exactly one thing, *Identify N tiles as one stamp*, and led straight into the stamp picker, so a ticked run could never be **discarded** or **set aside** (#597) together although those are precisely the two answers a card of junk and a run of one doubtful variant need; the shortlist (#607) was likewise one tile at a time. The wrong fix is a button per outcome on the bar: the outcomes are a **vocabulary that already exists one surface over**, and the bar cannot show the pictures the answer is read off — which is the whole argument of #585 and #592. So the bar counts and **opens**, `tile-identify-dialog.tsx` stays the one place a tile's fate is decided, and the generalisation is `tile: ScanTileData` → `tiles: SelectedTile[]` (the tile **with its sides**, since a run can cross two cards of a parcel and nothing downstream could derive them from one pair of sheets — `sheets` therefore leaves the props entirely). What a run genuinely cannot do is what is about one piece: **assign** — front and back are singleton slots, so one copy takes one tile — and the **settled** panels, which cannot be ticked in the first place; both read a `tile = tiles.length === 1 ? … : null` and are simply absent otherwise, rather than being widened into something that would have to pick one piece to stand for the rest. The assign query is **disabled** for a run, which is also what lets the mode latch settle immediately instead of waiting on a fetch that answers a question nobody asked. Below it, the same shape everywhere: `scan-tiles.ts` grows `discardTiles`, `parkTiles`, `returnTilesToQueue` and list-taking candidate verbs, with `loadSelectedTiles` — #596's own *refuse the whole pass before writing anything* lifted out of `identifyTilesAsNewCopies` and now shared with it — so a stale strip in a second tab costs a sentence rather than half a card worked through with nothing saying which half. Four product calls carry the rest. **The park note is one sentence written onto every tile**, not held anywhere shared: thirty pieces of one definitive pose one question, and the answer is read months later off whichever square the collector opens, so a note living on a selection would be gone by then; the prompt says *what to check on all N?* for the same reason the count is on every other button here. **A bulk discard asks once**, and this is the one place a run deliberately departs from the single tile's bargain — a discard is cheap because it is reversible, but a discarded tile has **no tick box**, so putting fifteen back is fifteen presses and the reversibility that justified the single press is gone at that size. It gets the park prompt's treatment (replacing the other actions) but **not** its Escape handover: the confirmation holds nothing the collector typed, so Escape closing the dialog abandons it at no cost. **The shortlist is a union, never an intersection** (`mergeTileCandidates`, pure): the ticked pieces arrive with lists of their own, and intersecting would silently drop the very narrowing the collector came back to read — so every possibility is listed with `onCount`, and a partial one *says so* (*on 3 of 5*) rather than being smoothed over, while adding and removing write to all of them and converge the run. **Put back is offered only when every ticked piece is parked**: a mixed run has pieces that were never set aside, so what it is offered is the park, which is the move that makes the run uniform. The strip's selection is deliberately **left ticked** when the dialog closes — it is pruned against the strip on every render, so an outcome that settled the pieces empties the bar on its own while a cancelled pass keeps the selection rather than making the collector tick fifteen squares again — and the *open* flag is cleared when the selection empties, or the next tick would open the dialog by itself.

- **A consumed tile can be identified again, through the whole chain** (`reidentifyTileCopy`) — the gap #584 left. Settling a consumed tile *here* rather than navigating to its copy was right, but it gave the **wrong** answer nowhere to go: a mis-identification is discovered on the card — the piece two squares over settles the shade, #598's gauge says 14 rather than 14½ — and acting on it meant leaving the pass, finding that one copy among a parcel's hundreds in the copies list, and editing it there, which costs what the mistake cost. So *Identify again* **hands upward exactly as *Identify as a new copy* does** and the order panel runs the identification over it: picker, the issue and stamp dialogs it can open, then `IntakeConditionDialog`, with the piece on screen throughout (#592). The tempting shortcut — a stamp-only re-point from a picker stacked on the tile dialog — is the wrong one, and that is the whole design decision: being wrong about *which stamp* is usually being wrong about what was read *off* it, so a correction that could not touch the condition, the certificate or the format would send the collector to the copies list for the other half of the same mistake, which is the trip this exists to remove. What differs is only the chain's two ends. **The condition step opens on the copy's own answers**, not on the remembered defaults — `TileCorrection.prefill`, built in `purchase-scans-card.tsx` from `ScanTileData.item` (grown the copy's `conditionId`/`certificateStatusId`/`formatId`/`locationId`/`locationRef`/dispositions/`stampId`, all scalars on a row that was already joined), because a card worked an hour ago must not silently re-answer the fields that were never wrong; a field left **empty is a clearing**, which is what lets the certificate come off from the one surface that shows it. **The submit re-answers the copy instead of creating one** — `updateItem`, so a changed stamp lands in `ItemVariantHistory` exactly as the same correction from the copies list does, and the number, the images and the delivery axis are untouched. Three consequences fall out and are all deliberate: **no `lotChoice`** (a copy takes its cost basis from a lot per ADR-0009 §3, so moving it is a decision about money and not about what the piece is — and only *open* lots can be offered, so asking would quietly move a copy off a closed one); **not gated on `canIdentify`**, since no copy is created and the pool a closed lot was split across is the same set before and after; and **it does not feed *Same as the last*** (#595), whose record carries a lot a correction never answered. The picker `marked`s the copy's current stamp as *current*, the tree being read against the answer it is replacing, and the confirm says *Save the identification* rather than *Identify the tile* — nothing is created, and a correction that read like an intake would leave the collector wondering whether they now hold two copies of the piece in their tweezers.

- **A tile can be parked** (#597) — a **fourth `ScanTile.state`**, `parked`: a piece *still to be identified*, deliberately set aside, carrying an optional note. Some variants are not settled from a picture at all (a watermark, two shades of one blue, a paper difference), and settling one means leaving the desk for the colour key, the UV lamp or the reference album — a context switch that costs far more than the identification it serves. **The value is not the flag; it is that the parked pieces collect**, so the trip is made once for thirty of them: the same batching the whole scan-first pass is built on, applied to the one piece the pass cannot finish. It is **neither of the two states it sits between**. Not `discarded` — that piece deliberately became nothing and leaves the queue for good, while this one is still going to become a copy. Not plain `unidentified` either, or sweeping a card would keep re-offering it, which is exactly the interruption this exists to stop. So a parked tile is **outstanding but not offered**, and every reader that switches on the state had to answer for that: `OPEN_TILE_STATES` (`scan-sheets.ts`) is the one list of "still to be identified", read by the identify, assign, discard, park and manual-pairing paths and — the load-bearing one — by `stampBatchIfFinished`. **A batch with a parked tile is not finished with**, or #578 sweeps the card after thirty days and the collector comes back to the doubtful piece to find the picture they came back *for* is gone; that is the failure this state is most able to cause and the reason `batchDoneAt` counts both open states rather than `unidentified` alone. **It stays selectable** (`scan-tile-selection.ts`, #596) and `identifyTilesAsNewCopies` takes it unchanged, which is where the state earns its keep rather than merely being tolerated: the return sitting *is* the moment several settle at once — five parked pieces that turn out to be one variant are one pass, not five — and leaving them out of the tick boxes would have made parking a way of removing work from the screen rather than deferring it. There is deliberately **no un-park step in front of identifying**; putting back is the *other* answer (a mis-click, or a doubt that resolved into nothing worth a separate pass), and `returnTileToQueue` is one door for both waiting-room states because it is one move. **No migration**: `scan_tile.state` carries no CHECK, matching `offer.state` and `PurchaseLot.status`, and `note` is the discard note's own column — the two notes differ in what they are *for* (a discard's says what the parcel held, for the sight-unseen stockbook; a parked one's says what to check, for the collector returning with the lamp) and not in shape. Discarding a parked tile therefore **keeps the note it was carrying**, since the screen sends a blank one on every discard and clearing it would throw away the only sentence written about the piece. **The note is asked for at the button**, which is the one place parking's shape departs from the discard it otherwise copies: a discard's note is the *rare* case, so it acts on one press and the note follows, while a parked tile's is the *ordinary* one — the whole value of the state is the sentence read on coming back — so *Set aside to check* opens a field in the footer where the button was, under the cursor that just pressed it, rather than parking first and leaving the box to be found on a panel across a screen this wide (*watermark?*, *dark or light blue?*, *check perf against Mi 200*). Enter commits, Escape abandons the question and **not the tile** — the shared escape stack listens on the document in the capture phase, so a handler on the input cannot stop it, and `dismissable={!parking}` is what hands the key over, which also keeps a stray backdrop click from taking the sentence with it — and the prompt **replaces the other actions** while it is open, since a focused field with *Discard* still beside it is one stray click from the wrong outcome. It stays optional (an empty field parks the tile) and it is **one write**, so there is never a parked tile briefly carrying no note. Editing it afterwards, from the parked tile's own panel, is the one write here that leaves the dialog open: that note is not an outcome, and the tile is still where the collector wants it. **They gather behind a chip on the Card scans header**, not in a section of their own: the strip is a map of the card, the parked pieces sit in each parcel's own tray in that order, and a *to check* list divorced from the strips would be pictures with nothing saying which card to pull — so the chip narrows the existing strips exactly as *N unidentified* does, and the two are **one filter value** rather than two toggles that could be pressed into showing nothing. The order-level `unidentifiedTileCount` stays `unidentified`-only with `parkedTileCount` beside it (one `groupBy`, since Prisma's `_count` cannot carry one relation under two `where`s): the chip *is* the sweep, and folding the parked pieces back into it would undo the state. The **close-lot dialog names both**, and that is where the asymmetry pays for itself — the parked ones have deliberately left the nagging count, so the moment before the money is frozen is the one place they have to be put back in front of the collector. On the strip a parked tile is **a mark and not a shade** (#582's rule): a filled `pause` badge in the warning hue against the copy's accent `check` and the discard's outlined `excluded` — three marks differing in glyph, fill and hue at once, which is what survives 15 px — with the picture **not** dimmed and a warning-hued edge rather than the accent one, because the accent means *this needs you now* and the whole point is that this piece does not, until the lamp is on the desk. Whether parked pieces should also gather **across purchases** is left open by the issue and was not built: the pieces are physically in each parcel's tray, so a cross-parcel list means pulling from several at once.

- **A parked tile carries the narrowing that parked it** (#607) — a shortlist of **candidate stamps**, and the correction that says when a shortlist should not have been reached for at all. Discovering that a piece *cannot* be identified from its picture is not free: to know that a watermark or a shade decides it, the collector has already worked out which stamps it could be, and #597 kept the note and threw that away, so the return sitting started from the catalogue again. `ScanTileCandidate` is `(tileId, stampId)` — **the pair is the primary key**, so the row *is* the fact, uniqueness needs no second index, and a stamp pressed twice in the picker is an upsert rather than an error; no `position`, a shortlist of two or three having no order worth storing. Both FKs cascade (a deleted stamp is no longer one of the possibilities; a shortlist is a working note, not a record anything is owed to). Candidates are added from **the identification's own picker** — `StampPickerBrowser` stacked over the tile dialog with #592's aside, `dismissable` handing Escape over as `StampSelect` does — and never from a second, lighter chooser: a candidate and an identification name the same kind of thing, and two vocabularies would differ in what they can reach (a variant deep in a tree, a stamp created on the spot, the copies-held badge). Three decisions about the *cost of using it*, all made after the first build asked for too many clicks. **The shortlist is on every open tile, not only a parked one**: the narrowing is what *discovers* that a piece cannot be settled at the desk, so it happens before the decision to park, and a list that appeared only afterwards meant setting the tile aside, closing the dialog and opening it again to write down what was already in mind — `addTileCandidate` gates on `loadOpenTile` and never on the state, since a candidate written a moment before parking is the same fact as one written a moment after. A waiting tile with nothing listed shows **one quiet line** (*Cannot tell? List what it could be…*) and no panel, because most tiles are identified where they stand and a form in front of the ordinary answer is what the whole scan-first pass exists to avoid. **The picker stays open across picks**, alone among this app's pickers: the case it exists for is *watermark A or B*, which is two picks, and closing on the first turns listing three stamps into three trips through the area tree — what makes that legible rather than ambiguous is that the aside shows the **shortlist as it stands** beside the piece, so a pick is visibly landed and the next reads as *and this one* — and that the tree **marks the stamps already taken** on their own rows (`SelectableStampNode`'s `marked`, a tinted row plus a filled accent chip naming the list): a picker that closes on the pick never has to answer *which of these have I chosen*, and one that stays open leaves the collector reading the tree against a list on the other side of the screen and pressing a stamp twice to be sure. The mark **names the list** rather than showing a bare tick, since a catalogue row already carries badges for *held* and *wanted* and a lone ✓ would read as one of those; the row stays **pickable** while marked, because a dead row reads as *not allowed* rather than *already in*, and on a mixed run pressing it again is genuinely meaningful — it writes the stamp to the ticked pieces still missing it, which is what the hint says. A candidate any piece in hand carries is marked, since the question the mark answers is *have I picked this* and the answer is yes. And the correction below is evaluated **at the park button as well**, which is what the issue asked for and is only possible because candidates now precede parking. Pressing one identifies the tile as that stamp **exactly as picking it from the picker would** — `onIdentifyTiles(pieces, pick)` enters `purchase-detail-panel`'s chain at the condition step with the picker's answer already given, and with **no prefill**, which is the difference from #595: what has been answered is the stamp and nothing else, so the condition, the format and the ref still arrive at the remembered defaults. **The ordinary picker stays exactly where it is** — the footer is untouched — because a narrowing can be wrong and a shortlist standing where *Identify as a new copy* stands would make the wrong case the expensive one. A candidate is drawn as **the picker's own row** and not as a label — `PhotoThumb` + `StampTitle` + `StampDetailLine` off `useIssueMembers`, one fetch per issue and shared with the picker's cache — because a shortlist is compared against a piece under a lamp: the thumbnail is what the piece is held against, the subtype separates two siblings sharing a number, the price says whether the difference is worth the trip, and the copies-held badge and want marker say whether this one is already in the box. That is why `TileCandidate` carries `issueId` and `collectionAreaId` (the **first** membership, the one-issue-per-stamp rule; the row is fetched per issue and formatted per area, #377) and `unknownVariant` (picking an umbrella means *this stamp, variant not yet known*, which the row must say as the picker's does). It **degrades rather than blocks**: the plain label renders while the members are in flight and permanently for a stamp on no issue, and the row is pressable throughout — a row absent during a fetch would be the one thing the collector came back to press. The candidates also ride the **strip**, under a parked square and in its hover, which is what makes the *to check* list plannable: five pieces narrowed to the same pair is one comparison at the colour key, not five. **Nothing survives the identification** — `consumeTile` deletes the rows in its own transaction, because what the copy became is the record and refinement history (#101/#130) is where a later change of mind is written; a shortlist beside it would be a second, staler account of one question. Putting the tile back deletes them too, **with the note and for the note's reason** (the doubt is spent either way), while a **discard keeps both**, the tile being the only record of what a sight-unseen parcel held. Writes go through `loadOpenTile`, so a settled tile takes no candidates, and the stamp is checked against the tile's own collection. **The part that is not a feature but a correction**: much of the case this serves already has a better answer, and the UI says so. A copy may point at a stamp at **any** level of the variant tree, and pointing at the parent *is* "I know which stamp, not which variant" — the copy is created, flagged unknown-variant, valued cautiously (#238), numbered and filed, and *Identify variant* re-points it later — so *it is Mi 200, but watermark A or B?* needs **no parking at all**, a parked tile keeping the piece loose in a tray instead. `sharedVariantParent` (pure, `tile-candidates.ts`) is when that is said: **two or more candidates, all children of the same parent, and every one of them effectively `actsAsVariant`** (`variant-classification.ts`, override before subtype, false when unclassified). The flag is the whole of it and dropping it would invert the feature — an error, a plate flaw or an overprint is its own collectible and the parent is a concrete stamp in its own right, so on a shortlist of *the base stamp or its overprint* the parent is **one of the answers rather than the question**. It is one sentence with an action beside it (*Identify as Mi 200 instead*), said once and never repeated as a nag, and it **undoes nothing on the collector's behalf**: the piece stays where it is until they answer. It is drawn in **two places and is one rule** — in the shortlist, and inside the *what to check* prompt the moment *Set aside to check* is pressed, which is the moment the issue asked for and the one where the sentence is worth most, since it is cheaper not to put the piece in the tray than to take it out again.

- **The scan is measurable** (#598) — a **ruler** (two points, a distance in millimetres) and a **perforation gauge** (mark the first and last hole of a run, say how many teeth lie between them), both inside #585's viewer. Many variants differ by a measurement, and a 1200 dpi scan already holds it; nothing read it out, so a perforation question meant an odontometer and a trip away from the screen — the same trip #597 parks a tile for. The gauge is *arithmetic over the ruler* rather than a second measurement, because perforation is quoted as teeth per 2 cm and that is exactly how a physical odontometer is read: nothing new has to be learned. Everything numeric lives in `scan-measure.ts`, pure, because these numbers are the whole product. Five decisions carry it.

  **The scale is stated, never inferred, and it is the whole risk.** A measurement is only as good as its pixels-per-millimetre, and philately separates perf 11½ from perf 12 — under 4%. **EXIF is not read at all**: #574's source corpus claimed 1200 dpi against images that were 3841 px wide with an EXIF width of 5121, stale metadata from an earlier edit, and it concluded to ignore it. That mattered less there, where only *relative* geometry was needed; a gauge is an **absolute** claim, and a wrong scale yields a number that looks precise, is wrong, and then gets written down as a variant's defining feature. So `Collection.scanDpi` (NOT NULL, default 1200 — unlike the retention columns beside it, there is no instance-wide scanner for a null to defer to) is a **prefilled default**, and every function in `scan-measure.ts` takes the scale as an argument with nothing anywhere deriving one.

  **The scale is edited in the measuring tool, and editing it there does not rewrite the collection default.** Not at upload: most tiles are never measured, so asking there asks everyone every time for something almost nobody needs, and the answer is then inherited weeks later by someone who cannot see it — #573's rule, that a value usually right may be defaulted but must be visible where it acts. In the bar beside the result it is on view exactly when it acts. The asymmetry is deliberate and is the point: one old card scanned at 600 measured once must not redefine what every later measurement of a 1200 dpi card assumes, so `setCollectionScanDpi` is reachable from Settings alone and the bar's field is component state that dies with the sitting.

  **No figure is ever shown without the scale it was taken at** — not *perf 11½* but *11½ (11.63) at 1200 dpi*. `formatMillimetresAt` and `formatGaugeAt` cannot render one without the other, which is what stops a figure taken under a wrong default from passing as a fact, and it costs a few characters. Both the measured figure and the nearest catalogue step are reported, always: the step is what a catalogue says and what the collector compares against, the raw one is what says whether the piece sits comfortably on that step or awkwardly between two — which is exactly the case worth knowing about.

  **A measurement is taken on the scan's own pixels and, where it cannot be, is not offered.** Marks are held in **scan pixels** (`toSheetPoint`), so a reading is right whatever derivative the browser is drawing — the tile's `full`, or the retained original where `useSheetRegion` has escalated past it. The zoom then affects only how precisely a mark can be *placed*, which the bar says below `1:1` rather than quietly returning a worse number. A side carrying **no box** has no scan geometry at all — the fallback there is the *photo's* size, which has been through `FULL_MAX_EDGE` and may be a downscale by an unknown factor — so the tools are **absent** on it rather than approximate.

  **Nothing is stored.** A measurement is read and used; there is no *measured perf* column. Storing one invites a field that will sit beside the catalogue's and disagree with it for reasons nobody recorded, while the collector's actual conclusion already has a home — the stamp it was identified as, or a parked tile's note. The ruler also does **not** snap to the piece's own edges: the perforated edge is what one would snap to, and a snap that is subtly wrong moves the very points the length is measured between.

- **The teeth between the markers are counted for you** (#614; `scan-perf-count.ts`), filling #598's field when a perforation drag is released. #598 had declined this on the grounds that a detector's failures would be **plausible numbers**, which in a measuring tool is the worst kind — and that objection is answered not by a better detector but by **where the number lands**: in a field already under the collector's eye, beside a gauge recomputed live, marked *counted* rather than typed, and one keystroke from being corrected. A wrong 13 next to a picture of twelve teeth is visible; a wrong gauge copied into a catalogue note is not. Typing takes the count back and the mark with it; the field remains the authority and nothing downstream knows which way it was filled.

  **It is a narrow question, not a hard one.** The two markers pin exactly how much edge is in view — `k` teeth between the first hole and the last span exactly `k` periods — so the question is never *what is the period* but *which integer number of cycles fits this window*: one DFT bin per candidate, take the best, refuse when nothing stands out. The candidate set is bounded by the **stated scale**, since a plausible gauge is 3–30 and the run's length in millimetres is already known. That is #598's scale earning its keep a second time, and it is the reason this is a dozen lines of arithmetic rather than a detection problem.

  **The count is read off the tile's photo and the length is not, and that asymmetry is the safety argument.** The photo is the scan for an ordinary single stamp and a downscale by an unstated factor for an oversized one. A count is a *frequency* and survives resampling — an unknown scale factor cannot change how many teeth lie between two marks — while millimetres are an *absolute* and do not. So the length keeps coming from the marks in scan pixels against the stated resolution, and the two numbers come from different places on purpose.

  **The pixels are fetched from this origin, not taken off the `<img>` on screen** — `?inline=1` on the photo route. This is the one real deployment trap in #614 and it is invisible in development: on the redirecting storage backend the route answers an image request with a 302 to a signed URL on the provider's origin, which is exactly what that redirect is for, and which also means the displayed picture is cross-origin and **taints any canvas it is drawn into**. `getImageData` then throws on every count, on every tile, and only on deployments using that backend. So the route grew a mode that streams the bytes itself, the same trade its `?download=` branch already makes and for the same reason: this runs once per measured run, not once per thumbnail in a list, so the proxy hop is the cheaper side. The tile's own `<img>` deliberately still takes the redirect.

  **It refuses rather than guesses.** Two thresholds — the winning bin against the profile's RMS, and its prominence over the field of candidates — with an octave guard for the line that grazes the holes' rims and sees both walls of each. Both were measured on synthetic edges rather than picked: a real run scores ~1.28 at a prominence of ~19, plain paper tops out at 0.26 and under 2.6, and the constants sit deliberately on the conservative side of that order-of-magnitude gap. Refusing a real edge costs one typed number, which was the state of the world before #614; inventing one costs a wrong gauge that looks exactly like a right one. Finding the edge, moving the markers and storing anything all remain out.

- **A watermark can be read off the back of a scan** (#625; `scan-watermark.ts` + `use-watermark-view.ts`) — a third toggle beside #598's ruler and gauge, redrawing the visible region through a filter chain. The argument is #598's: the measurement is already in the file. A watermark is a **thickness difference in the paper**, so a reflective scan of the back very often contains it without showing it — a weak, low-frequency luminance variation sitting under the paper grain — and today that means the trip away from the screen (tray, benzine, second look) that the measuring tools were built to remove. Four decisions carry it.

  **It is a way of looking, not a derivative.** Nothing is stored: no second image, no schema change, no background job, no cached crop on the server. That is the ruler's rule (`scan-measure.ts`) applied to a picture rather than a number, and for a stronger reason — a stored *watermark view* would be a picture of one filter's opinion sitting where a scan belongs, and the collector's actual conclusion already has a home (the stamp it was identified as, a parked tile's note).

  **The chain is four steps and each one is load-bearing, in this order.** One **channel** rather than luma (blue usually carries the best thickness contrast on cream and toned paper, so it is a control, not a constant); a **band-pass**, a heavy blur subtracted from a light one, which is what removes uneven illumination and the paper's tonal drift while keeping the millimetre-to-centimetre band a watermark occupies; **grain suppression before the stretch** (a small median), because a stretch applied first amplifies grain by exactly the gain it applies to the watermark and yields a louder picture of nothing; and a **tiled, interpolated local stretch** with a clip limit, because a global one is decided by the frame's extremes and does nothing in the middle where the watermark is. The clip limit is the part that stops CLAHE's classic failure — a tile of blank paper stretched by an unbounded gain, which here is a field of imaginary watermarks. Every radius is in **millimetres of paper**, converted through #598's stated scale, so the same watermark filters the same way off a 600 dpi card and a 1200 dpi one; a wrong scale costs a slightly off-band picture rather than a false fact, so this one falls back to an assumed stamp width instead of refusing the way a reading does.

  **The fifth step #625 floated was tried and dropped.** A false-colour ramp or posterisation does make the output easier to read — and what it makes readable includes its own banding, since the boundaries a ramp introduces are artefacts of the ramp on a signal this weak. In a tool whose whole risk is seeing what is not there, a step that raises confidence without raising truth is the wrong direction, and the two controls #625 scoped (channel, strength) are what shipped.

  **What it cannot do is said in the UI, not just here.** Show-through of the design printed on the front occupies the same band and lifts with the watermark; capture dominates the filter (a dark backing beats any chain); the honest goal is *legible when the candidate watermarks are known*, not *clean*. The bar says so beside the controls, and the strength slider's own hint says that a mark appearing only at maximum strength is grain. **The render is pinned to the picture, not to the screen** — held with the box in scan pixels it was computed from and drawn inside the same transformed layer as the photo — so a pan carries it along instead of sliding a stale filtered picture across the stamp; the worst a stale crop can be is *too small*, raw scan at the edges. It is deliberately **not** a fourth `MeasureTool`: it takes no marks, the plain drag stays the hand, and it composes with the ruler over the same tile.

- A copy's **delivery state** (ADR-0009 §5) has one vocabulary: `src/lib/delivery-state.ts` (pure — valid set, labels, tint tokens, lifecycle order, `isDelivered`), read by the domain layer, the route handlers and every screen. Do not restate the list. On the Copies list it is a chip **only when the copy is not delivered** (#272) — delivered is what a hand-added copy starts as and every sorted copy ends as, so chipping it would badge nearly every row, the same reasoning that leaves a single format unlabelled — plus a single-select filter, threaded through the list, holdings-valuation and year-facet endpoints alike so the bar and the panel never disagree with the rows. The two exception outcomes (`UNAVAILABLE_DELIVERY_STATES`) are excluded from the *not offered on platform X* worklist (#259): a copy that never arrived or arrived damaged is paid for but not in hand and never will be, so it cannot be listed — while the in-flight states stay, since a copy on its way is exactly what one prepares a listing for. The intake screen renders its own editable delivery control, so it passes `hideDeliveryState` exactly as it passes `hideDispositions`.

- A copy's **disposal** (#394–#396; ADR-0009 addendum) is a *fourth* axis on `Item`, orthogonal to disposition and delivery: it left the collector's hands **after** arriving — lost, damaged in storage, discarded. It is the involuntary counterpart to a sale, both being ways a copy stops being held, one with proceeds and one without. Not a delivery value, because `deliveryState` describes *intake* and ends at arrival: conflating "broke a year later" with its `damaged` outcome makes supplier quality unmeasurable, and `not_delivered` additionally drops the copy from its lot and redistributes the allocation (#122), which is exactly wrong here — the copy did arrive and was paid for. Not a disposition either, those being *intent* rather than possession. Three nullable columns, `disposedAt` doubling as flag and timestamp so every filter stays a plain `where`; vocabulary and predicate pure in `src/lib/disposal.ts` beside `delivery-state.ts`, a note **required** for `other` (`lost`/`damaged` say what happened, `other` says only that something did). The allocation is **never** touched — `costBasis`, `lotId`, `itemNo`, photos and history all stay, and `itemNo` is retired rather than reused. Two preconditions that name their own reason (#273's rule): only a **delivered** copy can be disposed of, and one in a **non-terminal** offer needs that offer withdrawn first — enforced at `assertAddableCopies`, the single chokepoint every composition path goes through. Reversing clears all three columns: the axis records possession, not a history of it. **`isHeld`** (not disposed, and `deliveryState` outside `UNAVAILABLE_DELIVERY_STATES`) is the single predicate behind availability, collection value and the copies-held badge (#348); in-flight states pass, being copies on their way *in*. Soldness is deliberately outside it — that already has one mechanism at every reader (`saleLineItems: { none: {} }` behind `excludeSold`, #207) and a second would be a pure field test needing a join. What the predicate excludes is **moved, not dropped**: `HoldingsSummary.writeOff` carries the retained cost basis of the copies in scope that are gone, as a third bar line — a **cost and never a catalog value**, since a copy that is gone is worth nothing to its owner however the catalog prices it, while what it cost was really spent and hiding it would flatter the purchases. Because disposed copies are hidden from the list by default (`includeDisposed`, the `includeSold` shape), `getHoldingsValuation` **lifts that one exclusion** and partitions the scope itself — summed over the *visible* set the write-off would read `0.00` in exactly the case it exists for — while leaving `excludeSold` alone, a realised sale being #168's subject rather than a loss.

- On a purchase lot, **"to sort" and "unsorted" are two different counts** (#375). The header chip and its list filter address the `to_sort` state *exactly* (`LotIntakeSummary.toSortCount`) — a copy still `ordered` or `in_transit` has not arrived, so it is not waiting on the collector, and counting it sent them looking for a stamp that is not on the table. The close confirmation keeps the wider `unsortedCount` (`ordered` / `to_sort` / `in_transit`, `UNSORTED_DELIVERY_STATES`), because a lot whose copies are all still ordered has not been through the sort pass either and closing freezes cost-basis. The chip is therefore *narrower* than the warning by design; do not re-merge them. **Mark sorted** likewise separates its two axes: the `delivered` transition always applies, while the disposition offers **Leave as is** (#274, `LotBulkChanges.keepDisposition`) beside the three flags — some copies were filed one by one during the sort pass and a blanket value would overwrite exactly the decisions that took longest. It is a chip of its own rather than an empty selection, because all-three-off already means *clear the dispositions* and the collector cannot tell the two apart otherwise; the flag exists on the server because "no disposition given" already meant "default to `inCollection`", and that default is what it suppresses.

- **Storing sorted copies is one act** (#565/#571): **Store** writes a location, an **optional** in-location ref, a disposition and `delivered` together (`LotBulkChanges.locationRef`, riding in the same `updateMany` as the location it belongs to). It began as `File copies…` beside its own ancestor `Mark all copies sorted`, and #571 merged the two — the ref and the disposition had no business behind different buttons, and a whole issue group could be filed with nowhere to type the ref. It is a **superset** of the three doors it replaced, which is what makes the merge legitimate: every field may be left alone, so *location: leave as is* is the old mark-sorted with no location and does not overwrite the filings made copy by copy during the pass. **Move to location** stays a separate action with a **required** location, because the change of address *is* the act — folding it in behind a pre-ticked *mark them sorted* box would let a collector relocating a card silently declare copies sorted that nobody touched, and that asymmetry between the two dialogs is the clearest statement of why they are two. No new status is introduced, because the delivery lifecycle already says one — `to sort` is "arrived, not yet put away", `delivered` is "sorted and filed" — so *to be filed* is the derived `to sort` and nothing else. The work unit is `to sort` **whatever the copy's disposition**: stock going onto transport cards and keepers going into an album are the same act and the ref is what differs between them, so *Leave as is* (#274's `keepDisposition`) leads in the dialog and the three flags are there for the batch that is being decided as it is put away. A ref without a `locationId` is **refused** (`assertRefHasLocation`) rather than written where nothing can read it back — a ref addresses a place *inside* a location — and clearing the location still clears the ref with it. The ref offered comes from `getLocationRefUsage`'s read of the **target location** and never the lot: the box is shared across every purchase, so a per-lot counter would drop two `A147`s from two stockbooks into one of them. **The card offered is the one being packed, not the next blank one** (#629): `highestLocationRef` is the dialog's default and `nextLocationRef` is a button beside the field. A transport card takes twenty stamps over several sittings, so *continuing* `A147` is the ordinary act and *starting* `A148` is the exception — and a dialog that opened on the next free number made the collector type the previous one back in every time, which is exactly the ref most easily mistyped. The pair is one function and its increment (`nextLocationRef` = `incrementLocationRef` of `highestLocationRef`), so the two can never disagree about which strip a box is on; the **blank cards sheet** keeps `nextLocationRef`, since what it prints is the cards *not yet packed*, and the dialog's print link starts one past the current card for the same reason. The counting-on is **in the ref's own shape** (`B-3000` → `B-3001`, `A007` → `A008`, BigInt so a long ref cannot round) and follows `compareLocationRef`'s prefix-first order, so a box holding `A1…A200` and `B1…B5` is at `B5` and offers `B6` next — `B` is the strip currently being filled. A location nothing has ever been ref'd in **offers nothing and stays blank** — and shows no next-ref button, since inventing `1` is the one thing the blank field exists to prevent — which is the normal case for an album and the reason the ref is optional at all; a ref **already in use is a confirmation, not an error** (the button reads *Add to A147* beside the count), because a twenty-stamp card is rarely filled in one sitting. Now that landing on a used ref is the *default*, the two cases are drawn apart (#629): the current card says so quietly, any other collision keeps the warning colour — a warning shown on every open is one nobody reads on the day it means something. Nothing is ever allocated behind the collector's back: the **blank ref cards** sheet (`locations/ref-cards`, the packing list's print-sheet pattern — plain server render, `.no-print` chrome, `.print-footer`) prints a run of numbers off the same counter and reserves none of them (the collector's own word for the object is *fiszka*, but the UI, the route and the docs say **ref card** — the app is in English throughout and the ref is what the card carries), because the physical order leads (print the strip → pack the stamps onto a card → record the filing). It lives on the **Locations** screen rather than only inside the Store dialog for that reason. The transport card itself is deliberately **not** modelled: refs allocated in packing order land as contiguous runs, so a card is a range. **Selection is the whole mechanism** (#571) and lives in `lot-selection.ts`, pure and unit-tested: three checkboxes — copy row → issue group → lot header — each ticking what is beneath it, each showing a dash while only part of it is, and none behaving specially. It is **one selection for the whole order, held above the lot cards** and passed down, with **one bar** above them all: a batch on the desk does not respect lot boundaries (three lots onto one transport card is one act), and a selection per card draws a bar over every card and makes the collector press the same button once per lot. It also makes **Group by** and the sort a change of *view* — a row's containers come from the copy itself (`CopyRef`: `item.lotId`, `item.issueId ?? "__none__"`), never from the heading it happens to be rendered under, so the same selection reads correctly by-lot, by-issue and flat. A container records *the container* (`CopyContainer` = optional `lotId` × `issueKey` × `filter` × `disposition`; all absent means the whole order), never its members, because they run past the loaded page; unticking anything under one is therefore an **exclusion** (`excludeSelectors` / `excludeItemIds`) and not a shorter list, which is what lets the boxes stay live instead of freezing under a select-all. Several containers ticked at once is one act and must be one write, so `LotBulkScope.selectors` is a **list of records** — sent as JSON, not as parallel arrays, which is exactly how a lot ends up paired with the wrong issue key — and `itemIds` rides beside it: the `where` is *target ∧ (⋁ selectors ∨ ids) ∧ ¬exclusions*, and a scope naming neither selector nor id is the whole target. A container carries **the chip it was taken under** (`CopyContainer.filter`), so the write means the set the collector was looking at; pressing that chip **retires** it (`dropFilteredContainers`, called from the chip's own handler, never an effect), since "all 40 to sort" must not silently become "all 900" — and that is also what keeps anything downstream from having to judge a row against a filter it cannot evaluate. `unpriced` is resolved **per selector** through `listUnpricedItemIds`, bounded by the container it belongs to. The bar **cannot count itself** — a group ticked under a chip has no client-side figure and `unpriced` is a valuation, not a column — so the number it prints comes from `countLotBulkScope` through the same `readLotBulkScope` the write uses, and the two cannot drift apart. The lot header's box works on a **collapsed** card, cards starting collapsed; the whole-lot `⋮` entries are gone, since a menu entry beside the bar would be the third door again. The bar and the group boxes serve the **order-level** issue-only and flat views too, because sorting is what those views are for — lot management (add stamps, price, close/reopen, delete) is what stays by-lot — and the scope is `onlyOpenLots`, because a closed lot's copies are read-only in every view and get no checkbox anywhere. `unpriced` is the one chip no column answers — it is a derived valuation — so it is resolved through `listUnpricedItemIds`, the same fallback the paged read already makes under it; `lotCopyFilterWhere` is the shared column half, so the list and the write can never disagree about what the chip means. #175 (virtualize the in-lot copies list) is **obsolete**: its premise is the whole-lot render that #172's cursor paging replaced.

- **How close a lot's series are to a complete set for sale** (#563) is `lot-set-completeness.ts` over the pure `computeForSaleSetCompleteness` (`checklist-completeness-rules.ts`), drawn on the issue-group header of the purchase-intake views alone. Deliberately a **second module** beside `checklist-completeness.ts`, not a widening of it: that one is one issue's whole disposition × condition grid off every copy held, read on the issue's own page (#519); this is one cell of it — for sale, any condition — asked of the issues a *lot* touches, over a narrower counting set, with the gap **named**. The naming is the whole point and is what the grid cannot do: `5/6` does not say what to look for on the next card, and id lists in every cell of a matrix would be a payload nobody reads them out of. Two figures, and the reason they are two is the reason the copies-held badge draws `3 (+2)` (#528). The fraction and the **missing stamps** range over the collection's **whole** for-sale stock, because that is the question that provokes an action — a figure scoped to the lot says `5/6` about a series whose sixth copy has been in the box for six months and sends the collector looking for a stamp they own, and *missing Mi 204* is an instruction to go and look, so it has to be talking about the box. `fromHere` is the quiet second figure, muted and clause-less on a header already carrying an area, a title, catalogue numbers and a stamp count: it separates a series being built out of *this* parcel, where the last one may yet surface from the copies not sorted yet, from one assembled months ago. It is **intersected** with the stock rather than counted on its own — the two sets come off one `where` plus a scope clause so they agree by construction, but a second figure able to exceed its first is unreadable and the arithmetic is cheaper than the trust. The counting set is **for sale** and **in hand**, `IN_HAND_DELIVERY_STATES` — a third reading of `COPY_BUCKETS`, `held` + `toSort`, named off the buckets rather than by restating two states: *you hold N* (#562) counts `held` alone because a copy on the desk is not filed, but a figure claiming *this set can go out* counts it, since the stamp is there to put in the envelope. What both refuse is `ordered`/`in_transit` — bought, rightly counted by every other figure in the app (ADR-0032 §7), and not listable. An **`in`**, not `copy-counts.ts`'s `notIn` of the two unavailable outcomes: this is the narrower claim, and listing what counts is what stops a future delivery state joining it silently. **Per checklist, never over an issue's union** (ADR-0031): a stamp two sets share is counted for both, and the checklist is *named* only where the issue carries more than one. Batching is the reason it may sit on a header at all — a lot's copies are cursor-paged but its issue **groups** are not (`getLotIntakeSummary` computes them whole), so the whole screen costs a fixed handful of queries however far the collector scrolls, which is #133's own stated reason for keeping the fuller breakdown in a popup. Its **own route** rather than a field on the copies summary, and enabled only while the card is open *and* grouped by issue: the collection-wide read is the expensive half and there is nowhere else to draw it. The issue ids come from `getIntakeIssueIds`, which reads `FIRST_ISSUE_MEMBERSHIP` — the same one-issue-per-copy rule the groups themselves are built on — so a header can never appear with no figure behind it or the other way round. The missing stamps are **formatted server-side** through `compactCatalogNumbers`, the derived lot label's own collapsing, against the **issue's** area and prefix overrides so they read like the catalogue chips directly above them; `catalogLabel` in `area-vendor.ts` is `copyCatalogLabel` generalized off a copy for that second reader. They cross the wire as the **runs** rather than as the one string those are joined into (`missingLabels`, split back out of `compactCatalogNumbers`' own bare-comma join — re-implementing that join here to avoid one `split` would be a second statement of #150's rule), because the chip prints **three and `+N more`** — the lot label's own rule (#121/#172), so a collector meets one convention rather than two — while the popover it already carries for stating the range prints them all. Slicing raw stamps instead would split `Mi·PL 1-2` down the middle, which is the whole reason the collapsing happens before the payload is built. The truncation is a **rendering** decision and the payload stays whole: a list cut to what the chip shows would leave the popover with nothing more to say. The **missing count is always printed** beside the fraction, since `12/30` plus three numbers leaves *how many am I still short* to be worked out by subtraction. A **complete** set is drawn with `SET_COMPLETENESS_CHIP_COMPLETE` (#671) — filled in `success` with a tick, `✓ 6/6` — the very mark the Copies list's per-condition chips take (#594), and the tick replaced the “— complete” this chip used to spell: the words were there because a border tint alone was too quiet to be the whole statement, and a filled chip with a glyph is not. Two surfaces asking *how much of this set is held* now answer in one visual vocabulary, however different the copies they count. Everything the labels need — the area tree, the prefix map, the stamps themselves — sits behind **one** `missingIds.length === 0` guard and runs in one `Promise.all`: a card whose every series is complete is the good case and costs nothing to say so. What #563 **cut** is a route into composing an offer from a complete set: a complete checklist may hold several for-sale copies of a stamp in different conditions, so *exactly those copies* names no particular set, and preferring one condition over another is the ranking ADR-0032 refuses — #570 instead carries a selection **the collector made** on the lot's copy list into `AddToOfferDialog`, which already asks the only question there is.

- A **ref card's format is a template** (#569), not a constant in the page: `RefCardTemplate` is the collection's named dictionary of card sizes in **millimetres** (`cardWidthMm`, `cardHeightMm`, `fontSizeMm`, `paddingTopMm`), edited in Settings → Ref cards beside the collage templates and read **live** by the blank ref-card sheet (#565) at print time. It is `CollageTemplate`'s CRUD shape with its seeding decision inverted, and the inversion is the point: a printed sheet is recorded nowhere, so there is nothing to copy onto and nothing an edit could change retroactively — no reference anywhere, no in-use check, no snapshot. Two details the page must keep. **No rows or columns field**: `grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fill, <cardWidth>)` fits as many cards per row as the paper allows, so nothing computes a page size and A4 and Letter both work unasked. **Zero gap is not `gap: 0` alone**: adjacent cards each keeping their own border print a double rule, and a cut down the middle leaves ink on both halves — so cards draw **right + bottom** only and the container closes **top + left**, making every interior line one line and every cut one pass. The card carries the **ref and nothing else**, pinned to the top (the rest is inside the transport card's pocket); adding fields for what is printed is out of scope by decision. The template last used is remembered per collection through `add-copy-defaults.ts`' `LS_LAST_*` idiom and carried into the URL, the sheet staying a plain server render (#330); with no template at all a built-in constant prints, and no row is written on the collector's behalf.

- An order's **ROI** (#559) is #179's cost side read against ADR-0012's sale side over the same copies, and it is **two figures, not one**: `netReturn = realized − everything spent on the order` answers *has this parcel paid for itself*, and `soldMargin = realized − the cost of the copies that actually sold` answers *how did the selling go* — the first reads deeply negative on a barely-sold order and the second says nothing about the remainder, so neither substitutes for the other and both are stated with the sold/unsold counts beside them. `realized` is the sale's **own** allocation (`realizedProceedsForItems` in `sales.ts` runs `distributeSaleShared` over the *whole* sale, never over the touched lines alone, because the shared amounts are apportioned by line price and a subset would not be the figure the sale screen shows). Attribution is `attributeLineToPurchase` (`purchase-return.ts`, pure): a line made **entirely** of the order's copies takes its net **whole and unsplit** — the amount is this order's however the copies weigh against each other, and splitting it would let one unpriced copy block a figure needing no split — while a **mixed** line goes through `allocateSaleLine`, so the two screens can never disagree about what a copy fetched. A blocked mixed split is **reported, never thrown and never zero**: those copies count as sold, their proceeds sit outside `realized`, and the bar says how many, a total short by a stated number of copies being honest where a silent zero would be a claimed loss. Hence there is no per-copy proceeds figure anywhere in the read model — the whole-line shortcut never computes one, and inventing a share would be a number nothing derived. Copies that **never arrived** are out of every figure (no cost-basis, and they cannot sell); copies since **disposed** stay in, the money having really been spent — the holdings bar's write-off row makes the same call. It is asked at **two scopes and is one calculation** — `getPurchaseReturn` and `getLotReturn` both narrow `returnOverCopies`, a lot being what was bought as a unit and what a cost-basis was allocated over — which is what makes a line spanning two lots of *one* order split between them by the very same weight a line shared with another purchase does: for that scope they are two purchases' worth of copies, and the whole-line shortcut ignoring the boundary would credit one lot with the other's proceeds. The rows are **not a frame of their own**: they hang off the existing holdings bar (`shared/holdings-summary-bar.tsx`, optional `ret` prop, a rule above them), cost and return being two halves of one question about the same copies, and they draw only once something in scope has sold — a standing `−100%` on every parcel and every lot is noise wearing a figure's clothes. The **spend is named on the Net return row** rather than left to be read off the rows above it, which split what was paid into what is still held and what was written off (#396) while this figure is both.

- A copy's row menu links to **its purchase** (#387) via `ItemListItem.purchase` (`{ id, label }`, the supplier + date pair the purchases list leads with, resolved in `ITEM_LIST_SELECT`'s lot join). The entry is **hidden, not disabled**, when there is none — a hand-added copy was never bought through an order and nothing on the row promises otherwise, which is #273's stated exception. It lands on `purchases/<id>?lot=<lotId>`, and the purchase detail gives that lot #374's arrival treatment: seeded into `useCardExpansion`, scrolled to `block: "center"` (its own header is sticky), and marked with a **persisting** ring + dismissable strip rather than only the one-shot `just-added-flash` — an order can hold a dozen lots and a flash is over before the eye has finished reading them. The `RowAction.hint` doubles here as the *object* of an enabled navigation, not only as a disabled entry's reason.

- **Attaching an existing copy to a lot** (#388, `attachItemsToLot`) is the third verb beside `intakeStamps` (which creates copies) and `removeLotItem` (which deletes them): a copy entered by hand before the receipt was recorded, or filed under the wrong purchase. Only `lotId` changes — delivery state, dispositions, location, `itemNo` and photos all stay, because a copy already in hand does not return to `ordered` on account of a late cost record. **Both ends must be open**, and for one reason: a cost basis is frozen at close (ADR-0009 §3), so a closed *target* would not include the copy in the split it has already made, and a closed *source* has already distributed its pool over a set this would silently shrink, under-costing the copies that stayed — reopening it is the way through, and the refusal says so. A copy on another **open** lot is allowed but never moved silently: `allowRelink` is a parameter the dialog sets only after the collector has confirmed a warning **naming** the orders being left (a count cannot tell them whether this is the mistake they are correcting). Copies already on the target lot are a no-op, not an error. Refusals are **collected, not thrown** — one copy stuck on a closed lot is no reason to abandon the other nineteen. Eligibility is one filter, `attachableToLotId`, expressed as two `OR` branches rather than a `lotId: { not: … }` (an inequality is not a reliable way to say "or no lot at all" about a nullable column) and pushed into `buildItemWhere`'s `and` so it cannot collide with the search's own `OR`.

- **The sort screen filters on two axes, and the groups are the part that has to be told** (#622/#623). *What still needs something* (`LotCopyFilter`: the per-lot `to sort` / `unpriced` / `no photos` chips) and *what the copies are kept for* (`CopyDispositionFilter`: `in-collection` / `for-sale` / `for-trade`, #99's three flags) are **separate axes, `AND`-ed**, not one widened chip vocabulary — the filing pass asks both halves of one sentence (*the stock copies I have not put away yet*), and folding disposition into `LotCopyFilter` would make the collector choose between them. So the pair travels together everywhere `filter` already travelled: `LotIntakePageOptions`, the five reads' query strings (`intake-filter-params.ts` is the one parser, so a list and the write that follows it cannot disagree), `CopyContainer` / `LotBulkSelector`, and the summaries. Disposition is a **column on every path** (`dispositionFilterWhere`), which is why it needs none of `unpriced`'s id-set fallback. It is held at the **order** level, unlike the per-lot chips — a card of stock is filed in one act whatever lot each piece came from. It was originally kept in **component state** rather than persisted, on the grounds that it hides copies and a filter surviving a reload would have the collector open an order and find most of it missing; that call was **reversed** on the collector's own instruction, and both filter axes are now remembered per order alongside the expansion state (`shared/purchase-ui-state.ts`, and see `ui-patterns.md` for the cap that keeps that store bounded). The stated risk is real and unchanged — it is outweighed by what the filter is *for*: the filing pass is the long job on this screen, it runs across several sittings, and re-narrowing the screen each time was the friction actually being paid. What makes it survivable is that a filter, unlike a stale expansion, **announces itself** — the chip stays lit for as long as it is on, so the copies being hidden are explained on the screen that is hiding them. Do not "restore" the old rule as a bugfix. Both axes are read back through `intake-filter-params.ts` rather than cast, since the stored value reaches five query strings; that module holds the vocabularies and imports only *types* from `items.ts`, which is what keeps it free of `server-only` and usable from the client. Pressing a chip retires the containers taken under the old axis (`dropDispositionContainers`, screen-wide rather than per-lot like `dropFilteredContainers`, because the chips govern every view at once).

  **The issue-group headers are the one part of the screen that is not paged**, and that is the whole of #623: they come from `LotIntakeSummary` / `PurchaseIntakeSummary`, so a summary blind to the filters kept drawing a heading — and a *No copies.* under it — for every issue the filter had emptied. The fix is to hand the summary the same filters (`IntakeFilterOptions`) and let it report `issueGroups` over the **matching** copies plus a `filteredCount`, keyed on them client-side. Not a new endpoint: the summary already enriches every copy in the scope, so the matching counts cost nothing extra and *every* filter combination is covered rather than the one in the screenshot. What stays over the **whole** set is deliberate: the header chip counts (a chip counting only what its own filter left would drop to zero the moment it was pressed), the holdings bar, the derived lot label, and `lotWeightBase` (the cost estimate is a property of the lot, so a filtered view must not print a different figure for the same copy). A filtered group header counts what it is *showing* — `countLabel="shown"`, since the number is no longer the group's size.

- **The selection bar pins, and the pinned things form a measured stack** (#621). `CopySelectionBar` was deliberately in-flow, on the argument that a third floating strip would be a third thing *covering* the list. Sticky answers the argument instead of ignoring it: the bar takes `top: 0`, its height is measured (`useMeasuredHeight`) and handed down as the lot header's `stickyTop`, which adds its own height again for the issue headers inside it — the same nesting #172 built one level lower, so the three pin beneath one another and cover nothing. The bar's slot is **mounted for the life of the screen** and merely `display: none` while nothing is ticked: a wrapper that came and went with the selection would report a height of zero for the first bar it ever showed (`useMeasuredHeight` observes from mount), and `display: none` also keeps it from claiming one of the column's flex gaps.

- **Locations are re-read client-side on this screen, not taken from the server render** (#624; same class as #212/#440). Filing a parcel is exactly when a collector discovers a location is missing, and they add it in another tab — a prop from the RSC pass can never reflect that, and a page reload mid-sort is the thing the sort screen exists to avoid. `useCollectionLocations` therefore carries `refetchOnWindowFocus: "always"`: the new location is created well inside any stale window, so staleness is the wrong lever; coming back to the tab is the signal. The server-rendered list still stands in until the first read answers, so the first paint is unchanged. Note this fixes the same bug on every other screen that uses the hook, which is the point of fixing it there rather than in the picker.

- **An order can be the incoming half of a trade** (#644; ADR-0039 §12). Closing an exchange creates a `Purchase` with the partner as supplier and `Purchase.tradeId` saying where it came from — the same marker `Purchase.auctionSale` is for an auction settlement (ADR-0021), and the reason is the same too: everything the intake apparatus does (scan sheets, tiles, sorting, the pool split, ROI) should answer for traded material without a second implementation of any of it, and nothing should pretend the collector bought anything. **There is no money on such an order.** Its lot prices are the **carried-over cost basis** of the copies that went the other way, so no revenue and no profit is recognised at a moment when nothing was spent; the currency is therefore the collection's base one and `fxRateToBase` is null, there being nothing to convert and no rate anybody could check. The only real cash is postage, typed on the order as `shippingCost` like any other and distributed by the engine that distributes every other shared cost.

- **A trade's lots are per receive line, hold no copies at creation, and wait on their sources.** `PurchaseLot.tradeLineId` names the line each lot is for, which is what lets a tile identified into it be read against what was promised — a **substituted variant** is `item.stampId != line.stampId` and a **bonus** is a copy no line asked for, both derived and neither stored. Unlike an auction settlement, closing creates **no** copies: the material is identified through the ordinary scan-sheet intake, which is the only place either of those can be seen at all. And `closeLot` grows one gate for these lots alone (`trade-cost-pending`): the pool is the cost basis of copies that may still be on open lots of their own — a big auction lot is intaken over weeks and its copies are tradeable long before it closes — so the lot stays `open` while any source copy is `pending`, refuses **by name** with the orders to go and close, and has its provisional prices restated by `syncTradePurchasePool` at the moment the gate lets it through. Never after the first lot of that order closes, though: a source purchase reopened for a correction would otherwise move money already distributed to copies.
