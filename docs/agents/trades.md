# Trades

Exchanges with another collector: what leaves, what arrives, the sections over both, and the lifecycle the whole thing moves through.

- **The model** (#646; ADR-0039) is three tables — `Trade`, `TradeSection`, `TradeLine` — and one more counter on `Collection` (`nextTradeNo`, allocated by the same `allocateEntityNumber` bump as every other short number, #432). It reuses three anchors rather than duplicating them: `Item.forTrade` (the disposition, ADR-0007 §4), `Contact.exchangePartner` (the role, ADR-0008) and `Want`'s `stamp × condition × certificate × format` key (ADR-0032), which is exactly the shape a receive-side line needs, nullable members and all — a null certificate status **is** a value ("no certificate", ADR-0006 §2) and a null format means single. A trade is deliberately **not** a purchase with a price of zero (a purchase has a money pool spread across its lines, ADR-0009 §3, and a fictional figure there would be read literally by every ROI reader) and **not** a sale paid in goods (a sale is for realized proceeds, ADR-0012, and the incoming material has nowhere to live until it is identified weeks later). The vocabulary — statuses, transitions, sides, balance-rule resolution — lives in the pure `src/lib/trade-rules.ts`, the database half in `src/lib/trades.ts`; nothing anywhere reads a status column's *spelling* for meaning.

- **`side` is an axis of the line, not two optional FKs on one row** (ADR-0039 §1). The give side names a **concrete copy** — the stamps are in the collection and a copy is a copy — while the receive side **cannot**, because the partner's stamps are in nobody's inventory, so it names the `Want` key plus a quantity. Two CHECK constraints in the migration state that shape rather than trusting every future writer to: a `give` row must carry `itemId` and nothing else, a `receive` row must carry `stampId` + `conditionId` and no `itemId`, and a give line's `quantity` is always 1 (a multiple is one copy in one format, never N singles — ADR-0020 holds here as everywhere). **There is no pairing between the sides** (§2): two independent bags, the section the only structure over them, and the counts routinely differ — ten cheap ones for two good ones is a normal value trade, so no screen, schema or engine may assume a give line has a receive line opposite it. `@@unique([tradeId, itemId])` keeps one copy off the same trade twice and, nulls being distinct in Postgres, leaves the receive side alone — two receive lines for the same stamp in the same condition are an ordinary way to write "two of these", and `quantity` is the other way.

- **A section inherits its balance rule whole, or states its own whole** (ADR-0039 §3). `TradeSection` carries four nullable override columns written and cleared **as a unit**, with `balanceByValue` the discriminator: null means inherit everything. `resolveBalanceRule(trade, section)` is the only reader, and per-field inheritance was rejected because "tolerance 0 because the trade says so" and "tolerance 0 because this section says so" are indistinguishable on screen while behaving identically. Every trade has **at least one section**, created with it (`DEFAULT_TRADE_SECTION_NAME`), because `TradeLine.sectionId` is required — a sectionless trade is a trade nothing can be put into, so deleting the last one is refused and so is deleting one that still holds lines (moving lines by implication is exactly the silent reshuffle a list both sides are reading must never do).

- **Shipping is two timestamps, not two states** (ADR-0039 §4). `sentAt` and `receivedAt` are independent, clearable, and set in either order — the parcels cross in the post and one is routinely a month late — which is the same split `Purchase` makes between its delivery status and its lot lifecycle. `setTradeShipping` takes each as an explicit optional field precisely so that "not sent yet" (`null`) and "don't touch sent" (absent) stay different instructions; it is **not** gated on the lifecycle, because a parcel that has been posted is a fact and an app that refused to write it down would simply be wrong.

- **The lifecycle is `preparing → shared → agreed → closed`, with one step back at every unsettled stage, `cancelled` from anywhere live, and nothing after `closed`** (ADR-0039 §5). `TRADE_STATUS_TRANSITIONS` is the authority and an illegal move is refused **by name** — a menu entry that appears to do nothing reads as a broken button. The lock at `agreed` is the point of the whole lifecycle: the partner is holding a copy of the list, so `isTradeContentEditable` returns false there and recording that reality diverged is a different act with its own shape, never a quiet edit to what was shaken hands on. The trade's *header* — partner, notes, shipping marks — is deliberately **not** governed by that lock, and the status is deliberately **not** a field on the edit form: a select on a form is exactly the control that lets someone type past a transition rule, so it is a row action offering only what is legal from where the trade is. *Partner has responded* is a **derived badge, never a status** (§6): a negotiation goes back and forth several times, and as a status it would record the collector's diligence rather than the trade.

- **Two valuations, never merged** (ADR-0039 §7). The trade's own balancing terms live on `Trade` because they are terms of the agreement: the optional agreed catalog (`catalogVendorId`, `Restrict`), the mode (`balanceByValue`), and two tolerances in two units — `countTolerance` in **pieces**, `valueTolerancePct` in **percent** — because one number whose meaning depends on a mode is a number nobody can interpret. `ownValueWarnPct` guards the collector's own valuation and raises a **warning, never a block**: a deliberately uneven trade is normal and the app has no business forbidding it. The agreed catalog is independent of the mode — a trade balanced 1:1 on pieces may still name the catalog both sides speak in. It is a **`CatalogVendor`, never a `CatalogName`**: a catalog name is one book covering one part of the world (*Michel Deutschland* prices nothing Polish) while a trade routinely spans several areas, so naming one book would leave half the lines unvaluable and force the trade to carry a list of books the collection already knows. What two collectors agree on is the publisher; which volume a line is read in follows from its stamp's area through the same `CollectionAreaCatalog` resolution every other valuation uses.

- **Deletion guards run outward, never inward.** `Trade.partnerId` is `Restrict` (a trade records something that happened *with a person*, and a nameless one records half of it) and so is `TradeLine.itemId` — a copy promised to a partner must not vanish from under the agreement. Deleting the *trade*, by contrast, cascades to its sections and lines and **leaves the copies alone**: a give line is a promise about a copy, never a claim on it.

- **The index screen** is `src/app/c/[collectionSlug]/trades/`, server-filtered and server-paged like purchases (a trade holds tens of lines and the list is meant to survive years of them), with the status filter and the search in the URL. One search box carries **two meanings** — `#7` is the trade number, anything else is the partner's name — and which one a query is, is decided **server-side**, in the route handler, so the box and the jump cannot come to disagree. The row counts **both sides separately**, because that difference is the trade, and prints the receive side's piece count apart from its line count (three lines can be thirty stamps). Value figures are not on the row: they need the balancing engine, and a blank where a number belongs is better than a zero that is not one. The row is a **real link** to the trade's screen (#557), and the `t` quick jump lands there too rather than on the filtered list — the detour existed only while there was nothing to land on. Trades sits in the sidebar under **neither** Selling nor Buying, set apart by spacing beside Contacts: it is the one part of the hobby where the two directions are the same act, so filing it under either would be picking one half of it.

- **The trade's own screen** is `src/app/c/[collectionSlug]/trades/[tradeId]/` (#637), and the lines behind it are `src/lib/trade-lines.ts` — beside `trades.ts` as `auction-lines.ts` sits beside `auctions.ts`, because that module's own header says it does not own them. The two sides sit **side by side inside each section card**, never stacked: the trade *is* the difference between them, and a screen that put one under the other would separate the answer from the question. The **give side is drawn through `InventoryItemRow`** and the receive side through a row of its own, and that asymmetry is the model showing through — a give line names a copy that exists, with a number, a location and a cost basis, while a receive line names material in nobody's inventory, so rendering it as a copy row would print `#00000` and five empty slots and call that consistency. Both rows carry the **same 5.5rem reserved thumbnail column**, so the two halves line up; the receive side's picture is the *collection's own* of that stamp, since the partner has photographed nothing. Entering a receive line is the **auction lot line's two steps** (`StampPickerBrowser`, then condition/certificate/format/quantity), because it is the identical question about the identical kind of not-yet-owned material — and that picker already creates an issue and a stamp in flight, which is the point: a partner's material routinely comes from an area the collection has never touched. A **whole checklist** may be picked instead of a stamp and fans out server-side into one line per member, never a stored "set" line, for `AuctionLotLine`'s reason: every downstream reader (the balance count, the partner's copy, identification at closing) works per stamp. Totals are **piece counts, never netted** and never valued — the figures are #638's engine — and the whole-trade totals sum the *sections'* counts rather than the columns', so they state what is on the trade rather than what a filter has left.

- **Each `(section, side)` is its own list, paged on the server** — `listTradeLinePage`, one API route, its own search and its own filters. Three decisions hang together here. **There is no hand ordering**: `position` is entry order and nothing renumbers it, because a dragged order encodes intent as a fact nobody can see, says nothing to the partner reading their copy, and goes stale the moment a line is added. (#646's `reorderTradeSections` survives in the domain, unsurfaced — sections are not dragged either; the schema comment calling `TradeSection.position` *dragged rather than typed* records that issue's intent, not this screen's.) **The arrangement is grouping** — area › year › issue › condition+cert, each level switched independently, nesting in that fixed order (the order the material contains itself; condition last because it describes the piece, not the stamp), flat by default. And **the arrangement is the server's**, in the pure `src/lib/trade-grouping.ts`, because grouping computed over one page is grouping that lies about the pages not fetched yet; search and the filters follow it there, or the headings would count rows the filter removed. The levels are the **screen's**, not each column's: two columns nested differently are two lists, not two halves of one. Order of business inside `listTradeLinePage` is load → filter → arrange → count → slice → enrich, and only the slice is enriched, so a side of two thousand lines costs two light queries and fifty enriched rows. The give side's filters are the Copies list's own, through `filterItemIds` (added to `items.ts` for this) rather than a second implementation of "which copies match"; *no photos* is give-only because a receive line describes material never held, and *no catalogue value* is computed per line (ADR-0020) so it cannot be a `where` on either side. What a column does **not** own is its **scroll**: the page scrolls, as it does for a purchase order's lots, and the section's header band pins instead, with the group headings pinning **one under another** — level *n* at the band's foot plus *n* heading heights, so an area stays put while its years scroll beneath it and a year while its issues do, and an outer heading paints over the inner ones (`10 - depth`) so a level scrolling up slides under its parent. Both offsets are *measured* — the `useStuck` / `useMeasuredHeight` pair, lifted out of the PO screen into `shared/sticky-header.ts` so the two screens pin identically rather than approximately. One band per card and not one per side, because the two columns have to stay aligned and two independently pinning headers stop lining up the moment one toolbar wraps; the card is `overflow: clip` rather than `hidden`, since `hidden` would make it a scroll container and stop every sticky header inside it pinning to the viewport at all.

- **What the give side may promise** (`listOfferableCopies`) is the plain reading, chosen so as not to fight #639: in hand (`IN_HAND_DELIVERY_STATES` — a copy on the desk can go in the envelope, one still in the post cannot), unsold, not disposed of, and not already named by a **live** trade (`preparing | shared | agreed`; a cancelled or closed one releases its copies). The picker **opens on `Item.forTrade` and can be widened to everything held**, and that is a default rather than a rule: the disposition is where a collector files what they will part with, but a partner asks by name for things that were never marked, and a picker that could not offer them would send someone off to edit a disposition mid-negotiation. The widening changes what the *server* sends rather than what the client hides. Eligibility is **re-checked on save** and refusals come back **named per copy** — a copy sold or promised elsewhere in the minute the dialog was open is exactly the case — with what did go on staying on, `attachItemsToLot`'s rule.

- **The balancing engine is two valuations that are never merged** (#638; ADR-0039 §7, revised in place there rather than contradicted). The arithmetic is the pure `src/lib/trade-balance.ts` and the database half is `src/lib/trade-valuation.ts`, beside `trades.ts` and `trade-lines.ts`. **Own** valuation is `valuateItemRows` *called, not restated* — the identical function the copies list prices a copy with, no override of any kind, in the collection's base currency — because a trade screen quoting a different figure for a stamp than every other screen would be describing a different collection. **Agreed** valuation is the same call against a different book, through the one input that may be swapped: the area → catalogue map (`buildVendorCatalogMap`, added to `pricing.ts`), so the rollup, format factors, edition selection and strict certificate match stay one rule rather than two copies of ADR-0020 and #238 to keep in step. A per-line `TradeLine.catalogVendorId` is a rescue — "this one we look up in Fischer" — and is one more map and one more pass, touching the agreed figure only. The two are summed apart (`TradeLineValue` carries two fields of two units, never one field and a flag), printed apart in two named currencies, and **a missing figure is counted, never assumed to be zero**: `TradeSideTotals.ownMissing` is the count the gate is built on. Skew is measured **against the larger side**, which is what "within 5%" means to a reader and is the only formula that survives swapping the sides.

- **The gate refuses by name, and three things satisfy it.** A trade may not leave `preparing` nor reach `agreed` while any line on either side has no own valuation, and the check re-runs on **every** attempt rather than being stamped once — a line added while `shared` must not slip through on a check that passed last week. It names the offending lines (#418's shape, five then a count), because "3 lines are unvalued" and nothing more sends the collector hunting through both sides of every section, and the same list is on the screen while the trade is still being composed so the refusal is met before the button rather than by it. A line is named by its **catalogue number, never its stamp's name**: `Stamp.name` is optional and in practice usually blank, so naming by name produced *"8 lines have no value yet: Unnamed stamp, Unnamed stamp, …"* — the exact failure naming them was meant to prevent. It is the primary vendor's number for the stamp's area, through `formatStampCN` and `buildAreaVendorMaps` (#357/#377), so the refusal and the row it is about print the same string; the name is the fallback. **Both sides read alike** — no copy number in front of a give line, that being an internal handle which would make the two halves of one refusal look like two different kinds of thing while adding nothing a collector reads a line by. The same label titles the per-line value dialog. What satisfies it: a catalogue price; an **unknown-variant rollup** (#238), flagged as the estimate it is — blocking on one would throw every umbrella stamp out of every trade, and a negotiating figure claims nothing of what a listing claims (#617), which is the deliberate asymmetry; and `TradeLine.manualValue`, in the base currency and marked as the collector's own figure wherever it is shown. The manual value is the escape hatch for material no catalog prices and is kept deliberately narrow — the default reflex stays *type the price on the stamp*, a price being a property of the stamp — but it is categorically different from the zero the app refuses to assume. The **agreed** gate applies only where value balancing decides, and a value-balanced trade naming *no* catalog is refused as the one fault it is (`agreed-no-catalog`) rather than as forty lines each blamed for a figure nothing asked any book for. The own-value skew, by contrast, **warns and never blocks**, in both modes: a deliberately uneven trade is normal, and a piece-count trade can be just as lopsided in what it gives away.

- **Freezing is by status, in three regimes, and it is released rather than kept.** `preparing` reads live catalogs at live rates. The first move to `shared` writes `TradeFxRate` — `ExchangeRate`'s shape with the collection swapped for a trade, keyed `(tradeId, fromCurrency, toCurrency)` because a trade converts toward **two** targets (own to the base currency, agreed to the trade's) and one row cannot mean both — refreshable while the negotiation runs and refused **by name** at `agreed`. `agreed` writes `TradeLineValuation`, one row per `(line, kind)`, `kind` being an axis for the reason `side` is one (ADR-0039 §1): the two valuations are the same shape asked of two books and neither may quietly acquire a field the other lacks. Its catalogue name and currency are **text, not foreign keys** — the whole point is that a catalog renamed or deleted next week cannot restate what two people shook hands on, and the partner is holding a printout. The snapshot is **deleted whenever the trade returns to an editable status**: one rule, *what is editable is not frozen*, and a snapshot shadowing a live edit is the one way that table could lie. `setTradeStatus` orchestrates all three, after the write, because they are things that happen to a *trade* when its status changes.

- **On screen it is measures down, sides across.** `trade-balance-panel.tsx` draws the whole trade under the terms card — the terms are what it is computed from — and a compact strip in each section card's band beside the rule it is judged against, because a section is the unit a collector reasons in (mint against mint), which is why sections exist. One endpoint for the whole trade (`…/trades/[tradeId]/balance`, its own query), never one per section: the sections' verdicts and the trade's are read off one set of figures at one moment, and assembling them from several calls could show every section balanced and the trade not. Pieces are on the panel in both modes and so is the skew, but the **section strip is silent unless the skew is actually warning** — a warning on every section warns about nothing. Values are deliberately **not** a column on the rows: `InventoryItemRow` already prints a catalogue figure and a second one beside it would be two numbers with no room to say which is which. The per-line surface is instead **Set value** on both rows' ⋮ (`trade-line-value-dialog.tsx`), which states what the line is currently worth and which book said so *before* offering to override it. Beside it, both rows carry the purchase-order intake's **quick catalogue-value** affordance: `CopyValue` with an `onSetPrice`, so an unpriced line reads *+ catalog value* and opens `QuickPriceDialog` in place. `TradeReceiveLineData` gained a `value` for it — the same `CopyValuation` the give side already carries, valued for the page alone and drawn at the same height in the same component, so the two columns can be read across. That the affordance is on the **receive** side is the point rather than symmetry for its own sake: what it writes is a price on the **stamp**, and a receive line routinely names material from an area the collection has never touched — which is exactly where a price is missing and exactly when the collector has the partner's list and a catalogue in front of them. One dialog per card remembering which row opened it (a hook cannot be called in a loop, #531), the shape the PO intake screen already uses, with the two subjects built by `trade-quick-price.ts`.

- **Reservation is read off the trade, never written down** (#639). There is no reservation table and no flag on `Item`: a copy is committed because a give line on an **agreed** trade names it, the same way a copy is sold because a `SaleLineItem` names it — one place for the truth, so there is never a second place for it to be wrong in. The pure half is `trade-reservation-rules.ts` and the database half `trade-reservations.ts`, which sits **below** `offers.ts` and `trades.ts` and imports neither, since each of them calls it about the other (`trade-access.ts`'s move, one level further down). **Committed means `agreed` and nothing weaker**: `preparing` and `shared` are negotiation — a list being composed, or one the partner has not answered — and reserving against them would stop a collector listing stock they may never send; `closed` and `cancelled` release. That is deliberately narrower than `LIVE_TRADE_STATUSES`, which answers a different question (which trade already *names* a copy, so the picker does not offer it twice) and stays as it is. **The gate is symmetric and sits on the two transitions**: a trade may not reach `agreed` while a give-side copy is live on a marketplace, and an offer may not reach `active` while one of its copies is committed — plus the same guard on composition **onto an already-active offer**, which is the identical collision arriving through a different door. Both refuse **by name** (#418's shape, through the same `nameFew` the valuation gate names its lines with), and both re-run on **every** attempt: a copy listed the day after a failed agreement must not slip through on a check that passed then. The **reverse is a warning and never a block**: a promised copy that has sold elsewhere or been disposed of is already gone, refusing to record the agreement would not bring it back, and what resolves it is a withdrawal (#642). It is raised for any **live** trade, not just an agreed one — a copy sold out from under a list still being composed is worth the same sentence — and told apart by *sold* against *no longer held*, because the two are answered in different places. **Where it shows**: on the trade — since #662, as a mark on the colliding **line** itself (the collision in error, the departure in warning), with the offer to open behind the row's menu and only a count above the columns, which is what still meets the refusal before **Agree** rather than by it — on the offer (`OfferDetail.tradeCommitments`, at every state), and on the copy — `ItemListItem.promisedTo`, chipped on the Copies row and on the copy's own screen from that one source, #506's surface for a decision about a copy shown where the copy is.

- **The partner's copy is a link, not an account** (#640; ADR-0039 §9). `TradeShareToken` is `AssistantToken`'s shape with the collection swapped for a **single trade**, and that swap is the whole security argument: an Assistant token acts as the collection's owner across the collection, while this one names one trade and every read it authorises — the page, the figures, the scans — is scoped to that trade's own lines, so a leaked link exposes exactly the list the collector chose to hand over. **One row per trade**, because a second live link is a second thing to remember to revoke with no way to tell which is in whose hands; minting and regenerating are therefore one act (an upsert on the unique `tradeId`), and only the SHA-256 hash is stored, so the address is shown once and a collector who loses it regenerates. **Every live status serves and `cancelled` does not**: a link is an address for a list rather than a stage of the negotiation, `closed` still serves because the partner is entitled to the record of what was exchanged, and minting is deliberately **not** the `preparing → shared` transition — that is the collector's own act, gated on the valuation check, and one button doing both would be a button doing two things. Changing `showValues` or the optional expiry is kept apart from minting, since turning the figures off on a list the partner is halfway through must not break their link.

- **The page is outside `/c/`, server-rendered whole, and carries only what it prints.** `/t/[token]`, with no collection slug — the reader has no collection, and a slug would be a second name for what the token already resolves — and so no collection layout, no sidebar and nothing to navigate into. It is one server render of the whole list with **no client bundle**, because its second job is to be printed and a list that prints what has been scrolled to is not a list; the one interactive thing on it, the arrangement, is the trade screen's own grouping levels as **links**, so an arrangement is a different address for the same page and the partner can forward the view they were reading. `readTradeShareView` reduces both sides to display-ready strings rather than shipping domain rows: a give line is a copy the collection knows the number, location and cost basis of, and the surest way to keep that off the page is for it never to be in the payload — which is also why the two sides are drawn by **one** row component here and by two on the collector's screen, where the asymmetry is the model showing through. Both sides are headed **by name** (*From Anna*, *From Karel*), never by *give* and *receive*, which invert on the other side of the table. Scans go through a **route of the token's own** (`/api/t/[token]/photos/…`) rather than a second kind of caller taught to the collection-scoped one: a mistake there would be a mistake about a whole collection instead of about one list, and the scoping question — is this picture on *this* trade's lines — is asked in the domain beside the read that put the ids on the page. `rate-limit.ts` is in-process and coarse, and exists because this is the one surface in the app reachable without a session, where a bearer token in a URL is all there is to limit.

- **`showValues` is the only thing that lets a figure out, and it is off by default.** With an agreed catalogue every line is priced in it and the book is named once in the header; without one the page falls back to the collector's **own** valuation with per-line attribution (`Fischer Polska 2026`), because a column of numbers out of several books with nothing to say so cannot be read. That fallback *is* the collector's own valuation reaching the partner, which is why the switch exists and why a default that disclosed would not be a choice. The two valuations stay **unmerged** as everywhere else: own in the collection's base currency, agreed in the trade's, each named, with the rate note printed from `TradeFxRate`. #640's issue asked for both totals converted into `Trade.currency`; that sentence predates #638's engine, and re-converting an own total would invent a third figure nobody could check. Missing figures are **counted, never summed as zero**, on this page as on the collector's.

- **What the partner says back is feedback, and feedback never edits the list** (#641; ADR-0039 §10). The decision the whole shape follows from is that a list must not rearrange itself under the person who agreed it — so an answer lands as its own record (`TradeFeedback`, the pure half in `trade-feedback-rules.ts` and the database half in `trade-feedback.ts`) and the collector accepts or ignores each item. **One row per line, replaced rather than appended**, because there are no accounts on that page: one trade, one partner, one link, so a second row for the same line is the same person talking over themselves — `lineId` is unique for it, the whole-trade note is held to one by a partial index, and saying nothing at all (no mark, no words) deletes the row rather than leaving an empty one to read as feedback. The mark is **one flag with two words on top**, `tradeShareSideHeading`'s reason: of the collector's material the partner says *not wanted*, of their own *cannot send*, and one label would be wrong on one side of every list. **Accepting a rejection deletes the line**, and that is the collector's act under the same lock every other line write obeys — at `agreed` it is refused **by name**, with the step that unfreezes it, which is what makes partner input there a request to reopen rather than a change (`closed` takes nothing more, and `cancelled` never reaches the question). The feedback row goes with the line it was about (`Cascade`): what is shown is what is outstanding rather than an archive of a list that has moved on. **Unresolved is unread** — no second read marker, an edit by the partner clears the resolution and puts the item back — and that is what *Partner has responded* is derived from, on the trade's screen and on its list row (ADR-0039 §6), so dealing with the marked rows is what clears it. On the collector's side each item is a **mark on the line it is about** (#662, revising this issue's inbox in place); items named out of their column — in a valuation refusal, or in the count above the list — are named by **catalogue number**, through the labeller lifted out of the balancing engine into `trade-line-label.ts` so a line named in a refusal and the same line named here are recognisably the same line.

- **A signal about a line is drawn on that line** (#662; ADR-0039 §10, revised in place there rather than contradicted). This reverses #641's inbox and #639's notice, and the argument is the row: a banner that says something about eight lines is eight lines to go and find in four section cards, while the row is where the collector is already looking and the only place where *this one* needs no explaining. The pure half is `trade-line-signals.ts`, which **indexes reads the screen already makes** rather than asking anything new — a remark knows its line, a collision and a departure know their **copy**, which is the only handle `trade-reservations.ts` has, so a give row carries both keys and a receive row carries one. The marks are `trade-line-signal-marks.tsx`: the same chips and the same tones `InventoryItemRow` already says *Sold*, *No longer held* and *Promised* in, drawn by **one component on both sides** so a rejection reads alike on a copy row and a receive row — with the words in the shared `Tooltip` and the decision in the row's single `⋮`, never a cluster beside the chip. Those entries survive the `agreed` lock, because a locked list still takes a decision about what the partner asked for and accepting a rejection is refused **by name** by the domain, not by a hidden menu; the give row's `readOnly` therefore turns on *having nothing to offer* rather than on the lock. A **handled** remark stays, muted, on its line — #641's disclosure re-homed rather than dropped. Above the columns only `trade-signals-summary.tsx` is left, and only what has **no row to hang on**: the partner's note about the whole exchange, and a count of what is outstanding *by kind* (a listing is withdrawn, a remark is answered, a departure is written off — "5 things to look at" says which of them none) with a **Go to the first** that scrolls to the row's anchor, or says plainly that the row is filtered out or not loaded yet. That count is what keeps #639's own argument intact: the refusal on **Agree** is still met while the list is being read rather than by pressing the button.

- **The want signal is on the receive side too, and it means something different there** (#664). The give side has carried `WantChip` all along through `InventoryItemRow` (#532) — on material about to *leave*, where holding a copy never closes a want, so it reads as an upgrade hint. A **receive** line is precisely a stamp that may already be on the want list, and it can answer the sharp question rather than the vague one: it carries the whole want key by construction (stamp × condition × certificate × format, ADR-0032), so the chip says *this line would satisfy it* and takes the colour of the want's **priority**, which is what a want list is for. Open wants only, like every other want reader — a settled want would keep the marker lit for ever. `TradeReceiveLineData.wants` is loaded **with the page** in `enrichReceiveLines`, beside the valuations and keyed by stamp: fifty rows, one batched read, and never a query per row in the client. This adds; it does not move.

- **The partner's page gained client code, and only where it takes an answer** (#641). #640's *no client bundle* was about the list, and the list is still one server render that prints whole; what is client code is the control on a row and the note box at the foot, because an answer is given one line at a time and a Send button under two hundred rows is a button somebody forgets to press. Each control saves its own line the moment it is left, through `POST /api/t/[token]/feedback` — a route of the token's own beside the photo route, for that route's reason, rate limited like every other surface reachable without a session. On paper the boxes disappear and what was typed into them stays, which is the same bargain the rest of that stylesheet makes.
