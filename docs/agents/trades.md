# Trades

Exchanges with another collector: what leaves, what arrives, the sections over both, and the lifecycle the whole thing moves through.

- **The model** (#646; ADR-0039) is three tables — `Trade`, `TradeSection`, `TradeLine` — and one more counter on `Collection` (`nextTradeNo`, allocated by the same `allocateEntityNumber` bump as every other short number, #432). It reuses three anchors rather than duplicating them: `Item.forTrade` (the disposition, ADR-0007 §4), `Contact.exchangePartner` (the role, ADR-0008) and `Want`'s `stamp × condition × certificate × format` key (ADR-0032), which is exactly the shape a receive-side line needs, nullable members and all — a null certificate status **is** a value ("no certificate", ADR-0006 §2) and a null format means single. A trade is deliberately **not** a purchase with a price of zero (a purchase has a money pool spread across its lines, ADR-0009 §3, and a fictional figure there would be read literally by every ROI reader) and **not** a sale paid in goods (a sale is for realized proceeds, ADR-0012, and the incoming material has nowhere to live until it is identified weeks later). The vocabulary — statuses, transitions, sides, balance-rule resolution — lives in the pure `src/lib/trade-rules.ts`, the database half in `src/lib/trades.ts`; nothing anywhere reads a status column's *spelling* for meaning.

- **`side` is an axis of the line, not two optional FKs on one row** (ADR-0039 §1). The give side names a **concrete copy** — the stamps are in the collection and a copy is a copy — while the receive side **cannot**, because the partner's stamps are in nobody's inventory, so it names the `Want` key plus a quantity. Two CHECK constraints in the migration state that shape rather than trusting every future writer to: a `give` row must carry `itemId` and nothing else, a `receive` row must carry `stampId` + `conditionId` and no `itemId`, and a give line's `quantity` is always 1 (a multiple is one copy in one format, never N singles — ADR-0020 holds here as everywhere). **There is no pairing between the sides** (§2): two independent bags, the section the only structure over them, and the counts routinely differ — ten cheap ones for two good ones is a normal value trade, so no screen, schema or engine may assume a give line has a receive line opposite it. `@@unique([tradeId, itemId])` keeps one copy off the same trade twice and, nulls being distinct in Postgres, leaves the receive side alone — two receive lines for the same stamp in the same condition are an ordinary way to write "two of these", and `quantity` is the other way.

- **A section inherits its balance rule whole, or states its own whole** (ADR-0039 §3). `TradeSection` carries four nullable override columns written and cleared **as a unit**, with `balanceByValue` the discriminator: null means inherit everything. `resolveBalanceRule(trade, section)` is the only reader, and per-field inheritance was rejected because "tolerance 0 because the trade says so" and "tolerance 0 because this section says so" are indistinguishable on screen while behaving identically. Every trade has **at least one section**, created with it (`DEFAULT_TRADE_SECTION_NAME`), because `TradeLine.sectionId` is required — a sectionless trade is a trade nothing can be put into, so deleting the last one is refused and so is deleting one that still holds lines (moving lines by implication is exactly the silent reshuffle a list both sides are reading must never do).

- **Shipping is two timestamps, not two states** (ADR-0039 §4). `sentAt` and `receivedAt` are independent, clearable, and set in either order — the parcels cross in the post and one is routinely a month late — which is the same split `Purchase` makes between its delivery status and its lot lifecycle. `setTradeShipping` takes each as an explicit optional field precisely so that "not sent yet" (`null`) and "don't touch sent" (absent) stay different instructions; it is **not** gated on the lifecycle, because a parcel that has been posted is a fact and an app that refused to write it down would simply be wrong.

- **The lifecycle is `preparing → shared → agreed → closed`, with one step back at every unsettled stage, `cancelled` from anywhere live, and nothing after `closed`** (ADR-0039 §5). `TRADE_STATUS_TRANSITIONS` is the authority and an illegal move is refused **by name** — a menu entry that appears to do nothing reads as a broken button. The lock at `agreed` is the point of the whole lifecycle: the partner is holding a copy of the list, so `isTradeContentEditable` returns false there and recording that reality diverged is a different act with its own shape, never a quiet edit to what was shaken hands on. The trade's *header* — partner, notes, shipping marks — is deliberately **not** governed by that lock, and the status is deliberately **not** a field on the edit form: a select on a form is exactly the control that lets someone type past a transition rule, so it is a row action offering only what is legal from where the trade is. *Partner has responded* is a **derived badge, never a status** (§6): a negotiation goes back and forth several times, and as a status it would record the collector's diligence rather than the trade.

- **Two valuations, never merged** (ADR-0039 §7). The trade's own balancing terms live on `Trade` because they are terms of the agreement: the optional agreed catalog (`catalogVendorId`, `Restrict`), the mode (`balanceByValue`), and two tolerances in two units — `countTolerance` in **pieces**, `valueTolerancePct` in **percent** — because one number whose meaning depends on a mode is a number nobody can interpret. `ownValueWarnPct` guards the collector's own valuation and raises a **warning, never a block**: a deliberately uneven trade is normal and the app has no business forbidding it. The agreed catalog is independent of the mode — a trade balanced 1:1 on pieces may still name the catalog both sides speak in. It is a **`CatalogVendor`, never a `CatalogName`**: a catalog name is one book covering one part of the world (*Michel Deutschland* prices nothing Polish) while a trade routinely spans several areas, so naming one book would leave half the lines unvaluable and force the trade to carry a list of books the collection already knows. What two collectors agree on is the publisher; which volume a line is read in follows from its stamp's area through the same `CollectionAreaCatalog` resolution every other valuation uses.

- **Deletion guards run outward, never inward.** `Trade.partnerId` is `Restrict` (a trade records something that happened *with a person*, and a nameless one records half of it) and so is `TradeLine.itemId` — a copy promised to a partner must not vanish from under the agreement. Deleting the *trade*, by contrast, cascades to its sections and lines and **leaves the copies alone**: a give line is a promise about a copy, never a claim on it.

- **The index screen** is `src/app/c/[collectionSlug]/trades/`, server-filtered and server-paged like purchases (a trade holds tens of lines and the list is meant to survive years of them), with the status filter and the search in the URL. One search box carries **two meanings** — `#7` is the trade number, anything else is the partner's name — and which one a query is, is decided **server-side**, in the route handler, so the box and the jump cannot come to disagree. The row counts **both sides separately**, because that difference is the trade, and prints the receive side's piece count apart from its line count (three lines can be thirty stamps). Value figures are not on the row: they need the balancing engine, and a blank where a number belongs is better than a zero that is not one. The row is a **real link** to the trade's screen (#557), and the `t` quick jump lands there too rather than on the filtered list — the detour existed only while there was nothing to land on. Trades sits in the sidebar under **neither** Selling nor Buying, set apart by spacing beside Contacts: it is the one part of the hobby where the two directions are the same act, so filing it under either would be picking one half of it.

- **The trade's own screen** is `src/app/c/[collectionSlug]/trades/[tradeId]/` (#637), and the lines behind it are `src/lib/trade-lines.ts` — beside `trades.ts` as `auction-lines.ts` sits beside `auctions.ts`, because that module's own header says it does not own them. The two sides sit **side by side inside each section card**, never stacked: the trade *is* the difference between them, and a screen that put one under the other would separate the answer from the question. The **give side is drawn through `InventoryItemRow`** and the receive side through a row of its own, and that asymmetry is the model showing through — a give line names a copy that exists, with a number, a location and a cost basis, while a receive line names material in nobody's inventory, so rendering it as a copy row would print `#00000` and five empty slots and call that consistency. Both rows carry the **same 5.5rem reserved thumbnail column**, so the two halves line up; the receive side's picture is the *collection's own* of that stamp, since the partner has photographed nothing. Entering a receive line is the **auction lot line's two steps** (`StampPickerBrowser`, then condition/certificate/format/quantity), because it is the identical question about the identical kind of not-yet-owned material — and that picker already creates an issue and a stamp in flight, which is the point: a partner's material routinely comes from an area the collection has never touched. A **whole checklist** may be picked instead of a stamp and fans out server-side into one line per member, never a stored "set" line, for `AuctionLotLine`'s reason: every downstream reader (the balance count, the partner's copy, identification at closing) works per stamp. Totals are **piece counts, never netted** and never valued — the figures are #638's engine — and the whole-trade totals sum the *sections'* counts rather than the columns', so they state what is on the trade rather than what a filter has left.

- **Each `(section, side)` is its own list, paged on the server** — `listTradeLinePage`, one API route, its own search and its own filters. Three decisions hang together here. **There is no hand ordering**: `position` is entry order and nothing renumbers it, because a dragged order encodes intent as a fact nobody can see, says nothing to the partner reading their copy, and goes stale the moment a line is added. (#646's `reorderTradeSections` survives in the domain, unsurfaced — sections are not dragged either; the schema comment calling `TradeSection.position` *dragged rather than typed* records that issue's intent, not this screen's.) **The arrangement is grouping** — area › year › issue › condition+cert, each level switched independently, nesting in that fixed order (the order the material contains itself; condition last because it describes the piece, not the stamp), flat by default. And **the arrangement is the server's**, in the pure `src/lib/trade-grouping.ts`, because grouping computed over one page is grouping that lies about the pages not fetched yet; search and the filters follow it there, or the headings would count rows the filter removed. The levels are the **screen's**, not each column's: two columns nested differently are two lists, not two halves of one. Order of business inside `listTradeLinePage` is load → filter → arrange → count → slice → enrich, and only the slice is enriched, so a side of two thousand lines costs two light queries and fifty enriched rows. The give side's filters are the Copies list's own, through `filterItemIds` (added to `items.ts` for this) rather than a second implementation of "which copies match"; *no photos* is give-only because a receive line describes material never held, and *no catalogue value* is computed per line (ADR-0020) so it cannot be a `where` on either side. What a column does **not** own is its **scroll**: the page scrolls, as it does for a purchase order's lots, and the section's header band pins instead, with the group headings pinning **one under another** — level *n* at the band's foot plus *n* heading heights, so an area stays put while its years scroll beneath it and a year while its issues do, and an outer heading paints over the inner ones (`10 - depth`) so a level scrolling up slides under its parent. Both offsets are *measured* — the `useStuck` / `useMeasuredHeight` pair, lifted out of the PO screen into `shared/sticky-header.ts` so the two screens pin identically rather than approximately. One band per card and not one per side, because the two columns have to stay aligned and two independently pinning headers stop lining up the moment one toolbar wraps; the card is `overflow: clip` rather than `hidden`, since `hidden` would make it a scroll container and stop every sticky header inside it pinning to the viewport at all.

- **What the give side may promise** (`listOfferableCopies`) is the plain reading, chosen so as not to fight #639: in hand (`IN_HAND_DELIVERY_STATES` — a copy on the desk can go in the envelope, one still in the post cannot), unsold, not disposed of, and not already named by a **live** trade (`preparing | shared | agreed`; a cancelled or closed one releases its copies). The picker **opens on `Item.forTrade` and can be widened to everything held**, and that is a default rather than a rule: the disposition is where a collector files what they will part with, but a partner asks by name for things that were never marked, and a picker that could not offer them would send someone off to edit a disposition mid-negotiation. The widening changes what the *server* sends rather than what the client hides. Eligibility is **re-checked on save** and refusals come back **named per copy** — a copy sold or promised elsewhere in the minute the dialog was open is exactly the case — with what did go on staying on, `attachItemsToLot`'s rule.

- **What is deliberately not here yet.** The balancing engine and its frozen valuations, reservation of committed copies against marketplace collisions, the partner's read-only share link, per-line partner feedback, realisation (withdrawals, shortfalls, substitutions, bonuses), the printable packing list, closing into a purchase with a carried-over cost basis, and the Colnect exchange-list import. Each of those ships its own columns with it (ADR-0039 §"Consequences"); this file is extended by them rather than restating their decisions in issue bodies.
