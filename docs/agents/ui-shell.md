# Dialogs, Sidebar & App Shell

Dialog primitives, the escape stack, sidebar structure, config screen placement, notifications.

- Modal dialogs for list actions (add, edit). Build with shared `src/app/dialog-shell.tsx` primitives — do not duplicate dialog header, close, viewport constraint, or height behavior. A dialog rendered **from inside a row** must portal to `<body>` whenever any ancestor makes its own stacking context — a transform-centred dialog panel, but also a plain `opacity` below 1, which is how an ended auction row is muted (#354). Inside one, `DialogShell`'s z-index only ranks the dialog *within that row*, so the sticky toolbar and the app header paint straight over it.

- **Dialog buttons are one shape** (`baseBtn` in `dialog-shell.tsx`) and two details keep a footer's buttons the same **height**: `inline-flex` centring, because `Icon` is an inline-block with a `vertical-align` below the baseline and so stretches a button's line box — an icon-bearing *Discard* rendered taller than the plain-text primary beside it — and a **transparent** 1px border, so the borderless primary matches the secondary/destructive whenever content exceeds `minHeight`. Deliberately **no `gap`**: call sites write `<Icon /> Label` and that literal space is their spacing. `DialogActions` groups its footer **by role** — the other ways out (a step back, an alternative outcome, a control qualifying the action) in `leading` on the left, cancel and the action on the right, each group tight within itself — rather than letting `leading`'s bare `margin-right: auto` set the spacing and a four-button footer read as four unrelated buttons.

- **Escape closes one surface**, the topmost (#361). Every dismissable overlay registers with `useEscapeLayer` (`src/app/escape-stack.ts`); the stack is ordered by mount, which *is* the order surfaces were opened in, so the last entry is the only one that hears the key. `DialogShell` and `PhotoLightbox` register for you — a nested dialog, picker or lightbox therefore needs no Escape handling of its own. Do **not** go back to a document-level capture listener with `stopImmediatePropagation`: that only wins by registration order, so the *outer* dialog ran first and closed itself out from under the lightbox that had just opened above it. The shared listener deliberately does not stop the event, so popovers that are not layers (row-action menus, autocompletes, the translation popover) keep their own listeners and their own behavior; a dialog that must yield to one steps out of the stack with `dismissable={false}` (compose-set while the gap popover is up).

- Sidebar sections follow **what the collector is doing**, not what the data is: `Catalog`, `Collection`, then trading split by direction into **Selling** (Offers, Marketplaces, Sales) and **Buying** (Want list, Purchases, Auctions) — selling first, because buying is what a collection is built from but selling is what is worked on daily, and the section reached first should be the one opened most. **Partners** (#762) closes the list: Trades, Contacts and Colnect serve *both* directions at once — a trade is the one act where material leaves and arrives in one agreement (#646), one address book holds the house you bid with and the marketplace you list on, and a Colnect swap list is what a partner reads before offering anything (#686) — so they are a section of their own rather than the tail on `spacedAbove` spacing they used to be. Spacing was enough while everything was on screen; once sections fold, a tail hanging under a collapsed `Buying` reads as part of it. **Overview** stays outside every section and never folds: it is the one screen about the collection as a whole, and the way back when everything else is closed.

- A **`NavGroup`** is two or more entries that are **one subject** (#376, #762) — an icon'd row over indented children. `Stamps` → [Variant prices], `Inventory` → [Card scans], `Offers` → [Lot Builder, Bulk Listing], `Auctions` [Lots, Sales], `Marketplaces` [Sold on Allegro, On Delcampe]. Where the subject **has a main screen, the group's own row is that screen** (`href`): Stamps, Inventory and Offers are each a daily list with extra screens hanging off it, and a dead heading with that list repeated as its first child named the same thing twice and spent a row saying nothing. Where the subject has **no** main screen the row stays a plain heading — Lots and Sales are two equal views of Auctions, and Marketplaces is the name of a set rather than of a page — because pointing such a row at one of its own children would duplicate that child and light two rows at once. Lots and Sales are two entries because they answer different questions on different days ("what do I bid on next", across sellers, versus "what do I owe for this parcel") and reaching the second only through the first made the daily screen double as a doorway; they are *nested* because they are two views of one subject rather than two parts of the collection the way Purchases and Offers are. The same test put Variant prices under Stamps and Card scans under Inventory, and it is what split the marketplace screens off the five-child Offers group: composing a listing and reading back what a marketplace has since done with it are opposite ends of the work, and one group over all five said they were the same sitting. Children take no icon — the group's names the subject and the indent says who belongs to it. **The indent has to be readable, and for one release it was not**: a child's label sat four pixels right of its parent's (36px against 40px) with the guide line under the parent's *icon*, so the two ranks rendered as one texture and the sidebar read as chaos. The rule now is that the line stands where the parent's icon ends (`1.25rem`) and a child's label starts half a step past the parent's (44px against 36px), with the child one step down in size and colour (`0.8125rem`, `--color-text-muted`): the indent says *whose* it is, the type says *what rank* it is, and neither carries the distinction alone. `isActive` takes an `exact` flag for a pair on one branch (every sale route lives under `/auctions`, so `startsWith` would light *Lots* on a sale) and an `except` list for a parent that keeps its *other* sub-routes (an offer's own screen is still the offer list's). Groups themselves do **not** collapse — only sections do — because two ranks of fold would put a screen behind two clicks, and a group exists precisely because its members are read together.

- **Each section carries a quiet hue** (#762), and it is not decoration: the sidebar reuses colours the app already reads elsewhere, so a tint says the same thing twice rather than something new. Green, blue and violet are the **disposition** colours — in collection, for sale, for trade — so `Collection`, `Selling` and `Partners` wear the colour their own records already carry, and `Buying` takes amber, money going the other way. `Catalog` takes the near-neutral **slate**, and that is the point of it: five saturated families one under another is the loud sidebar this was fixing, and the catalog is the section to spend the least colour on — the app's reference layer, read to look something up rather than worked in all day. Hunting for a fifth hue is what once put Selling on pink and made the whole column shout; the fix was to stop hunting. Where a hue would be read as a *state* it is left alone — red and orange say something is wrong or urgent everywhere else in the app — and teal is the accent. Where the hue lands is the whole restraint. At **full strength**: the section heading, the guide line of its groups, every resting entry's **icon**, and an **active row's** plate, bar and label (`--color-tag-*`, `-soft`, `-border`). **Mixed back towards the text colour** (`color-mix(… 55%, --color-text-secondary)`, and `--color-text-muted` for a nested child or a group heading, so the rank survives the tint): every **resting label**. A row reads as part of its family without the sidebar becoming twenty saturated links beside the screen's own content, and the full hue is left to mean one thing — the row you are on. The icon is where colour can be read as identity; the label is where it still has to read as text. **Weight and size are spent on the section heading, not on the entries** (`0.8125rem`/`700` uppercase against the entries' `0.875rem`/`400`): the heading is the only row in the column that is not a link, so weight there separates the ranks, where a column of bold entries spends the strongest signal available on saying "these are links" and flattens what it was meant to rank. Inside the list, weight stays the active row's alone. **Active speaks in its section's hue** rather than in the app accent: where you are and which part of the app you are in are one statement, not two. Only Overview and the footer, which sit in no section and answer to the collection as a whole, still light in accent teal — that is what the `null` tint outside a `NavSection` means. **Hover stays colourless** (`--color-bg-subtle`), so the row under the pointer is never mistaken for the live one. A heading also carries a **hairline rule from its label to the sidebar's edge** in the section's `-border` tint: it is part of the heading rather than a divider sitting in the gap between two sections — a line belonging to a heading says what it covers, where a line in the gap belongs to neither side of it — and it is `aria-hidden`, repeating what the heading already states. The tint reaches a `NavGroup` through `SectionTintContext` rather than a prop at every call site — a group cannot be in two sections, so repeating it would only be a way to get it wrong. **Active is the tinted plate plus a 2px inset bar** on the row's leading edge: on a list this dense the plate alone is easy to lose, and a bar down one edge is what the eye can run along. A **collapsed** section holding the current screen wears that same pair on its heading.

- **Sections collapse; what is open is decided by where you are** (#762). Twenty destinations under five headings scrolled on a laptop — the state #75 was filed about, arrived at again — and collapsing was preferred to dropping entries, since each one is somebody's daily screen on some day: what changes is how many are on screen at once. The **default is derived, not stored**: the section owning the current route is open and the rest are closed, so arriving anywhere shows that screen's neighbours and nothing else. Only **explicit toggles** are persisted (`sidebar-sections`, a JSON map through `usePersistedCollectionValue`, so per collection and per browser) — a collector working across two sections should not reopen the second one on every navigation, and a stored *whole* state would instead mean re-deriving nothing and going stale as routes are added. `parseOpenSections` is deliberately tolerant, on `parseRecentEntities`' reasoning: storage from an older build must degrade to the route rule, never to a sidebar that throws. Which section owns which route is one table, `SECTION_ROUTES`, and it is stated as **route prefixes rather than read off the rendered entries** — a collapsed section renders none of them, so the mapping has to answer before its children exist. Collapsing the section you are standing in is allowed (you are already on the screen) and the heading then takes the **active tint**, so a folded section still says *you are here*. The heading is a `<button>` with `aria-expanded`, not a styled `div`, and a closed section's entries are **unmounted** rather than hidden so no `NavItem` keeps a hover state behind the fold. Pre-hydration the stored map reads empty, which is exactly the route-derived state, so the server renders what a first visit shows and adopts the collector's own toggles after hydration.

- **Recently visited records** (#599) hang off the quick-jump box rather than being a surface of their own. The two answer the same question — "take me to a record" — from opposite ends (by the number one is holding, or by having just been there), so one control and one `⌘K` serve both; typing narrows the recents *by name* while the same text is still read as a jump, which is the half of "take me to a record" the prefix scheme cannot serve. A **panel on focus**, never a permanent list in the sidebar: the way back to something is wanted at the moment one goes looking, not for the whole time one is reading, and the nav is what the sidebar's space is for. It **portals to `<body>`** and closes on outside-click / Escape / resize, on the notification panel's reasoning (the sidebar scrolls, so it sets `overflow-y`, and a box clipped on one axis is clipped on both); it is a popover, not an escape layer. Storage is **localStorage per collection** (`use-recent-entities.ts`, beside `usePersistedCollectionValue`): a visit is a fact about one person's browsing, not about the collection, so it earns neither a table, a write on every page view, nor a pruning rule. What a visit *is* — dedupe by (kind, id), most-recent-first, capped at `RECENT_ENTITY_LIMIT` — is pure in `src/lib/recent-entities.ts` where the unit tests hold it, and `parseRecentEntities` is deliberately tolerant (browser storage written by an older build must degrade to a shorter list, never to a sidebar that throws) and refuses an absolute `href`, since nothing here writes a link off the app. An entry is **self-contained** — kind, id, href, label, sublabel — so the panel draws seven rows without seven queries and a deleted record simply 404s once and falls off the end. Recording is the **detail page's** job (`<RecordRecentVisit>`, a null-rendering client component): only the page knows what record it is about and what that record is called, and the label is the screen's own, so the panel and the page cannot disagree. The auction sale records from its *panel* instead, being the one detail screen whose record is fetched client-side. `RecentEntityKind` is deliberately **not** `QuickJumpEntity`: that set is "what carries a short number", this one is "what has a screen to go back to" — a stamp has a page but no number, an auction lot has a number but is read on its sale's screen, so the sale is what gets recorded.

- Config screen placement: **catalog taxonomy** that is set up once (Areas, Catalogs, Conditions) lives in **Settings** tabs; **operational data** touched routinely (Locations) gets its own **Collection** nav page. Both reuse the same adjacency-list tree pattern (`buildTree`, `*-tree-select.tsx`, `RowActionsMenu`) — mirror `areas-panel.tsx` / `locations-panel.tsx` rather than reinventing tree/list/dialog scaffolding.

- **How wide a Settings tab is allowed to get is the tab's own answer** (#691), `TAB_MAX_WIDTH` in `settings-tabs.tsx` — not the page's, which is why the cap moved off the `settings/page.tsx` wrapper and onto the tabs. The default stays **56rem**: almost every tab is a column of labelled fields, and a form field stretched across a wide monitor is harder to read, not easier. The **Areas** tab is the exception at **80rem**, because it is the one tab that is a *tree*: every level of nesting spends width on indentation while each row also states the catalog configuration in force beside the name (#675), so at 56rem a fourth-level area had its valuing-volume name cut mid-word and its prefix chips crowded against the stamp count. Widening was preferred over shrinking the indent — the indent *is* how the nesting is read — and over reflowing the chips onto a second line, which would make a row's height depend on how deep it sits. It stays **bounded**: an uncapped row would strand the chips at the far edge of a wide screen from the name they describe. The cap covers the tab strip as well as the panel, so the border under the tabs still runs the width of what it sits above. Inside the row the rule is **the name yields first**: the chips are single tokens that never wrap or shrink (`whiteSpace: nowrap`, `flexShrink: 0`), the valuing-volume chip is the one carrying prose so it is the one that flexes (`4rem`–`24rem`, ellipsis, full value in its tooltip), and the stamp count and `⋮` hold their width — half a catalog prefix says nothing, while a shortened area name is still the row's identity.

- The sidebar's **top row is identity, not a control**: the collection's initial and name, with the notification bell beside it. There is no collection switcher — changing collection is rare, so it is an **All collections** entry in the footer beside Settings, and a dropdown was a permanent control in the chrome's most prominent row for an occasional act. The collection layout therefore no longer loads the owner's other collections on every screen.

- The **notification centre** (#367) is a bell **in the sidebar's top row**, beside the collection name — the app has no top chrome, and a badge in the footer beside Sign out is a badge nobody sees. An **icon with a count bubble**, never a nav entry: it is not a destination, and a full-width labelled row put a permanent extra item in the list of places to go for something that is usually empty. The bubble sits *on* the bell rather than beside it, so a count appearing does not shift the row's other contents; the switcher button gives up its own bottom border to the row that now holds both. It **reports nothing of its own**: every group is an existing derivation read through the same exported function its own screen reads it through (`listAuctionLots` at the lots screen's own `closing` windows; the needs-action SQL, ADR-0013 §4), so the panel and the screen its "N more →" lands on cannot disagree about what "closing today" means. Sources are **providers, not groups**, in `src/lib/action-items.ts`: a provider returns one *or more* groups, which is what lets the two offer groups come out of one read — they are one flag split by *reason* (#167's sold copy vs #215's live bid), and the derived offer label they need would otherwise pay for the area tree twice (#379). Adding a source is one entry in `PROVIDERS`; the route handler, the badge and the panel are written against the shape and never against the list. `NeedsActionRow` therefore carries `soldCount`/`biddingCount` **beside** the untouched `deadCount` — a copy dead both ways is one dead copy and two problems, so they are counted separately rather than derived from one another, and the badge's total is the **rows** under it for the same reason. A group carries a **severity** (`critical` / `warning` / `info`), which is what decides both its tint and the panel's reading order — graded by **consequence, not by subject**: a possible **double sale** is `critical` (both offer groups: one piece of stock committed in two places, costing money and a rating, undoable only by the collector), a **deadline that can still be met** is `warning` (a lot closing today), and **bookkeeping that never fixes itself** is `info` (an ended lot with no outcome). It is the lot list's own rule — colour means *act now*, which is why an ended lot there is muted rather than reddened — applied across sources. Provider order is only the tie-break, so a source's place in `PROVIDERS` can never outrank a worse problem from another one. The badge is tinted by `ActionItemsResult.severity` — the **worst** thing waiting, never the count, or three ended lots would outshout one double sale — and that grade is resolved **server-side** so the bell and the panel cannot grade one list two ways. In the panel the **heading** carries the tint, not the rows: a list of names is read, and tinting every line makes it harder to triage rather than easier. An item carries its instant as ISO and the client formats it (`formatRelative`): "in 3 hours" needs the browser's clock and locale, and a server-rendered string goes stale while the panel sits open. Hrefs are **collection-relative** — the slug is a routing concern and the domain layer only holds the id. It **refreshes off the query cache itself**: the bell subscribes to `getQueryCache()` and re-reads (debounced) whenever any *other* key is invalidated, because every mutation here is a server action followed by the screen invalidating what it shows — so recording a lot's outcome empties a group and drops the badge at once, without a single mutation site knowing the panel exists. Wiring each source's mutations in by hand would undo the registry: a new provider would mean hunting down every action that could feed it. The interval and the focus refetch stay as the backstop for anything that refreshes by `router.refresh()` alone. Nothing is acted on in the panel: an action taken in a popover is one taken without the context that makes it the right one. It is a **popover, not a layer**, so it keeps its own Escape/outside-click rather than joining the escape stack (#361), and it **portals to `<body>`** — the sidebar scrolls, so it sets `overflow-y`, and a box that is not `visible` on one axis is not visible on the other either. `QueryProvider` moved up in the collection layout to wrap the sidebar too, so the shell and the screen share one cache. A group **bigger than `COLLAPSE_THRESHOLD` collapses to its summary line** (#581) — chevron, title, count, and a *See all →* at the end of the same row: some groups are long-lived by design (an Allegro order is not recorded until the buyer pays, an ended auction waits on the same money), so their rows sat in the panel for days and crowded out everything else. Past a handful the rows have stopped being a list of things to do and become a **quantity**, which reads better as one line. A **size** rule rather than a per-group flag, and it applies to `critical` groups too: any group that big is a backlog whatever it is about, a hand-kept list of "the noisy ones" goes stale, and one line saying *six* possible double sales is not a quieter statement than six lines saying it once each. Decided **server-side** beside the severity, for the same reason — one rule stated once cannot be applied two ways — but the items still ride along, because a collapsed group opens **in place** (local state, so it resets with the panel) and a summary that can only be left to find out what is under it is not a summary. The collapsed row carries the group's link itself, so the screen is reachable without opening the group first, and the expanded body drops its own *N more →*.

- Tabs inside dialogs are visual grouping only — one logical save action. Dialog height determined by default tab; switching tabs must not change height. Body scrolls, header/footer fixed. The default panel therefore stays **in flow** and every other one overlays it (`position: absolute; inset: 0`, own `overflow-y`), hidden with `display: none` and **never unmounted** — a hidden panel's fields must still reach `FormData`, since a tab is grouping and not a second form. The contact dialog (#403) is the case that forced this: it grew a **Platform** and an **Auction defaults** tab beside **Contact** because the platform role alone now carries six groups of settings, and one column of them was a wall. Which tabs exist follows the **roles ticked**, so a plain address-book contact still sees no tab bar; unticking a role *does* unmount its panel, deliberately, because that is how the action learns to clear what the role carried. Which tab is shown is **derived** from the requested one against the roles present — never a `setState` in an effect — so unticking Platform while standing on its tab falls back on the same render. A `required` field on a hidden tab is not focusable and the browser refuses the submit without saying why, so it carries an `onInvalid` that returns to its own tab.

- The **app icon** (#65) is a perforated stamp silhouette in the accent teal with a white `S`, living in `src/app/` under Next's file conventions — `icon.svg` (the source of truth, and what modern browsers load), `favicon.ico` (16/32/48, for anything that still asks for `/favicon.ico`) and `apple-icon.png` (180px). Next emits the `<link>` tags from the filenames, so the root layout's `metadata` carries no `icons` block; adding one would mean two places to keep in step. The perforations are **cut out with a mask** rather than drawn as notches in a background colour, so the mark sits on whatever the tab strip or a bookmark bar is painted with — the one exception is `apple-icon.png`, which gets an opaque white plate underneath because iOS ignores transparency and squares the icon itself. The `S` is a **stroked path, not `<text>`**: an icon is rendered where the app's fonts are not. The `.ico` and the `.png` are generated from `icon.svg` (`rsvg-convert`), so a change to the mark means regenerating both rather than editing them.
