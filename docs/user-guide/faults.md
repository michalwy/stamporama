# Faults

A copy's **condition** — MNH, MH, Used — says nothing about what is individually wrong with the
piece: a thin, a thinned gum, a missing perforation tooth, a crease, a hinge remnant. A **fault**
records that. A copy can carry any number of them, picked from a list of faults you define once.

## Your list of faults

**Settings → Faults** (in the *Catalog* group, beside conditions and certificate statuses) holds the
list. A new collection starts with the common faults already there:

*Thin*, *Thinned gum*, *Missing tooth*, *Short perforation*, *Crease*, *Tear*, *Hinge remnant*,
*Stain*.

Rename, reorder or remove them as you like, and add your own. Each fault has:

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | what the fault is called, in your collection's language. Unique within the collection — a second fault with the same name is refused. |
| **Translations** | what each of your listing languages calls it, shown when your platforms list in another language. Leave one blank to use the name. |

**Drag a row** to change the order. It is the order a copy's faults are shown in everywhere, so
setting it to the order you check a stamp in keeps the chips reading the same way on every copy.

Each row says how many copies carry the fault. **A fault some copy carries cannot be deleted** — the
**Delete** button is greyed out and says on how many copies the fault is. Take it off those copies
first (bulk editing does that in one pass, see below). A fault nothing carries is deleted after you
confirm.

## Giving a copy its faults

Faults are chosen in the copy's **Add copy** / **Edit** dialog, in the **Faults** field right under
the condition. The copy's own page opens the same dialog from its **Edit** button.

- **Press ↓** in the empty field to see every fault you have not picked yet, or **start typing** to
  narrow the list; click a fault, or highlight it and press Enter, to add it.
  The list opens upwards when the field is near the bottom of the window, and **Escape** closes the
  list without closing the dialog.
- **A name that is not on your list** becomes a new fault: type it and press Enter, or pick
  *New fault "…"*. It is added to your list when you save the copy, at the end of the order — a
  cancelled dialog adds nothing.
- Take a fault off with the **×** on its chip, or Backspace in the empty field.

Faults can also be given while **identifying a scan tile**, and marked on the tiles before that,
with the card in hand — see [Marking the condition before identifying](purchases.md#marking-the-condition-before-identifying).

Faults belong to the **copy**, the piece in your hand. Nothing comes from the stamp the copy is
linked to, and a fault on one copy is never on another.

## Where faults are shown

- On the **Copies list**, a copy's faults are chips on the line with its condition, certificate and
  format.
- On the **copy's own page**, under **Faults**, beside the condition.

A copy with no faults shows nothing at all.

## In an offer's description

A platform's description template can name a copy's faults, so a buyer is told what is wrong with
the piece without you typing it into each listing — in the platform's language, from each fault's
**Translations**. See [Telling a buyer about faults](contacts.md#telling-a-buyer-about-faults).

## What faults take off the value

A fault itself carries no figure. How much a copy's faults take off its value is a percentage you
type on the copy, in the **Value reduction** field beside **Faults** — see
[Lowering a copy's value for its faults](inventory.md#lowering-a-copys-value-for-its-faults). The
copy's own page shows it under **Value reduction**.

## Finding copies by fault

The Copies list's **Faults** filter shows the copies carrying **any** of the faults you tick.
**No faults**, at the top of its panel, shows the copies with no fault at all; ticked beside some
faults, it shows those copies as well. Like the filters beside it, it is remembered between visits.
See [Filters and sorting](inventory.md#filters-and-sorting).

## Many copies at once

[Bulk editing](inventory.md#bulk-editing-the-selection) has a **Faults** section with two halves:
**Add** puts the faults you name on every picked copy, and **Remove** takes them off. Every fault you
do not name stays exactly as it is on each copy.

Its **Value reduction** section sets one percentage on every picked copy (**Set to…**), takes it off
them (**Clear**), or leaves each copy's own (**Leave as is**).
